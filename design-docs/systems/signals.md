# Project Signal — Signals and Information

## 1. Purpose

Information is a primary gameplay system in Project Signal.

Project Signal is not primarily about revealing hidden tiles. The player spends substantial strategic effort determining:

* what is happening
* where it is happening
* how certain that knowledge is
* how old that knowledge is
* what could explain the observed evidence
* what the opponent may be trying to conceal
* whether a signal deserves action

The player rarely possesses complete objective knowledge. The strategic problem is not merely locating the enemy. It is building a useful understanding of a living world from incomplete, uneven, aging, and sometimes deliberately misleading evidence.

**Established principle:** Information is part of the strategy, not a presentation layer placed over otherwise complete knowledge.

**Hard rule:** Neither faction automatically receives complete knowledge of the simulated world.

**Intended gameplay behavior:** Players observe evidence, form hypotheses, commit resources under uncertainty, and later discover whether their interpretation was correct.

---

## 2. Core Information Philosophy

> **The world contains objective reality, but players interact with incomplete representations of that reality.**

Information gameplay revolves around:

* observation
* interpretation
* uncertainty
* confidence
* temporal freshness
* conflicting evidence
* deception
* changing hypotheses

Project Signal rewards reasoning rather than simple map reveal.

The world does not exist merely to populate the player's interface. Things happen whether or not either faction is present to witness them. Sensors, scouts, ecological familiarity, accumulated experience, and player reasoning expose portions of that reality without collapsing the distinction between what exists and what is known.

The central doctrine is:

> **The world provides evidence. The player decides what that evidence means.**

---

## 3. Reality Layers

Project Signal maintains a conceptual separation between three realities:

### World Reality

World Reality is the objective simulated state.

It contains what actually exists, where it exists, what it is doing, and what processes are occurring regardless of whether either faction knows about them.

This corresponds to the earlier concept of `OmniscientReality`.

### Vanguard Knowledge

Vanguard Knowledge is the Vanguard faction's current representation of the world.

It contains only what Vanguard has:

* observed
* detected
* classified
* inferred
* remembered
* estimated
* annotated
* learned through prior exposure

It may be incomplete, stale, ambiguous, or incorrect.

This corresponds to the earlier concept of `HumanReality`.

### Plastai Knowledge

Plastai Knowledge is the Plastai faction's current representation of the world.

It contains only what the Plastai have perceived, inferred, remembered, or learned through their own radically different means.

It may also be incomplete, stale, ambiguous, or incorrect.

This corresponds to the earlier concept of `AlienReality`.

**Hard rule:** World Reality never automatically propagates into faction knowledge simply because the information exists in the simulation.

**Hard rule:** Neither faction receives privileged omniscience as a normal gameplay state.

---

## 4. Objective Reality

Objective world state exists independently of player knowledge.

A brood exists whether Vanguard knows about it or not.

A convoy continues traveling even if the Plastai have not observed it.

Wildlife migrates without requiring either player to witness the migration.

Weather develops beyond observation coverage.

Infrastructure construction progresses in regions the opponent has not recently reconnoitered.

Resources move through logistics networks regardless of whether the opposing faction understands those networks.

The simulation does not change reality merely because reality is unseen.

**Hard rule:** Hidden information changes player knowledge, not objective world state.

This distinction allows reconnaissance, deception, stale intelligence, and retrospective discovery to operate coherently. The player may eventually discover that an important event happened hours, days, or weeks earlier without ever having witnessed the event directly.

---

## 5. Faction Knowledge

Each faction maintains an imperfect representation of World Reality.

Faction knowledge may contain:

* confirmed observations
* uncertain contacts
* inferred activity
* historical sightings
* estimated positions
* known infrastructure
* suspected infrastructure
* annotations
* hypotheses
* outdated information
* unresolved signals
* conflicting reports

Faction knowledge is not automatically correct.

A contact may have moved.

A facility may have changed purpose.

A brood may have matured.

A convoy may have reached its destination.

An apparently empty region may contain activity that has never been detected.

A player-created annotation may be completely wrong.

**Established principle:** The map represents what the faction currently knows or believes, not an authoritative view of reality.

---

## 6. Observation Versus Interpretation

Project Signal distinguishes between:

1. what was directly observed
2. what the faction can reasonably classify
3. what the player believes the observation means

These are not equivalent.

An observation might be:

> Large biological movement detected near a wetland.

Possible interpretations include:

* ordinary migration
* biomass harvesting
* brood relocation
* preparation for attack
* response to predators
* response to weather
* movement unrelated to either faction

The observation may be accurate while every interpretation remains uncertain.

Likewise, Vanguard may correctly detect significant biological activity while misunderstanding its relationship to Plastai lifecycle, nutrient logistics, or strategic intent.

The Plastai may correctly perceive substantial Vanguard construction while misunderstanding whether it supports extraction, communications, aviation, defense, or an offensive buildup.

**Hard rule:** Detection does not automatically produce explanation.

**Hard rule:** Accurate observation does not guarantee accurate interpretation.

---

## 7. Signals

A **signal** is evidence produced by activity in World Reality that may reveal something about the underlying state of the world.

Signals may include:

* visual contact
* movement
* heat
* sound
* radio activity
* communications traffic
* industrial emissions
* atmospheric change
* ecological disturbance
* wildlife displacement
* construction
* excavation
* transport activity
* fires
* smoke
* water disturbance
* infrastructure activity
* nutrient movement clues
* unusual absence of expected activity

These categories are conceptual rather than a final rigid taxonomy.

Signals matter because world activity leaves evidence.

A signal is not necessarily an enemy contact and does not necessarily communicate intent.

---

## 8. Signal Sources

Signals originate from many systems.

Possible sources include:

* enemy activity
* friendly activity
* wildlife
* weather
* terrain
* infrastructure
* ecological processes
* logistics
* accidental events
* interaction between several of these systems

This multiplicity is essential.

If every unusual observation represents hostile activity, information gameplay collapses into enemy-icon detection.

A frightened herd might indicate Plastai activity. It might indicate Vanguard aircraft. It might indicate predators. It might indicate wildfire. It might indicate several of these simultaneously.

**Established principle:** The environment produces meaningful informational noise because it is itself an active simulation.

---

## 9. Plausible Multiple Interpretations

Important signals support multiple plausible explanations wherever practical.

Examples include:

* increased aircraft activity may indicate attack preparation, logistics, reconnaissance, defensive redeployment, or relocation
* a quiet brood site may be abandoned, dormant, depleted, concealed, or holding prepared capability
* wildlife fleeing a valley may indicate fire, predators, industrial activity, Plastai presence, or combat
* rail construction may support extraction, logistics, air operations, settlement of a new industrial area, or offensive buildup
* aquifer-related activity may support expansion, nutrient routing, brood movement, recovery, or preparation elsewhere

The same evidence may also mean different things depending on prior observations.

A single transport convoy means little.

A repeated stream of convoys toward a newly constructed airfield may mean substantially more.

**Hard rule:** Important signals do not map one-to-one onto strategic intent.

**Intended gameplay behavior:** Players combine context, history, geography, and competing evidence to determine which explanation deserves action.

---

## 10. Detection

Detection is the process of noticing that something exists or that something has changed.

Detection depends on circumstances such as:

* distance
* terrain
* sensor coverage
* weather
* line of sight
* signal strength
* movement
* concealment
* environmental noise
* faction-specific capabilities

Detection may reveal only the existence of activity.

The player may know that something happened without knowing exactly what happened.

The player may know that something is moving without knowing its identity.

The player may know that an area changed without knowing what caused the change.

**Hard rule:** Detection does not necessarily imply classification or identification.

---

## 11. Identification

Information develops through conceptually distinct levels of understanding.

A player may first detect:

> Something large is moving.

Later observations may classify it as:

> Biological activity.

Further observation may identify it as:

> A known Plastai phenotype.

Even then, the player may not understand:

> What is it doing, why is it here, and what will it do next?

The important hierarchy is therefore:

* detected
* classified
* identified
* understood

These are conceptual states, not necessarily final UI labels.

This distinction is especially important for Vanguard xeno ignorance.

A high-resolution sensor image may provide excellent physical observation while providing poor strategic understanding of unfamiliar biology.

**Hard rule:** Better sensing technology does not automatically eliminate interpretive uncertainty.

---

## 12. Confidence

Knowledge is not always binary.

Information may carry different levels of confidence based on:

* sensor quality
* number of observations
* recency
* corroborating evidence
* environmental conditions
* familiarity with the observed phenomenon
* conflicting signals

A recent direct observation under clear conditions may be highly reliable.

A weak contact behind terrain during severe weather may remain tentative.

Repeated observation of the same pattern may increase confidence without necessarily revealing intent.

Conflicting evidence may decrease confidence or split interpretation into several plausible hypotheses.

**Established principle:** Confidence matters.

**Intended gameplay behavior:** The player can distinguish strong evidence from weak evidence without requiring a literal percentage attached to every contact.

**Open design question:** The exact representation of confidence remains unresolved.

---

## 13. Information Age

Every observation conceptually belongs to a point in time.

This is critical because the world continues changing while the player is not observing it.

As information ages:

* units move
* infrastructure changes
* broods mature
* convoys arrive
* aircraft redeploy
* weather shifts
* wildlife migrates
* resources deplete
* intentions change

Information that was completely accurate when collected may later become dangerously misleading.

The player therefore needs to understand not only what was observed but **when** it was observed.

This system connects directly to `systems/time.md`.

**Hard rule:** Information does not remain permanently current merely because it was once correct.

---

## 14. Stale Intelligence

Previously accurate information may become wrong without being explicitly deleted.

Examples include:

* a force has moved
* a brood has emerged
* an airfield now hosts different aircraft
* a migration corridor is temporarily empty
* a convoy has changed route
* a resource site has been depleted
* infrastructure has expanded
* a dormant location has become active

Project Signal does not treat fog of war as a binary choice between visible truth and black unexplored terrain.

A far more important state is:

> This is what we last knew.

A previously observed brood site may therefore remain visible as historical knowledge even though the player no longer knows its current state.

**Established principle:** Fog of war includes outdated beliefs.

---

## 15. Information Persistence

Different kinds of evidence persist for different lengths of time.

Examples include:

* a radio burst may disappear almost immediately
* footprints or tracks may persist before fading
* smoke persists for some time
* damaged vegetation may remain visible
* infrastructure remains
* carcasses persist
* ecological depletion may remain visible for extended periods
* burned terrain may preserve evidence of earlier events
* construction may reveal long-term commitment even when activity stops

Persistent evidence enables retrospective reasoning.

A player may enter an area after the relevant event and reconstruct portions of what occurred from its aftermath.

**Intended gameplay behavior:** Reconnaissance can discover traces of events, not only currently active entities.

**Open design question:** Exact persistence and decay behavior remains system-specific.

---

## 16. Reconnaissance

Reconnaissance is the deliberate act of reducing uncertainty.

It may involve:

* patrols
* drones
* aircraft
* fixed sensors
* biological scouts
* observation posts
* environmental monitoring
* repeated exposure
* indirect ecological evidence

Reconnaissance does not automatically provide answers.

A scouting mission may determine that a location contains biological activity without establishing its purpose.

An aircraft may observe construction without determining the strategic plan behind it.

A Plastai scout may detect industrial disturbance without understanding the technology involved.

**Established principle:** Reconnaissance reveals evidence.

It does not invoke an omniscient information service.

---

## 17. Persistent Surveillance

Periodic reconnaissance and persistent observation are different capabilities.

Periodic reconnaissance provides snapshots.

Persistent surveillance provides better continuity and makes changes easier to detect.

Persistent coverage requires real investment through systems such as:

* sensor networks
* communications infrastructure
* repeated patrols
* biological presence
* maintained observation platforms

The player does not receive permanent perfect knowledge because an area was seen once.

Maintaining awareness consumes positioning, infrastructure, network capacity, personnel, biological presence, or other strategic resources.

**Hard rule:** Permanent knowledge requires continued justification through actual observation or persistent evidence.

---

## 18. Sensor Coverage

Sensor networks create regions of differing information quality.

Coverage may be:

* strong
* weak
* intermittent
* blocked
* degraded
* absent

Coverage interacts with:

* terrain
* weather
* infrastructure
* communications
* distance
* sensor type
* faction-specific capabilities

Project Signal avoids reducing all observation to one universal sight-radius mechanic.

Different activities expose themselves differently, and different means of observation answer different questions.

**Established principle:** Information quality is spatially uneven.

---

## 19. Vanguard Sensing

Vanguard possesses powerful technological means of observation.

Its information strengths include concepts such as:

* advanced technical sensors
* aerial reconnaissance
* remote platforms
* fixed sensor networks
* broad-area monitoring
* communications observation where appropriate
* industrial telemetry from its own systems
* networked observation infrastructure

These systems can make Vanguard extremely capable at detecting physical phenomena across large areas.

They do not solve interpretation.

**Hard rule:** Advanced sensors do not equal perfect biological understanding.

The Vanguard forces present in Project Signal are extraction and defense forces, not a complete xenobiological research expedition. They can measure unfamiliar activity with great precision while remaining uncertain about what that activity actually represents.

---

## 20. Vanguard Xeno Interpretation

Vanguard may observe something accurately and interpret it incorrectly.

Its uncertainty includes:

* lifecycle stages
* phenotype roles
* brood development
* ecological coordination
* aquifer behavior
* native species interactions
* genetic adaptation
* whether activity is deliberate or natural
* whether apparent inactivity represents absence, dormancy, or preparation

A sensor can accurately report massive biological movement without knowing whether the movement is migration, feeding, relocation, maturation, or military preparation.

Repeated exposure improves recognition.

Known patterns become easier to classify.

Previously encountered phenotypes become less alien.

Recurring behaviors acquire context.

This learning does not eliminate uncertainty.

**Hard rule:** Vanguard never receives effortless biological omniscience merely because its hardware is technologically advanced.

---

## 21. Plastai Sensing

Plastai information capabilities derive from their existence as native biological actors embedded in the world rather than from human-style technological sensor networks.

Established strengths include:

* native ecological familiarity
* biological observation
* familiarity with local wildlife behavior
* distributed physical presence
* interaction with water and aquifer systems
* sensitivity to ecological disturbance through direct experience of the environment

Their knowledge may be exceptionally strong in categories that humans find difficult to interpret.

This does not make the Plastai omniscient.

They still require observation, presence, repeated exposure, and context.

**Hard rule:** Native familiarity is not magical global awareness.

**Open design question:** Exact biological sensing mechanisms remain intentionally unresolved.

---

## 22. Plastai Knowledge Limitations

The Plastai possess epistemic blind spots of their own.

They may struggle with:

* unfamiliar Vanguard technology
* industrial intent
* remote technological activity
* activity occurring beyond their observation network
* orbital capability
* long-distance technological coordination
* distinguishing the purpose of unfamiliar infrastructure
* understanding why several physically separate Vanguard systems belong to one operational plan

A rail line is physically obvious.

Its strategic purpose may not be.

An airfield is visible.

Its relationship to orbital deliveries, distant fuel production, aircraft basing, or a future offensive may require observation over time.

**Established principle:** Both factions possess meaningful uncertainty, but they are uncertain about different things.

---

## 23. Information Asymmetry

Faction asymmetry extends beyond sensor range.

Vanguard and Plastai understand different categories of evidence differently.

The same event may be:

* obvious to one faction
* ambiguous to the other
* detected through completely different mechanisms
* interpreted through different prior knowledge

Vanguard may precisely measure an ecological anomaly while failing to understand its biological significance.

Plastai may immediately recognize that local wildlife behavior is abnormal while failing to understand the distant technological process causing it.

This asymmetry reinforces faction identity.

**Hard rule:** The factions are not symmetric sensor packages with different numerical ranges.

---

## 24. Learning Through Exposure

Repeated exposure improves recognition.

Examples include:

* repeated encounters with a phenotype improve classification
* repeated observation of an industrial pattern helps Plastai infer purpose
* recurring signal patterns become easier to identify
* prior encounters provide context for future observations
* repeated correlation between two events makes their relationship easier to recognize

Learning represents accumulated knowledge rather than arbitrary omniscience.

The player gradually develops a vocabulary for the opponent.

**Established principle:** Recognition improves through experience.

**Guardrail:** This does not require a generic experience bar that eventually converts uncertainty into perfect information.

---

## 25. Player Annotation

Players can preserve their own interpretations of the world through map annotation.

Useful annotations include:

* suspected brood sites
* possible migration corridors
* likely enemy routes
* suspected infrastructure purpose
* uncertainty
* planned reconnaissance areas
* observed signal patterns
* regions requiring later verification

Annotations belong to the player's reasoning layer.

They do not become objective truth simply because the player marked them.

A label such as:

> Possible brood relocation corridor

remains a hypothesis until evidence supports or disproves it.

**Established principle:** Player-authored interpretation remains distinct from system-confirmed observation.

**Open design question:** The exact annotation tools and interface remain unresolved.

---

## 26. Hypotheses

Project Signal allows the player to hold interpretations without immediately confirming them.

Conceptually:

> I think this is a brood site.

> I think this rail line supports an airfield.

> I think the herd movement is natural.

> I think this region is being prepared for emergence.

> I think the apparent retreat is deliberate.

These beliefs may remain unresolved for significant periods.

The player can act on them before certainty exists.

Later evidence may:

* support the hypothesis
* weaken it
* contradict it
* reveal that several explanations were partially correct

**Established principle:** Hypothesis formation is gameplay.

---

## 27. Confirmation

Confirmation generally requires stronger evidence than initial detection.

Possible confirmation sources include:

* direct visual observation
* repeated sensor contact
* close reconnaissance
* destruction or capture
* unmistakable behavior
* corroboration from several signal types
* persistent observation over time

Even confirmed identity does not necessarily confirm intent.

The player may conclusively know that a brood exists while still being uncertain whether it is defensive, dormant, expanding, preparing emergence, or supporting activity elsewhere.

**Guardrail:** Avoid excessive automatic UI declarations such as `CONFIRMED ENEMY BASE` when the actual evidence supports a narrower conclusion.

---

## 28. Negative Evidence

Absence can itself be information.

Examples include:

* wildlife disappearing from a region
* expected convoy traffic stopping
* an airfield suddenly becoming quiet
* a brood site showing no surface activity
* communications going silent
* a migration failing to occur
* expected industrial output disappearing

Negative evidence remains ambiguous.

Silence may indicate:

* abandonment
* concealment
* damage
* deception
* relocation
* dormancy
* temporary interruption

An absence becomes most meaningful when the player knows what normally occurs.

This makes history and pattern recognition important.

**Hard rule:** Silence is evidence, not automatic proof of absence.

---

## 29. Pattern Recognition

Some information becomes meaningful only across time.

Examples include:

* recurring aircraft traffic
* repeated nutrient movement
* migration displacement
* progressive construction
* regular communications bursts
* repeated convoy routes
* several quiet brood sites activating sequentially

A single event may be mundane.

A pattern may expose a strategic process.

Project Signal rewards players who notice relationships across observations rather than reacting only to isolated icons.

**Established principle:** Temporal patterns can matter more than individual contacts.

---

## 30. Signal History

Historical observations remain strategically useful.

The player should be able to reconstruct:

* what happened
* when it happened
* what changed
* where activity moved
* what was believed at the time

This allows the player to reason about plans unfolding across accelerated game time.

A current empty valley may become meaningful when historical observations show:

1. wildlife gradually disappearing
2. nutrient-related activity increasing
3. surface activity briefly spiking
4. the region becoming quiet

Historical information therefore supports both intelligence analysis and replay.

This system connects directly to `systems/time.md`.

---

## 31. Conflicting Evidence

Different observations may disagree.

Examples include:

* one sensor indicates movement while another sees nothing
* a suspected brood site appears inactive despite nutrient activity
* aircraft traffic suggests one purpose while construction suggests another
* ecological evidence indicates disturbance while direct contact remains absent

Conflicting evidence does not represent arbitrary system failure.

It creates uncertainty that the player can investigate.

Possible causes include:

* different sensor capabilities
* different observation times
* terrain
* weather
* concealment
* movement between observations
* incorrect interpretation
* deliberate deception

**Established principle:** Contradiction produces questions rather than automatically resolving itself.

---

## 32. False Positives Without Dice-Roll Nonsense

Project Signal prefers understandable ambiguity over arbitrary false information.

False conclusions should primarily arise from:

* incomplete evidence
* natural phenomena
* sensor limitations
* stale information
* environmental interference
* incorrect player interpretation
* deliberate opponent behavior

A sensor may have imperfections.

However, the information game does not rely primarily on hidden rolls that randomly fabricate nonexistent enemies.

The player should usually be able to understand why an incorrect conclusion was reasonable in hindsight.

**Hard rule:** Uncertainty should have systemic causes wherever practical.

---

## 33. Deception

Deception is a major strategic system.

It works by manipulating the evidence available to the opponent.

Examples include:

* visible buildup in one region
* hidden preparation elsewhere
* unusual logistics
* dormant broods
* decoy infrastructure
* changed communications patterns
* deliberate route choices
* apparent abandonment
* nutrient rerouting
* aircraft relocation

Good deception causes the opponent to reach a reasonable but incorrect conclusion from genuine observations.

The deceiver does not merely hide information.

The deceiver shapes interpretation.

**Established principle:** Deception targets the opponent's model of reality.

---

## 34. Deception Without Fake-Signal Buttons

Project Signal does not primarily implement deception as:

> Press button to create fake enemy signal.

Instead, deception emerges from legitimate systems:

* actual movements
* actual construction
* actual resource allocation
* actual communication behavior
* actual ecological manipulation
* actual dormant capability
* actual positioning
* actual logistics

A decoy may exist, but creating and supporting it constitutes real strategic behavior.

A visible buildup used as misdirection requires actual assets or observable activity.

A quiet region may be made suspicious through deliberate operational choices rather than arbitrary UI noise.

**Hard rule:** Deception is strongest when it manipulates real observable systems.

---

## 35. Vanguard Deception

Vanguard deception can exploit the visibility and ambiguity of technological operations.

Established or directly supported methods include:

* ambiguous construction
* aircraft relocation
* decoy infrastructure
* changed communications patterns
* visible logistics supporting a false interpretation
* movement that suggests one operational priority while supporting another
* concentrating visible defensive or sensor activity in misleading ways

Large Vanguard systems naturally produce signals.

The faction's deception problem is often not becoming invisible but ensuring those signals tell the wrong story.

---

## 36. Plastai Deception

Plastai deception exploits biology, ecology, distributed preparation, aquifer logistics, and delayed capability.

Established concepts include:

* maintaining visible activity at site A while secretly preparing site B
* dormant brood sites
* nutrient rerouting
* reabsorption and later relocation where supported by brood logistics
* activity that resembles ordinary biological migration
* delayed or triggered emergence
* apparent abandonment
* holding matured organisms in stasis before sudden deployment

These systems allow Plastai to separate visible surface activity from actual strategic preparation.

A site that appears to be the center of activity may be expendable.

A quiet site may contain stored capability.

A surface loss may conceal preparation elsewhere in the aquifer network.

**Established principle:** Plastai deception exploits the opponent's difficulty interpreting biological activity.

---

## 37. Ecological Noise

Ecology is a major source of informational ambiguity.

Wildlife creates real signals unrelated to faction intent.

Examples include:

* migration
* fleeing herds
* disturbed vegetation
* predator movement
* aquatic disturbance
* carcass concentrations
* changes in grazing or feeding patterns

Faction activity can also alter ecology indirectly.

A herd fleeing Vanguard aircraft may later create a signal interpreted as Plastai movement.

Plastai harvesting may change predator behavior far beyond the actual harvesting site.

A wildfire may alter migration and obscure unrelated activity.

The ecology system therefore participates directly in the information game.

See `world/ecology.md`.

---

## 38. Weather Noise

Weather modifies both observation and interpretation.

Weather may:

* obscure visual observation
* reduce some sensor quality
* alter animal behavior
* produce smoke, fog, lightning, precipitation, or atmospheric effects
* interrupt reconnaissance
* alter movement
* create environmental disturbances
* change the meaning of other signals

Weather does not simply toggle sensors off.

Its effects depend on the event, the world state, terrain, faction capabilities, and the signal being observed.

A thunderstorm may hide one type of activity while making another more obvious.

Its ecological consequences may also continue producing signals after the storm itself has passed.

See `world/weather.md`.

**Established principle:** Weather complicates information rather than functioning only as a visibility penalty.

---

## 39. Terrain and Information

Terrain structures observation.

Examples include:

* mountains blocking lines of sight
* forests concealing movement
* valleys channeling activity
* open plains increasing visibility
* wetlands creating dense ecological signatures
* water systems exposing or concealing different types of movement
* terrain forcing predictable logistics corridors

Terrain therefore affects both what can be observed and what conclusions the player can reasonably draw.

A narrow valley containing repeated movement is more strategically meaningful if terrain severely limits alternate routes.

A forest may conceal units while their effects on wildlife remain detectable.

See `world/map.md`.

---

## 40. Logistics as Intelligence

Logistics reveal strategy.

Observable logistical activity includes:

* rail traffic
* convoy flow
* airlift
* orbital deliveries
* storage buildup
* nutrient redistribution
* biomass depletion
* new transport infrastructure
* changes in supply intensity

A faction's logistics can reveal what it may be preparing to do.

This does not make interpretation automatic.

Heavy traffic may support:

* expansion
* recovery
* defense
* stockpiling
* construction
* offensive action

For Plastai, nutrient movement through the aquifer network serves a similar informational role to technological logistics without becoming a biological copy of trucks and trains.

See `systems/resources-logistics.md`.

**Established principle:** Strategic commitment leaves logistical evidence.

---

## 41. Communications as Signal

Communications can reveal activity without revealing exact content or intent.

Communications patterns may expose:

* activity level
* command presence
* network expansion
* operational tempo
* changes in behavior
* concentration or dispersal of technological systems

Loss of communications may also be meaningful.

However, silence remains ambiguous.

It may represent:

* destruction
* deliberate emissions discipline
* abandonment
* network failure
* relocation
* changed operational behavior

**Guardrail:** Communications interception does not automatically reveal exact enemy orders.

---

## 42. Infrastructure as Signal

Construction exposes long-term commitment.

Important infrastructure includes:

* rail
* roads
* bridges
* airfields
* depots
* refineries
* relays
* brood sites
* aquifer entrances
* other major logistics nodes

Infrastructure often remains easier to detect than the intent behind it.

A new rail line provides strong evidence that Vanguard considers a corridor important.

It does not necessarily reveal why.

A new aquifer entrance demonstrates major Plastai investment.

It does not necessarily reveal which future operation it supports.

**Hard rule:** Large strategic commitments usually leave evidence.

---

## 43. Contact Classification

A useful conceptual progression is:

* unknown signal
* possible contact
* classified contact
* identified entity
* understood activity

This taxonomy is not yet a mandatory UI specification.

Its purpose is to preserve the distinction between detection and understanding.

The game should be able to represent situations such as:

> Strong unknown movement signal.

> Probable biological contact.

> Known Plastai phenotype.

> Known phenotype moving northwest.

without automatically adding:

> Preparing to attack Refinery 3.

**Hard rule:** Identification of the entity and interpretation of its purpose remain separate.

---

## 44. Threat Assessment

Threat is partly an interpretation layer.

A detected entity may be:

* harmless
* potentially dangerous
* strategically important
* an immediate threat

These categories depend on context.

A large Plastai organism moving through distant wilderness may pose little immediate danger.

The same organism approaching a lightly defended refinery may be critical.

An aircraft sitting at an airfield is not necessarily a threat.

A sudden concentration of aircraft, fuel movement, and increased sortie activity may warrant immediate attention.

The game may organize evidence.

It should not routinely decide strategic meaning for the player.

At higher assistance levels, more interpretation may be offered.

---

## 45. Assisted Interpretation

Difficulty affects how much interpretive support the game provides.

At more assisted settings, the game may:

* highlight unusual patterns
* suggest possible interpretations
* recommend attention
* prioritize significant changes
* automatically pause or slow time for configured suspicious developments

At less assisted settings, the player identifies more patterns independently.

The underlying world and fundamental evidence remain consistent.

This connects directly to `systems/time.md`.

**Established principle:** Assistance changes how much analytical work the interface performs, not whether the simulation secretly gives the opponent better information.

---

## 46. Information and Difficulty

Harder difficulty does not primarily make sensors arbitrarily worse.

Harder settings reduce assistance such as:

* automatic interpretation
* warnings
* prioritization
* suggested meaning
* convenient summaries
* automatic attention management

The player performs more analysis personally.

This aligns with Project Signal's broader difficulty doctrine: difficulty comes from reduced assistance rather than magical AI bonuses or arbitrary penalties.

**Hard rule:** Difficulty should not undermine the internal logic of the information model merely to make the game harder.

---

## 47. Signals and Time Compression

Signals are one of the primary mechanisms through which the world requests player attention during accelerated time.

The system should:

* accumulate observations
* preserve important historical changes
* interrupt compression when configured conditions occur
* avoid pausing for trivial noise
* support historical review
* surface strategically meaningful developments

A player may fast-forward through routine activity until a configured trigger occurs, such as a relevant contact entering a protected area.

The player can define some of these attention thresholds independently of difficulty.

Assistance level may determine how much the system interprets events before deciding they deserve attention.

See `systems/time.md`.

**Established principle:** Time compression and information management are inseparable systems.

---

## 48. Player Attention

The map operates at a scale where the player cannot observe every region continuously.

The interface must help organize information such as:

* new observations
* changed observations
* unresolved signals
* high-priority alerts
* stale intelligence
* player-marked areas
* recently changed patterns

This support is organizational rather than omniscient.

The system helps the player find information that may deserve thought.

It does not automatically solve the meaning of that information.

---

## 49. Information Overload

A simulated continent-scale world can generate far more signals than a human player can process.

The design therefore prioritizes meaningful information.

Avoid:

* notification spam
* hundreds of identical contacts
* forcing the player to inspect every individual animal
* presenting raw simulation telemetry as gameplay
* turning every routine event into an alert

Aggregate where appropriate.

A migrating herd should normally appear as a meaningful ecological event rather than several thousand individual notifications.

A large movement front may matter more than every creature composing it.

A communications pattern may matter more than every individual transmission.

**Hard rule:** Simulation detail does not justify information overload.

---

## 50. Uncertainty Must Be Legible

The player should understand why something is uncertain.

Possible explanations include:

* no current sensor coverage
* observation is old
* weather degraded visibility
* classification confidence is low
* terrain blocks confirmation
* conflicting observations exist
* unfamiliar biology prevents interpretation
* the signal was brief
* only indirect evidence exists

Uncertainty should not feel like invisible designer dice.

The player does not need every internal calculation.

They do need enough context to understand why the game cannot provide a stronger answer.

**Hard rule:** Uncertainty must have a legible cause.

---

## 51. Information Never Guarantees Correct Decisions

Even excellent intelligence can leave strategic ambiguity.

The player may know exactly what exists while still not knowing:

* why it exists
* what the opponent intends
* where it will move
* what other systems support it
* what will happen next

A fully identified Vanguard airfield remains strategically ambiguous.

A directly observed Plastai brood remains strategically ambiguous.

Perfect physical observation does not automatically create perfect strategic understanding.

**Established principle:** Strategy begins where observation ends.

---

## 52. Signals and Counterplay

Major strategic capabilities usually produce evidence before their full consequences occur.

Examples include:

* Vanguard strategic buildup creates logistics, construction, traffic, communications, and infrastructure signals
* Plastai major brood development creates biological, nutrient, ecological, and surface signals
* exceptionally large biological projects require enormous resource commitment and produce correspondingly significant evidence

Counterplay begins with noticing preparation.

The warning may be incomplete.

The warning may be misinterpreted.

The opponent may actively disguise its meaning.

But powerful capabilities should rarely appear from literal informational nothingness.

**Hard rule:** Major preparation produces clues.

---

## 53. Hidden Does Not Mean Undetectable

A hidden system usually means that it is:

* difficult to observe
* visible primarily through indirect effects
* ambiguous
* concealed by geography
* concealed by behavior
* dependent on specialized reconnaissance to understand

It does **not** usually mean:

* completely invisible until activated

A concealed brood may reveal ecological effects.

An underground process may alter nutrient movement.

A hidden industrial plan may still require roads, power, shipments, aircraft, or construction.

**Hard rule:** Powerful surprise is earned through concealment, timing, misdirection, and opponent misinterpretation rather than absolute invisibility.

---

## 54. Information and Strategic Commitment

Acting before certainty is available is a central source of tension.

Examples include:

* move aircraft north or keep them covering the refinery
* strike a suspected brood site or continue observing
* build through a valley believed to be safe
* divert reconnaissance toward unusual migration
* abandon an area based on incomplete evidence
* commit a large Plastai force against infrastructure whose actual purpose remains uncertain

Waiting for perfect information has a cost.

Reconnaissance consumes time and capability.

The situation continues changing.

The opponent continues acting.

The player therefore routinely chooses between:

* acting early on incomplete evidence
* gathering more information
* accepting the risk of delay

**Established principle:** Uncertainty creates commitment decisions rather than simply inconvenience.

---

## 55. Emergent Narrative

The information system should generate stories that arise naturally from imperfect knowledge.

Examples include:

* a player follows migration evidence and discovers a hidden brood
* Vanguard destroys an empty decoy site while the real threat emerges elsewhere
* Plastai interpret rail construction as extraction infrastructure and discover too late that it supports an air campaign
* stale intelligence causes a strike against a location abandoned days earlier
* both players misread the same ecological event
* a harmless signal becomes meaningful only after weeks of repeated observation
* a quiet region seems safe until historical evidence reveals that several unrelated clues were part of the same preparation
* a player correctly identifies an enemy buildup but misjudges its target

These stories should not require scripted plot twists.

They emerge from:

* incomplete observation
* real world systems
* player reasoning
* deception
* time
* strategic commitment

---

## 56. What the Signals System Must Never Become

The signals system is:

* **not** simple black-tile fog of war
* **not** permanent knowledge after one sighting
* **not** automatic perfect identification
* **not** magical omniscience for either faction
* **not** a one-to-one mapping between signal and intent
* **not** random false contacts with no systemic explanation
* **not** endless notification spam
* **not** a UI that automatically solves interpretation
* **not** a deception system based mainly on fake-signal buttons
* **not** a system where hidden means literally undetectable
* **not** a system where sensors reveal exact enemy plans
* **not** a system where technology eliminates uncertainty
* **not** a system where every unusual event is enemy action
* **not** a system where the player cannot understand why information is uncertain
* **not** a universal sight-radius system with different numbers for each faction
* **not** a simulation telemetry dashboard masquerading as gameplay
* **not** a system where more expensive sensors simply convert uncertainty into omniscience
* **not** a requirement that every signal have an explicitly authored mystery attached to it
* **not** a replacement for player judgment

The player should be capable of being wrong for understandable reasons.

That possibility is a feature.

---

## 57. Open Design Questions

The following details remain unresolved and should be settled through prototyping rather than prematurely hardened into doctrine:

* What exact contact classification states appear in the interface?
* How is confidence represented without creating false numerical precision?
* How does information age visually and mechanically?
* What information decay rules apply to different evidence types?
* What exact player annotation tools are available?
* How much historical information can the player inspect directly?
* What form does the signal-history or timeline interface take?
* How aggressively are large numbers of related signals aggregated?
* What exact biological sensing mechanisms support Plastai observation?
* What exact sensor families define Vanguard observation?
* How much automated pattern recognition exists at each assistance level?
* How are conflicting reports represented?
* Can players explicitly attach hypotheses to signals, or do annotations provide enough support?
* What exact interpretation assistance changes between difficulty levels?
* Which signal types support persistent physical traces?
* How much information can reconnaissance recover retrospectively after an event has ended?
* What threshold determines when a pattern becomes important enough for assisted systems to surface?

These questions concern representation and implementation detail.

They do not change the established information doctrine.

---

## 58. Signals Design Test

Any future signals, reconnaissance, intelligence, sensor, deception, or information mechanic should be tested against the following questions:

* Does objective reality remain separate from faction knowledge?
* Does this produce evidence rather than automatic truth?
* Can the same signal support multiple plausible explanations?
* Does information become stale?
* Is uncertainty understandable?
* Does geography affect observation?
* Do weather and ecology affect interpretation?
* Does this reinforce faction asymmetry?
* Can the opponent manipulate the evidence through real actions?
* Does major preparation create detectable clues?
* Does this reward repeated observation?
* Can historical evidence change the meaning of a current signal?
* Can silence or absence carry information without becoming definitive proof?
* Can the player form a reasonable hypothesis without receiving automatic confirmation?
* Can an observation be physically accurate while strategically misunderstood?
* Does the mechanic create meaningful decisions about whether to reconnoiter, wait, or commit?
* Does deception arise from legitimate world systems?
* Does the system avoid arbitrary fake information where systemic ambiguity would work better?
* Does the interface organize uncertainty without solving it?
* Does the mechanic help manage a continent-scale world without creating notification spam?
* Does it create opportunities for emergent stories?
* Does it reinforce the **Observe → Interpret → Prepare → Commit → Reveal → Adapt** loop?

Most importantly:

> **The world provides evidence. The player decides what that evidence means.**
