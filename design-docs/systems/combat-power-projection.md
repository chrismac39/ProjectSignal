# Project Signal — Combat and Power Projection

## 1. Purpose

**Design status: Established principle**

Combat exists to resolve strategic decisions made before weapons are fired.

Combat rewards reconnaissance, positioning, logistics, geographic understanding, preparation, and commitment. It exposes the asymmetry between Vanguard and Plastai and creates consequences that persist after an engagement ends.

Combat is important, but it is not the only meaningful activity in Project Signal. Reconnaissance, concealment, logistics, infrastructure, ecology, movement, deception, and preparation remain meaningful even when no battle occurs.

A player should often be able to look backward from a battle and understand that its outcome was substantially shaped before contact began.

---

## 2. Core Combat Philosophy

**Design status: Hard rule**

> **Combat is the visible consequence of earlier decisions about information, logistics, positioning, preparation, and commitment.**

The central combat question is often:

> **Why were these forces here, at this time, with this level of support?**

rather than:

> **Who clicked faster?**

Project Signal is not built around high-APM tactical control.

Execution during combat matters, but tactical execution does not erase strategic mistakes made beforehand.

A force that has superior equipment but poor information, inadequate support, bad positioning, or delayed reinforcement can lose to a weaker force that prepared correctly.

---

## 3. Power Projection

**Design status: Established principle**

Power projection is the ability of a faction to bring meaningful force to a specific location within a useful amount of time.

Power projection depends on:

* distance
* time
* weapon and sensor range
* logistics
* infrastructure
* intelligence
* readiness
* weather
* terrain
* resource support

Total faction strength is therefore different from local combat power.

A faction may possess enormous military capability while remaining weak in a particular valley, watershed, transport corridor, or remote region.

The important measurement is not merely:

**What does the faction possess?**

It is:

**What can the faction bring to this location before the situation is decided?**

---

## 4. Concentration Versus Coverage

**Design status: Hard rule**

Both factions can create terrifying local superiority.

Neither faction can dominate the entire map simultaneously.

Concentration creates power by sacrificing coverage elsewhere.

For Vanguard, meaningful force concentration may require:

* aircraft
* artillery
* missiles
* drones
* mechanized forces
* sensor coverage
* communications
* fuel
* ammunition
* transport
* maintenance infrastructure

All of these depend on location, readiness, and supporting networks.

For Plastai, meaningful force concentration may require:

* brood development
* biomass accumulation
* nutrient routing
* dormant organisms
* aquifer connectivity
* suitable emergence points
* ecological preparation
* concealed positioning

These also require prior investment.

A major commitment in one theater therefore creates opportunities elsewhere.

The player should routinely face situations in which enough power exists to solve several problems individually but not all of them simultaneously.

This tension is one of the primary balancing mechanisms of Project Signal.

---

## 5. Geography as a Combat System

**Design status: Hard rule**

Combat does not occur independently of the map.

Geography determines:

* access
* visibility
* movement
* firing positions
* concealment
* route availability
* retreat paths
* reinforcement routes
* supply
* sensor effectiveness
* infrastructure placement

Relevant terrain includes:

* mountains
* valleys
* forests
* wetlands
* rivers
* open plains
* mountain passes
* bridges
* lakes
* shorelines
* aquifer access points

A valley may create a natural movement corridor.

A mountain range may separate forces that are geographically close in straight-line distance.

A bridge may matter more than the combat units defending it.

A forest may provide concealment while reducing observation.

An open plain may favor long-range observation and firepower.

An aquifer outlet may become strategically important because it connects underground Plastai movement to a particular surface region.

Terrain is therefore part of combat capability rather than decorative background.

---

## 6. Distance as a Combat Constraint

**Design status: Hard rule**

The massive scale of the Project Signal map is a core combat-balancing mechanism.

Distance constrains:

* response time
* reinforcement
* aircraft basing
* artillery reach
* logistics
* evacuation
* counterattack
* intelligence freshness
* maintenance support
* redeployment

A powerful force hundreds of kilometers away may be strategically irrelevant to an engagement that will be decided before it arrives.

Likewise, intelligence concerning a distant region may become obsolete before a response can reach it.

The map scale prevents raw faction strength from becoming universal presence.

Distance creates theaters.

---

## 7. Information Before Combat

**Design status: Hard rule**

Successful combat usually depends on what the player knows before committing.

Before acting, the player should care about questions such as:

* What is actually there?
* How confident am I?
* How old is this information?
* Is this the real concentration or a decoy?
* What support exists nearby?
* What can reinforce?
* What routes connect this location?
* What signals am I missing?
* What capability might already be prepared but concealed?

Poor interpretation can produce disastrous combat outcomes even when the player possesses superior weapons.

The combat system therefore begins with the information system.

A player who correctly understands the operational picture earns the opportunity to apply force effectively.

A player who misunderstands that picture may waste force against the wrong target, arrive too late, or expose another region while reacting to a deception.

---

## 8. Detection and Targeting

**Design status: Established principle**

Detection and effective targeting are separate problems.

A player may:

1. detect activity,
2. classify a contact,
3. identify a likely target,
4. and still lack enough certainty to conduct an effective precision attack.

Targeting can depend on:

* sensor quality
* observation duration
* line of sight
* weather
* communications
* target movement
* terrain
* confidence
* information age

Long-range weapons require credible targeting.

The existence of a powerful weapon does not automatically grant knowledge of where it should fire.

This distinction is particularly important for Vanguard. Its ability to destroy a correctly identified target can be extreme without making every detected anomaly immediately vulnerable to destruction.

---

## 9. Preparation Windows

**Design status: Established principle**

Powerful actions generally require preparation.

Examples include:

* moving artillery
* positioning aircraft
* establishing fuel support
* establishing ammunition support
* developing a brood
* routing nutrients
* preparing dormant organisms
* constructing airfields
* expanding sensor coverage
* staging forces
* preparing transport routes

Preparation creates power, but it also creates:

* signals
* vulnerability
* delay
* opportunity for counterplay
* opportunity for deception

A player should therefore rarely create major power from nothing at the instant it is needed.

Strong capabilities become strategically interesting because they must first be made ready.

---

## 10. Vanguard Combat Identity

**Design status: Established principle**

Vanguard combat is defined by extreme technological lethality constrained by geography, logistics, infrastructure, information, and limited personnel.

Its major characteristics include:

* extreme ranged lethality
* strong precision capability
* aviation
* mechanized mobility
* remote systems
* sensors
* sparse human exposure
* infrastructure dependence
* logistical dependence

The Vanguard is terrifying when it correctly identifies a target and concentrates sufficient capability against it.

Its weakness is not that its weapons are secretly ineffective.

Its weakness is that maintaining correct information, positioning, support, and coverage across an enormous theater is difficult.

---

## 11. Vanguard Firepower

**Design status: Hard rule**

Vanguard weapons are not made artificially weak merely because they are powerful.

If Vanguard successfully:

* detects
* identifies
* targets
* positions
* supplies

a capable strike system, devastating damage is a valid and desirable outcome.

Balance comes primarily from:

* limited coverage
* readiness
* response time
* logistics
* basing
* weather
* uncertainty
* vulnerable infrastructure
* competing priorities
* opportunity cost

It does not primarily come from reducing advanced weapons to arbitrary damage values that allow every target to absorb unrealistic punishment.

Powerful weapons remain powerful.

The challenge is putting that power in the right place with sufficient information at the right time.

---

## 12. Vanguard Aviation

**Design status: Established principle**

Airpower is one of Vanguard's strongest forms of power projection.

Aircraft can exert major influence over ground combat through reconnaissance, rapid response, precision attack, transport, and concentrated firepower.

Their effectiveness depends on:

* airfields
* operating range
* fuel
* maintenance
* sortie generation
* weather
* travel time
* communications
* targeting information
* relocation
* theater coverage

Aircraft dramatically compress distance compared with ground forces, but they do not eliminate distance.

An aircraft based hundreds of kilometers away still requires travel time.

An aircraft undergoing maintenance is not available.

An airfield without fuel cannot produce useful sorties.

Aircraft committed to one region are not simultaneously supporting another.

Relocating aviation capacity changes where Vanguard can rapidly project force, but relocation itself requires infrastructure and preparation.

Airpower can strongly influence ground combat.

It cannot be everywhere at once.

The primary balancing mechanism for Vanguard aviation is strategic geography and commitment rather than universal enemy anti-air coverage.

---

## 13. Airpower and Counterplay

**Design status: Hard rule**

Plastai does not require ubiquitous anti-air weapons simply because Vanguard possesses aircraft.

Counterplay to airpower can include:

* dispersion
* concealment
* operating outside effective coverage
* attacking airfields
* attacking fuel infrastructure
* disrupting logistics
* exploiting severe weather
* creating simultaneous threats
* subterranean repositioning
* dormant forces
* misleading targeting
* forcing Vanguard to choose which theater receives air support

Direct biological anti-air capability may exist where appropriate.

It is not the universal solution.

The deeper counter to aircraft is to attack the conditions that make aircraft decisive:

* detection
* targeting
* basing
* availability
* concentration
* timing

A Plastai player who creates crises in several distant regions can impose meaningful choices even without destroying a single aircraft.

---

## 14. Vanguard Artillery and Long-Range Strike

**Design status: Established principle**

Artillery, missiles, and related long-range strike systems are strategic tools.

Their effectiveness depends on:

* range
* ammunition
* location
* targeting
* communications
* transport
* supporting infrastructure
* readiness

Long-range fire creates powerful local zones of influence.

It does not create unrestricted global reach.

Moving an artillery system changes the area it can affect.

Moving ammunition changes its endurance.

Destroying communications can reduce the usefulness of otherwise intact weapons.

Removing targeting confidence can force Vanguard to delay or accept uncertainty.

A long-range weapon system therefore occupies strategic space even when it is not firing.

---

## 15. Vanguard Ground Forces

**Design status: Intended gameplay behavior**

Vanguard ground forces connect technological firepower to physical territory.

Their strategic roles include:

* security
* reconnaissance
* escort
* screening
* infrastructure defense
* exploitation after ranged strikes
* mobile defense
* protecting logistics
* controlling critical routes

Mechanized and unmanned systems allow Vanguard to operate across large distances while limiting direct human exposure.

Ground forces remain necessary because aircraft and long-range fires cannot physically secure every facility, road, rail line, bridge, sensor installation, or extraction site.

The purpose of Vanguard ground combat is not to recreate a conventional tank-and-infantry RTS roster.

Ground forces exist within the larger system of infrastructure, information, mobility, and power projection.

---

## 16. Scarce Human Exposure

**Design status: Hard rule**

Vanguard personnel are strategically scarce.

Combat doctrine therefore minimizes unnecessary human exposure.

Where practical, Vanguard prefers:

* remote systems
* autonomous systems
* unmanned vehicles
* stand-off weapons
* sensors
* machines capable of operating in dangerous environments

Human personnel still exist where human presence is operationally necessary.

When humans occupy exposed facilities or dangerous positions, their presence matters strategically.

Losing a vehicle and losing the people required to operate a rare facility are not equivalent events.

This scarcity is part of Vanguard's combat identity.

---

## 17. Plastai Combat Identity

**Design status: Established principle**

Plastai combat is defined by biological specialization, preparation, concealment, distributed development, and the ability to convert prior ecological investment into sudden local force.

Its major characteristics include:

* biological specialization
* preparation
* distributed presence
* concealment
* ecological mobility
* dormant capability
* sudden emergence
* force concentration after prior investment

Plastai power does not come from armies appearing magically wherever needed.

Its power comes from preparing biological capability in advance and arranging the conditions under which that capability can later emerge.

To the opponent, the appearance of force may be sudden.

To the Plastai player, it represents a long chain of previous decisions.

---

## 18. Plastai Surface Combat

**Design status: Intended gameplay behavior**

Plastai surface combat does not need to mirror conventional human military categories.

Its organisms can instead derive combat roles from biological specialization and local requirements.

Relevant characteristics can include:

* varied phenotype roles
* terrain adaptation
* local numerical concentration
* specialized organisms
* close engagement
* ambush
* rapid exploitation of prepared terrain
* biological forms adapted to specific operational problems

Plastai forces should not simply become alien equivalents of infantry, tanks, artillery, and aircraft with renamed statistics.

Their battlefield behavior should emerge from the faction's biological systems.

---

## 19. Plastai Underground Strategic Movement

**Design status: Hard rule**

The aquifer network provides strategic underground movement.

It can:

* enable repositioning
* create uncertain attack directions
* allow development away from the eventual point of use
* support deception
* connect distant prepared regions

Aquifer movement remains constrained by:

* network connectivity
* access points
* capacity
* development
* travel time
* geography
* nutrient infrastructure

The aquifer network is not instant teleportation.

A force cannot simply disappear from one side of the world and immediately emerge wherever the player clicks.

Its strategic value comes from changing the geometry of movement while retaining constraints that can be understood, anticipated, exploited, and disrupted.

---

## 20. Dormant Forces

**Design status: Established principle**

Plastai can hold previously developed organisms in dormant or stasis-like states.

Dormancy allows:

* prepositioned force
* delayed emergence
* strategic surprise
* reserve capacity
* separation between development time and combat timing

Dormant capability must already have been:

* developed
* supplied
* positioned
* prepared

Dormancy stores combat power.

It does not create combat power for free.

A concealed dormant reserve therefore represents real prior investment that could have been used elsewhere.

---

## 21. Triggered Emergence

**Design status: Established principle**

Triggered emergence creates delayed payoff for earlier planning.

The desired player experience is:

> **I prepared this weeks ago, and now it matters.**

It is not:

> **I pressed a button and an army appeared.**

Triggered emergence can produce dramatic local reversals because the visible manifestation of the force occurs much later than the decisions that created it.

This is a core expression of Plastai strategic planning.

---

## 22. Plastai Concentration

**Design status: Hard rule**

Plastai force may appear suddenly in local terms while still requiring substantial prior preparation.

This distinction is fundamental.

To Vanguard, an emergence may look abrupt.

To the Plastai player, that emergence represents:

* scouting
* biomass collection
* nutrient routing
* brood development
* positioning
* concealment
* timing

The final emergence is the visible end of a hidden logistical process.

---

## 23. Plastai Vulnerability to Discovery

**Design status: Hard rule**

Preparation creates power and vulnerability.

When Vanguard correctly identifies a major Plastai development site, concentrated Vanguard firepower may devastate it.

That outcome is intentional.

The strategic tension is:

**Plastai benefits from concealment and distributed preparation.**

**Vanguard benefits from accurate detection followed by concentrated destruction.**

Neither receives guaranteed success.

A discovered brood site is not automatically destroyed if Vanguard lacks available strike capability.

A concealed brood site is not automatically safe if its preparation produces signals that an attentive player can interpret.

---

## 24. Reconnaissance–Strike Loop

**Design status: Established principle**

A core Vanguard combat loop is:

> **Detect → Classify → Confirm → Position → Strike → Assess**

Each stage matters.

Detection without classification creates uncertainty.

Classification without confirmation risks attacking the wrong target.

Confirmation without available force produces no immediate effect.

Positioning without logistics may create an unsustainable concentration.

A strike without assessment leaves the player uncertain about the result.

For Vanguard, the difficult part should often be identifying the correct target and arranging the strike rather than discovering that advanced weapons somehow cannot destroy it.

---

## 25. Prepare–Conceal–Emerge Loop

**Design status: Established principle**

A corresponding Plastai combat loop is:

> **Gather → Route → Develop → Conceal → Position → Emerge → Adapt**

The two faction loops interact asymmetrically.

Vanguard attempts to discover what Plastai is preparing before that preparation becomes combat power.

Plastai attempts to complete preparation without revealing enough information for Vanguard to intervene effectively.

The resulting conflict begins well before direct combat.

---

## 26. Combat as Commitment

**Design status: Hard rule**

Using combat power creates opportunity cost.

Examples include:

* aircraft committed north are unavailable south
* artillery moved into one valley cannot immediately cover another
* fuel consumed supporting one campaign is unavailable elsewhere
* nutrients used for combat organisms are unavailable for expansion
* biomass converted into one form cannot simultaneously become another
* dormant broods prepared near one corridor represent investment not available elsewhere

Commitment also exposes intent.

Large movements, infrastructure changes, sortie patterns, nutrient routing, and other preparations may create signals.

A player gains local strength by accepting strategic exposure elsewhere.

---

## 27. Simultaneous Threats

**Design status: Established principle**

The scale of the map allows multiple competing crises to exist simultaneously.

Examples include:

* Plastai pressure two distant Vanguard facilities
* Vanguard stages attacks against several suspected brood regions
* weather removes a previously available response option
* logistics disruption coincides with combat
* a remote reconnaissance contact appears while another battle is already escalating

These situations force prioritization.

The player should not always have enough attention, time, or locally available force to solve every crisis perfectly.

This is especially important for limiting Vanguard's otherwise enormous technological power.

---

## 28. Force Preservation

**Design status: Established principle**

Combat does not always end in total destruction.

Players can:

* disengage
* withdraw
* reroute
* hide
* abandon terrain
* preserve valuable units
* preserve scarce organisms
* preserve personnel

A battle can be lost locally while preserving forces for later use.

Conversely, holding an objective at the cost of irreplaceable capability may constitute a strategic defeat.

Force preservation is therefore a legitimate combat objective.

---

## 29. Retreat and Withdrawal

**Design status: Intended gameplay behavior**

Retreat matters because movement takes time and geography constrains escape.

Withdrawal interacts with:

* route availability
* terrain
* damaged systems
* pursuit
* infrastructure
* reinforcement
* concealment
* overextension

A retreating force may sacrifice territory while preserving combat capability.

A pursuing force may create opportunities while simultaneously stretching beyond its own logistics or support.

Withdrawal is a strategic decision rather than a generic failure state.

---

## 30. Attrition

**Design status: Established principle**

Attrition emerges from concrete strategic costs.

It can result from:

* repeated engagements
* maintenance load
* ammunition expenditure
* fuel expenditure
* combat losses
* resource drain
* ecological depletion
* infrastructure damage
* personnel loss
* disrupted transport

Attrition is not primarily represented as arbitrary passive health loss.

A prolonged campaign becomes costly because sustaining combat consumes real capability and degrades the systems supporting continued operations.

---

## 31. Damage Persistence

**Design status: Hard rule**

Combat changes the map state.

Persistent consequences can include:

* destroyed infrastructure
* damaged bridges
* cratered routes
* fires
* depleted ammunition
* lost personnel
* dead organisms
* ecological disruption
* altered migration
* damaged logistics
* exposed positions
* abandoned facilities

Battlefields do not simply reset after combat.

A destroyed bridge can alter the next campaign.

A wildfire can change future ecology.

A damaged airfield can reshape regional air coverage.

A revealed brood site can remain strategically compromised even if it survives.

---

## 32. Infrastructure as Combat Objective

**Design status: Established principle**

Infrastructure may be more strategically important than the units currently defending it.

Important targets can include:

* airfields
* rail junctions
* bridges
* depots
* refineries
* communications relays
* orbital receiving sites
* brood sites
* nutrient infrastructure
* aquifer access points

Destroying a transport node may matter more than destroying a patrol.

Disabling an airfield can reduce power projection across an entire region.

Destroying a nutrient hub can disrupt future Plastai development even if few combat organisms are present.

Combat therefore frequently targets systems rather than armies.

---

## 33. Human Personnel as Combat Objectives

**Design status: Established principle**

Vanguard personnel are unusually valuable targets because human presence is deliberately scarce.

A Plastai attack that penetrates an underdefended facility and kills rare personnel can therefore create strategic consequences far beyond the number of casualties involved.

Personnel loss is not reduced to a generic kill-count mechanic.

Its significance comes from what those people were required to operate, maintain, command, construct, or support.

---

## 34. Brood and Development Sites as Combat Objectives

**Design status: Established principle**

Vanguard can target future Plastai capability before that capability reaches the battlefield.

Relevant targets include:

* brood sites
* developmental infrastructure
* nutrient hubs
* emergence preparations
* major concentrations of developing organisms

Destroying future capability before maturation is a major form of counterplay.

The same principle that makes scarce Vanguard humans strategically valuable makes immature or prepared Plastai capability strategically valuable.

Both factions possess assets whose importance exceeds their immediate battlefield damage output.

---

## 35. Combat and Weather

**Design status: Hard rule**

Combat follows the weather rules established in `world/weather.md`.

Weather can alter:

* aviation
* visibility
* targeting
* movement
* fires
* communications
* ground conditions
* information quality

Weather does not simply apply a universal combat percentage modifier.

A thunderstorm may ground aircraft, reduce sensor effectiveness, create fire risk in a dry region, alter movement conditions, or provide concealment.

The same weather event can therefore advantage one operation while disrupting another.

Combat adapts to world state.

---

## 36. Combat and Ecology

**Design status: Established principle**

Combat interacts with the ecological systems defined in `world/ecology.md`.

Combat can:

* scatter wildlife
* destroy biomass
* create carcasses
* trigger migration
* ignite fires
* alter habitats
* create temporary ecological disturbances

These effects can feed back into future strategy.

A region repeatedly bombarded by Vanguard may become less valuable as a biomass source.

A battle may redirect wildlife into another region.

Fire may temporarily deny terrain or permanently alter its ecological character.

Combat therefore affects more than the forces directly involved.

---

## 37. Combat and Logistics

**Design status: Hard rule**

Combat depends on the resource and logistics systems defined in `systems/resources-logistics.md`.

Combat consumes and depends on:

* fuel
* ammunition
* nutrients
* repair capacity
* transport
* throughput
* reserves
* replacement capability

Neither faction fights at maximum intensity indefinitely without logistical consequence.

High combat power requires support.

Attacking that support can therefore be more effective than attacking the combat force directly.

---

## 38. Combat and Time

**Design status: Established principle**

Combat interacts with the time-compression systems defined in `systems/time.md`.

Different engagements can justify different levels of player attention.

Minor or predictable engagements may continue without forcing a complete pause.

Major threats may cause time to slow.

Critical events may trigger a pause according to the game's assistance model and player-configured triggers.

Simultaneous engagements may compete for attention.

The purpose of the time system is not to force the player to personally supervise every weapons exchange.

It is to give the player enough opportunity to intervene when meaningful strategic decisions become available.

---

## 39. Combat and Signals

**Design status: Hard rule**

Combat participates in the information environment defined in `systems/signals.md`.

Combat produces strong signals, including:

* explosions
* fires
* communications traffic
* movement
* aircraft activity
* ecological disturbance
* damaged infrastructure
* changes in logistics patterns

Fighting reveals capability and intent.

Using a previously unknown weapon system can teach the opponent something.

Deploying aircraft to one region reveals operational interest.

A sudden ecological disturbance may reveal activity even when direct observation remains poor.

Combat therefore generates information as well as destruction.

---

## 40. Surprise

**Design status: Hard rule**

Surprise emerges from incomplete information rather than arbitrary invisible spawning.

Sources of surprise include:

* deception
* stale intelligence
* hidden preparation
* unusual routes
* weather
* timing
* incomplete classification
* dormant forces
* misinterpreted signals

A surprise attack usually has detectable precursors.

The opponent may fail to observe them, misclassify them, misunderstand them, or choose not to act.

This makes surprise fair at the system level even when its local result is devastating.

---

## 41. Ambush

**Design status: Established principle**

Both factions can create ambushes, but they do so differently.

Plastai may exploit:

* concealment
* terrain
* dormancy
* ecological noise
* prepared emergence
* unusual movement through aquifer-connected regions

Vanguard may exploit:

* sensor superiority
* stand-off weapons
* remote systems
* prepared fire zones
* hidden artillery coverage
* unexpected aviation response

Ambush is not represented primarily as a generic damage multiplier.

Its advantage comes from position, information asymmetry, timing, and preparedness.

---

## 42. Overwhelming Local Force

**Design status: Hard rule**

Project Signal allows moments in which local combat feels brutally one-sided because one player successfully arranged the strategic conditions for overwhelming force.

Examples include:

* Vanguard identifies a brood site and destroys it with concentrated firepower.
* Plastai emerges against an underdefended refinery after drawing Vanguard response elsewhere.
* A facility containing rare human personnel is unexpectedly overrun.
* Aircraft arrive at exactly the critical moment.
* A Titan-scale organism emerges because the opponent detected but failed to disrupt its preparation.

These moments can feel unfair locally.

They remain fair globally when the setup required investment, created signals, imposed opportunity costs, and provided opportunities for counterplay.

The desired emotional response from one player may be:

> **This is bullshit.**

while the other thinks:

> **My plan worked.**

That is acceptable when the local imbalance is the payoff for successful strategic preparation.

---

## 43. Counterplay Before Contact

**Design status: Hard rule**

The strongest counterplay in Project Signal often occurs before combat.

Examples include:

* detecting buildup
* disrupting logistics
* destroying infrastructure
* denying biomass
* changing basing
* rerouting forces
* evacuating personnel
* deceiving the opponent
* preparing reserves
* repositioning sensors
* abandoning vulnerable infrastructure
* forcing enemy commitment elsewhere

A powerful capability does not require an equally powerful direct battlefield counter.

The opponent may instead counter the conditions necessary for that capability to be used effectively.

This principle is essential to maintaining faction asymmetry.

---

## 44. Counterplay During Combat

**Design status: Intended gameplay behavior**

In-combat decisions still matter.

Possible responses include:

* retreat
* reinforcement
* rerouting air support
* changing targets
* using terrain
* dispersing
* concentrating
* abandoning objectives
* activating prepared reserves
* protecting evacuation routes
* choosing not to escalate further

Tactical decisions can improve or worsen an engagement.

They do not routinely overturn overwhelming strategic disadvantage through superior clicking.

---

## 45. Asymmetric Combat Balance

**Design status: Hard rule**

Balance does not require equivalent tools.

Vanguard does not need a mechanical equivalent of the Plastai aquifer network.

Plastai does not need a biological equivalent of a bomber.

The factions interact through different strengths and vulnerabilities.

Balance emerges from:

* information
* geography
* time
* logistics
* preparation
* vulnerability
* opportunity cost
* competing objectives

The question is not whether both factions possess the same answer.

The question is whether each faction can create meaningful strategic problems for the other and whether those problems have understandable opportunities for response.

---

## 46. No Universal Hard Counter Matrix

**Design status: Hard rule**

Combat does not reduce to a universal rock-paper-scissors counter chart.

Avoid designing the game around patterns such as:

* aircraft counter X
* X counters artillery
* artillery counters Y
* Y counters aircraft

Natural hard counters can exist where they make physical or strategic sense.

They are not the foundation of combat balance.

Position, preparation, support, information, geography, and timing matter more than memorizing a universal counter table.

---

## 47. Unit Value

**Design status: Established principle**

A unit's value comes from more than raw combat statistics.

Value depends on:

* role
* location
* support
* information
* timing
* scarcity
* mobility
* readiness

A modest unit in the correct place may be more valuable than a powerful force stranded hundreds of kilometers away.

A lightly armed reconnaissance system may enable a devastating strike.

A transport unit may determine whether a regional defense can be sustained.

A biologically specialized organism may be nearly useless in one environment and decisive in another.

Combat value is contextual.

---

## 48. Combat Scale

**Design status: Established principle**

Combat can occur at many scales, including:

* reconnaissance clashes
* convoy attacks
* raids
* infrastructure defense
* regional battles
* major assaults
* mass emergence events

Not every contact deserves equal attention.

The combat system must support small interactions without making them feel identical to regional crises.

Likewise, large battles should feel significant because of the forces, resources, infrastructure, and strategic commitments involved rather than simply because more units appear on screen.

---

## 49. Tactical Detail

**Design status: Established principle with unresolved implementation**

Project Signal represents enough tactical detail for the following to matter:

* terrain
* range
* positioning
* force composition
* support
* retreat
* movement
* local concentration

It does not become a traditional squad-level tactical game.

The player operates at a higher level of command.

Exactly how much direct moment-to-moment unit control remains unresolved.

The final tactical abstraction should preserve strategic causality without creating constant micro-management.

---

## 50. Automation and Command

**Design status: Intended gameplay behavior**

Low-level combat behavior should be partially automated where doing so preserves strategic focus.

Possible controlled behaviors include:

* engagement rules
* target priorities
* defensive behavior
* retreat thresholds
* autonomy levels
* preservation priorities

The player should normally express strategic intent rather than manually control every weapon cycle.

Automation does not remove meaningful decisions.

It moves those decisions toward doctrine, preparation, commitment, positioning, and intervention.

---

## 51. Rules of Engagement

**Design status: Intended gameplay behavior**

Players may be able to define behavioral instructions such as:

* engage on contact
* observe only
* avoid combat
* defend an area
* withdraw under specified conditions
* prioritize infrastructure
* preserve personnel
* preserve specific valuable units

These instructions allow forces to behave sensibly while time is compressed or the player's attention is elsewhere.

The exact user interface and command model remain unresolved.

---

## 52. Combat Outcome Legibility

**Design status: Hard rule**

The player should be able to understand why an engagement went the way it did.

Relevant explanations can include:

* insufficient ammunition
* poor sensor coverage
* unavailable air support
* weather
* terrain
* overwhelming local numbers
* delayed reinforcement
* infrastructure loss
* surprise emergence
* stale targeting information
* interrupted logistics

Combat resolution should not feel opaque or arbitrary.

The game does not need to expose every internal calculation, but cause and effect must remain interpretable.

The player should be able to learn from defeat.

---

## 53. Catastrophic Loss

**Design status: Hard rule**

Catastrophic outcomes are acceptable when they follow understandable system interaction.

Project Signal allows:

* a brood site to be annihilated
* a convoy to be wiped out
* a refinery to be overrun
* rare humans to be killed
* aircraft to be lost
* a major organism to die before achieving its intended purpose
* infrastructure networks to collapse regionally

The game does not smooth every failure into minor attrition merely to protect the player.

Strategic mistakes can have severe consequences.

Those consequences must remain traceable to understandable causes.

---

## 54. Recovery After Combat

**Design status: Established principle**

The post-battle strategic state matters as much as the immediate battlefield result.

After combat, players may need to:

* repair
* replace
* reroute
* rebuild
* harvest losses where biologically appropriate
* reorganize
* reassess intelligence
* change priorities
* alter regional posture

A victorious force may still be exhausted, under-supplied, exposed, or unable to exploit its success.

A defeated force may preserve enough capability to recover elsewhere.

Combat therefore creates the next strategic problem.

---

## 55. Escalation

**Design status: Intended gameplay behavior**

Combat can escalate naturally through player commitment.

A small contact may lead to:

1. reconnaissance,
2. reinforcement,
3. artillery commitment,
4. aircraft commitment,
5. brood activation,
6. infrastructure attacks,
7. wider regional conflict.

Escalation is not automatic.

Players choose whether increasing commitment is worth the cost.

A minor contact may therefore remain minor if neither player believes escalation serves a strategic purpose.

---

## 56. Deterrence

**Design status: Established principle**

Combat capability influences behavior even when it is never used.

Possible deterrents include:

* known air coverage
* suspected artillery
* known brood concentration
* dangerous terrain
* fortified infrastructure
* suspected dormant capability
* uncertain enemy reserves

Deterrence emerges from player knowledge and belief.

A capability does not need to fire in order to shape the opponent's decisions.

Uncertainty can itself become strategically meaningful.

---

## 57. Combat and Deception

**Design status: Established principle**

Combat preparation can be used deceptively.

Examples include:

* staging aircraft to suggest an attack
* visible force concentration
* concealed dormant forces
* intentionally exposed targets
* feigned withdrawal
* false regional buildup
* attacks designed primarily to draw response away from another theater
* conspicuous infrastructure preparation

Deception is strongest when it uses real systems and credible commitments.

A convincing false buildup may consume real resources.

That cost is desirable because it prevents deception from becoming free information noise.

---

## 58. Strategic Objectives

**Design status: Hard rule**

Not every battle is fought for annihilation.

Combat objectives can include:

* delay
* disruption
* infrastructure destruction
* personnel rescue
* biomass denial
* reconnaissance
* forcing relocation
* protecting construction
* concealing another operation
* buying time
* provoking commitment
* creating or denying access

A force can accomplish its strategic objective without destroying the opposing force.

Likewise, destroying many enemy units does not automatically constitute success if the actual objective fails.

---

## 59. Endgame Combat

**Design status: Established principle**

Endgame conflict emerges around major strategic projects, networks, regional transformation, and increasingly consequential commitments.

Examples include:

* defending Vanguard extraction or orbital infrastructure
* disrupting major Plastai projects
* defending or preventing mass emergence
* establishing regional denial
* destroying critical networks
* protecting the infrastructure required for a final strategic objective

Endgame combat does not collapse into both players sending every surviving unit toward a single final base.

The same principles of geography, information, logistics, preparation, and distributed commitment continue to apply at larger stakes.

---

## 60. Emergent Narrative

**Design status: Intended gameplay behavior**

The combat system should naturally generate stories such as:

* Vanguard destroys what appears to be a critical brood site only to discover that the site was a diversion.
* Plastai overruns an underdefended refinery while Vanguard aircraft are committed hundreds of kilometers away.
* A thunderstorm grounds air support during a critical assault.
* Destruction of a rail connection delays reinforcement long enough for an entire region to collapse.
* Dormant organisms emerge exactly where Vanguard suspected activity but chose not to investigate.
* A convoy survives by abandoning the direct route and rerouting through a longer valley.
* Both factions avoid a battle because the cost of commitment is greater than the objective is worth.
* Vanguard destroys a developing Plastai concentration before it ever becomes a battle.
* Plastai attacks infrastructure not to hold it, but to force Vanguard aircraft away from another theater.

These stories arise from interacting systems rather than scripted combat events.

---

## 61. What Combat Must Never Become

**Design status: Hard guardrails**

Combat in Project Signal must never become:

* an APM competition
* StarCraft combat placed onto a giant map
* balanced primarily through arbitrary damage and HP tuning
* a universal rock-paper-scissors counter chart
* instant cross-map reinforcement
* aircraft that solve every strategic problem
* universal anti-air as the only answer to Vanguard aviation
* instant Plastai spawning
* aquifer teleportation
* combat detached from logistics
* combat detached from information
* every encounter becoming a tactical minigame
* a system where powerful capabilities must feel weak to be fair
* hidden attacks with no potentially detectable preparation
* a system where every battle ends in total annihilation
* terrain functioning as cosmetic decoration
* weather functioning as a generic percentage modifier
* infrastructure loss being strategically irrelevant
* armies materializing simply because resources exist globally
* combat capability being independent of where infrastructure and forces are located
* tactical clicking routinely overcoming catastrophic strategic preparation errors
* symmetry created by giving both factions renamed versions of the same tools
* large weapons becoming universally available merely because they have been unlocked
* surprise being generated through arbitrary information withholding rather than imperfect information
* victory being measured primarily through body count

The combat system protects the identity of Project Signal by remaining subordinate to the larger strategic simulation.

---

## 62. Open Design Questions

**Design status: Unresolved**

The following questions remain open and should not be prematurely locked:

* What exact level of direct tactical control does the player receive?
* What combat resolution model best supports strategic causality without excessive simulation cost?
* How are individual units grouped, selected, and commanded?
* What role, if any, do explicit formations play?
* How detailed are unit autonomy behaviors?
* How are damage, disablement, repair, and recovery abstracted?
* How are retreat and disengagement mechanically executed?
* How are Vanguard air missions assigned and controlled?
* What exact workflow governs artillery and long-range targeting?
* How much direct anti-air capability should Plastai eventually possess?
* What combat information belongs in the primary interface?
* How are wounded Vanguard personnel represented, if at all?
* How are extremely large engagements simulated and rendered efficiently?
* How are underwater and aquatic engagements handled if aquatic warfare becomes a major system?
* How much individual biological behavior is simulated within large Plastai concentrations?
* How much direct targeting control should coexist with doctrine and automation?

These are implementation and refinement questions.

They do not reopen the established principles that combat is driven primarily by information, geography, preparation, logistics, distance, and commitment.

---

## 63. Combat and Power Projection Design Test

Any future combat mechanic should be evaluated against the following questions:

* Does this reward preparation?
* Does information matter before the fight?
* Does geography matter?
* Does distance matter?
* Does logistics constrain this capability?
* Does the capability have a real geographic footprint?
* Can the opponent observe or infer preparation?
* Can the opponent counter it before direct contact?
* Does commitment create vulnerability elsewhere?
* Does this reinforce faction asymmetry?
* Does it avoid requiring equivalent tools on both sides?
* Can powerful capabilities remain genuinely powerful?
* Does local power depend on positioning and support rather than global ownership?
* Does the player understand why the outcome occurred?
* Does combat leave persistent consequences?
* Can withdrawal or preservation sometimes be preferable to annihilation?
* Does this create meaningful opportunity cost?
* Does this avoid unnecessary tactical micro-management?
* Does this interact with signals rather than bypass them?
* Does this interact with logistics rather than bypass them?
* Does this interact with the physical map rather than ignore it?
* Could this create an emergent story?

The governing combat principle is:

> **Power should be frightening when it is correctly positioned, supplied, informed, and prepared — and limited primarily by the difficulty of making all four true in the right place at the right time.**
