# Plastai Faction Design

## 1. Faction Identity

The Plastai are an individually intelligent alien species whose civilization expresses technology through biology rather than conventional manufactured tools and machinery.

Their defining capabilities arise from three connected biological systems:

- extreme developmental plasticity through the NYMPH → COCOON → MATURE lifecycle,
- genetic assimilation of useful biological mechanisms from other life,
- a civilization-scale nutrient economy connected through underground aquifer systems.

The Plastai do not manufacture an equivalent of every Vanguard machine. They solve problems by controlling development, combining acquired biological mechanisms, exploiting ecology, specializing mature phenotypes, and manipulating living systems.

Their strategic identity is built around preparation rather than immediate projection. Nutrients can be gathered in one region, moved through connected aquifers, concentrated elsewhere, and committed to organisms that may remain concealed or dormant until needed.

The Plastai therefore excel at hidden buildup, adaptation, distributed presence, ecological exploitation, and sudden concentration of previously prepared force.

They are not a hive mind, an instant-spawn swarm, or a conventional RTS faction reskinned with biological graphics.

## 2. Hard Rules

The following rules define the Plastai and should not be violated by future mechanics.

- Every Plastai is an individually intelligent organism.
- The Plastai are explicitly not a hive mind.
- Large-scale coordination does not imply shared consciousness or loss of individuality.
- Plastai civilization does not rely on conventional manufactured tool technology.
- Their primary technological paradigm is biological: capability is expressed through organisms, developmental specialization, symbiosis, and environmental manipulation.
- Plastai mechanics should not simply reproduce Vanguard machinery using biological visuals.
- Every Plastai follows the lifecycle NYMPH → COCOON → MATURE.
- Mature organisms require physical development. Combat units cannot simply be spawned instantly because resources are available.
- Extreme phenotype polymorphism is fundamental to the faction.
- Mature form follows function. Specialized organisms may differ radically in size, anatomy, locomotion, metabolism, protection, senses, and combat capability while remaining products of the same underlying lineage.
- Genetic assimilation acquires biological mechanisms, not copies of whole animals.
- The planetary biosphere is a source of biological capability as well as biomass.
- Acquiring a genetic mechanism does not automatically grant unlimited practical use of that mechanism. Developmental capacity, resources, maturation requirements, and biological constraints still matter.
- Surface biomass and other useful material must be physically gathered and processed before being used.
- Nutrient movement is a physical logistical system, not a faction-wide abstract resource wallet.
- Underground aquifers form the core of Plastai strategic nutrient circulation.
- Aquifer movement is constrained by geography, connectivity, throughput, distance, access, and infrastructure.
- The aquifer system must never become unrestricted teleportation.
- Creating new aquifer surface access is possible but represents a major investment.
- Plastai may move biological potential through their network more easily than they move finished mature organisms.
- Plastai forces may emerge suddenly, but they are never created suddenly.
- Dormancy and delayed emergence require the organisms to have already been developed and paid for.
- Major biological projects require preparation.
- Preparation produces physical consequences.
- Physical consequences can produce observable signals.
- Plastai information advantages arise from concealment, preparation, ecology, geography, and enemy uncertainty rather than magical omniscience.
- Powerful Plastai strategies must have possible counterplay when the opponent correctly discovers and interprets their preparation.
- Plastai actions should obey the wider Project Signal principle of physical causality: effects occur because something within the simulated world causes them.
- Plastai strategic advantages should not eliminate the importance of the contested surface world.
- The Plastai can contest the air through appropriate biological adaptations and ground-based capabilities, but Vanguard airpower remains a distinct factional strength.
- Conversely, Vanguard may detect, disrupt, or locally attack the aquifer system without becoming equivalent to the Plastai within the subterranean domain.

## 3. Player Fantasy

Playing the Plastai means thinking ahead.

The player does not primarily ask how to manufacture the largest conventional army and move it directly toward the enemy. The player manipulates biology, ecology, geography, developmental time, and nutrient flow to create future opportunities.

A successful Plastai player:

- discovers useful biological mechanisms,
- exploits ecological opportunities,
- establishes and expands brood infrastructure,
- routes nutrients through the aquifer network,
- prepares phenotypes for anticipated problems,
- hides strategic investment among ordinary biological activity,
- establishes new points of emergence,
- maintains dormant or delayed forces,
- shifts pressure between geographically separated regions,
- allows the opponent to form incomplete or incorrect interpretations,
- and concentrates prepared force at the moment it becomes useful.

The ideal Plastai strategic moment is not:

> **A unit became available and was spawned.**

It is:

> **The conditions for this event were prepared long ago, and now the plan works.**

The opponent should sometimes experience Plastai actions as sudden or surprising. When reconstructed afterward, however, those actions should have a physical history within the simulation.

## 4. Biological Civilization

Plastai civilization solves technological problems primarily through biology.

Vanguard civilization typically separates the operator from the capability:

> **person + machine + infrastructure = capability**

The Plastai instead tend to embody capability directly:

> **phenotype + biological support system = capability**

A requirement for excavation may produce specialized digging anatomy. A requirement for long-range detection may produce specialized sensory organisms. Aquatic operations may require radically aquatic phenotypes rather than conventional vehicles adapted for water.

This does not mean individual Plastai are incapable of manipulating objects, moving material, constructing earthworks, excavating terrain, or interacting intelligently with their environment. It means persistent manufactured tools and machinery are not the foundation of their technological progression.

Biological specialization can occur through:

- specialized mature phenotypes,
- symbiotic organisms,
- dedicated biological infrastructure,
- environmental engineering,
- specialized sensory systems,
- biological weapons and defenses,
- nutrient-processing organisms,
- brood and developmental organisms,
- and combinations of acquired biological mechanisms.

Function determines form.

A flying Plastai should not simply be a normal ground warrior with wings attached. A ranged phenotype should possess anatomy built around producing, storing, aiming, and launching its biological projectile. An aquatic organism should reflect the structural consequences of aquatic existence.

The faction must avoid becoming a catalog of human machines recreated in flesh.

## 5. Intelligence and Individuality

Every Plastai is individually sapient.

They are not drones. They do not share a single consciousness. There is no faction-wide telepathic awareness, central biological mind, or automatic sharing of everything perceived by every organism.

Extreme specialization does not negate intelligence.

A small scout, massive combat phenotype, aquatic specialist, excavator, or nutrient-handling organism can still be an intelligent individual even when its mature anatomy is profoundly specialized for a particular function.

Large-scale Plastai coordination represents communication, organization, biological infrastructure, and deliberate action rather than hive consciousness.

This distinction is essential to faction identity. The Plastai may superficially resemble eusocial or swarm organisms because of their specialization and enormous biological coordination, but they are not an insect-hive civilization.

Project Signal does not need to simulate Plastai politics, domestic life, belief systems, or broader social institutions. Individual intelligence is established because it defines what the faction is, not because the game is a society simulation.

## 6. Lifecycle and Development

Every Plastai follows:

> **NYMPH → COCOON → MATURE**

The nymph represents generalized developmental potential.

The cocoon is the period in which the organism undergoes profound biological reorganization and commits to a mature phenotype.

The mature organism expresses the developmental choices made during this process.

This lifecycle is foundational to Plastai gameplay.

A mature phenotype cannot appear immediately when nutrients become available. Nutrients must be committed, development must occur, and maturation takes time.

This creates a strategic distinction between resources, developing capability, and finished capability.

Enemy interference during development therefore matters. Destroying a brood site, interrupting nutrient supply, discovering hidden cocoons, or attacking during a vulnerable developmental period can prevent resources already committed from becoming usable mature organisms.

The lifecycle prevents Plastai production from behaving like conventional RTS instant unit queues.

Developmental choices can create radically different mature organisms from the same underlying species. Different forms may emphasize locomotion, armor, sensory capability, metabolism, combat, aquatic function, excavation, camouflage, or other strategic functions.

Developmental plasticity is therefore the foundation upon which genetic assimilation and phenotype design operate.

## 7. Polymorphism and Cast Forms

Extreme mature-form polymorphism is fundamental to Plastai biology.

Mature Plastai may vary dramatically in:

- size,
- proportions,
- locomotion,
- sensory organs,
- musculature,
- integument,
- skeletal structure,
- metabolism,
- respiratory systems,
- environmental tolerance,
- offensive anatomy,
- and strategic function.

Specialized forms may support:

- surface movement,
- scouting,
- reconnaissance,
- direct combat,
- ranged biological attack,
- excavation,
- nutrient transport and processing,
- aquatic activity,
- aerial activity,
- brood establishment,
- defense,
- camouflage,
- environmental manipulation,
- megafaunal-scale operations,
- and other specialized functions.

These are developmental outcomes rather than separate conventional unit species.

A shared Plastai lineage should remain biologically coherent even when mature silhouettes become extremely different.

The faction should favor specialized solutions over a single universally optimal combat body.

## 8. Genetic Assimilation

Genetic assimilation allows the Plastai to acquire useful biological mechanisms from other life.

Assimilation does not mean copying an organism wholesale.

Assimilating a flying organism does not unlock that animal as a Plastai unit. It may instead expose mechanisms such as:

- lightweight structural tissue,
- efficient respiration,
- aerodynamic surfaces,
- balance organs,
- or other useful adaptations.

A subterranean organism might provide mechanisms related to vibration sensing, excavation, low-oxygen tolerance, or subterranean navigation.

Other organisms may provide mechanisms for thermal tolerance, camouflage, toxins, armor, regeneration, aquatic function, unusual metabolism, or sensory capability.

These mechanisms become part of the Plastai developmental repertoire and may be recombined into phenotypes serving entirely different purposes from the organism from which the mechanism originated.

The biosphere therefore functions partly as a technological landscape.

Discovering unusual fauna can create the question:

> **What biological solution does that organism possess, and what could the Plastai do with it?**

Acquisition does not equal immediate mastery. Particularly sophisticated mechanisms may require advanced developmental capacity, substantial nutrient investment, difficult maturation, or compatible supporting traits.

Low-complexity mechanisms also remain useful. Progression should not reduce assimilation to a linear sequence where every later trait simply replaces an earlier one.

## 9. Nutrient Economy

The Plastai depend on the living world for large portions of their strategic resource economy.

Surface activity gathers biomass and other useful material from the environment. This material is processed into forms suitable for storage, biological development, maintenance, and subterranean transport.

The strategic loop is broadly:

> **Harvest → Process → Store → Circulate → Allocate → Develop**

Nutrients can be invested in:

- new organisms,
- brood expansion,
- biological infrastructure,
- aquifer expansion,
- stored reserves,
- ecological projects,
- replacement forces,
- specialized phenotypes,
- and extremely large biological projects.

These uses compete with one another.

A major investment in hidden future capability therefore represents resources that are not currently being expressed elsewhere.

The nutrient economy should remain strategic. The player should make meaningful decisions about sources, capacity, routing, reserves, and major allocations without manually managing biological pipes or individual units of nutrient slurry.

Biomass is not simply "Plastai money." Geography, processing, developmental capacity, nutrient composition, transport access, time, and ecological sustainability all matter.

## 10. Aquifer Network

Underground aquifers form the Plastai strategic nutrient circulation network.

Surface brood sites gather and process resources, but those resources do not necessarily remain where they were collected. Suitable nutrients can enter the subterranean network and move toward other connected Plastai sites.

The aquifer system therefore allows the Plastai to separate:

> **where resources are harvested**

> **from**

> **where biological capability is developed.**

This is one of the faction's defining strategic advantages.

Natural interfaces such as springs, lakes, wetlands, and other viable groundwater access points can connect surface brood activity to the underground network.

Brood-establishing or other specialized developmental organisms can travel through suitable parts of this network and establish activity at connected surface interfaces.

Aquifer movement remains physical.

Its effectiveness depends upon:

- hydrological connectivity,
- geography,
- distance,
- network capacity,
- nutrient throughput,
- viable access points,
- biological infrastructure,
- and investment.

Different regions may have limited or disconnected groundwater systems. Moving enormous quantities of material should create different constraints from moving small founder organisms.

The Plastai can also create new artificial surface interfaces at great resource expense.

This allows them to establish a brood site somewhere Vanguard did not previously consider a natural emergence point. Artificial interfaces prevent the strategic map from becoming a memorization exercise in which Vanguard permanently learns every possible Plastai surface location.

Creating a new interface is a major project. It requires preparation and produces physical consequences that may be detectable through appropriate observation.

The aquifer network enables strategic repositioning without becoming teleportation.

The Plastai can route nutrients away from one region, establish developmental capability elsewhere, and grow future forces at the destination. Existing mature armies do not simply disappear from one site and instantly appear at another.

The central asymmetry is:

> **Vanguard primarily transports finished capability. Plastai can transport the biological potential from which future capability is grown.**

## 11. Broods, Dormancy, and Triggered Emergence

Plastai brood systems can prepare organisms before their immediate deployment.

Once nutrients have been committed and development has occurred, appropriate organisms may remain dormant or in a biologically arrested state until emergence is desirable.

This creates a distinction between:

- nutrients awaiting commitment,
- developing organisms,
- prepared dormant organisms,
- and active mature organisms.

Dormancy does not create units for free.

The organism already exists. Its resources have already been committed. Developmental time has already occurred. Maintaining dormant organisms may still impose metabolic, environmental, capacity, or biological costs.

Different phenotypes may have different abilities to tolerate long-term dormancy.

Emergence may be deliberately delayed.

Prepared organisms may also use biologically plausible triggers for emergence, including appropriate environmental or sensory conditions. Triggered emergence must remain grounded in physical detection and biological response rather than functioning as a magical ambush command.

This permits brood sites to contain latent military potential.

A site with little visible surface activity may therefore still contain substantial prepared capability.

However, hidden preparation should create indirect signals. Nutrients had to arrive. Development consumed resources. Cocoons occupy physical space. Brood infrastructure had to support them. Depending on Vanguard reconnaissance capability, these consequences may be detectable.

The governing rule is:

> **Plastai forces may emerge suddenly, but they are never spawned suddenly.**

If a large force erupts from a previously quiet region, the simulation should be able to account for where its biomass came from, where it developed, how long development took, where it was stored, and why it emerged.

## 12. Mobility

Plastai mobility is biological and terrain-dependent.

Different mature phenotypes may specialize in:

- walking or running,
- climbing,
- swimming,
- amphibious movement,
- burrowing or excavation,
- aerial movement,
- or other biologically supported locomotion.

Surface forces physically traverse terrain according to their anatomy and environmental conditions.

Aquifer movement provides a separate form of strategic mobility. Founder organisms, nutrients, and other suitable biological material can move through connected subterranean water systems, allowing the Plastai to establish or reinforce developmental potential far from the original harvesting location.

This differs fundamentally from Vanguard mechanized mobility.

Vanguard tends to move existing capability rapidly through transportation infrastructure and airpower.

The Plastai can instead move biological resources and developmental potential through concealed subterranean geography and create capability near its eventual point of use.

Aquifer access does not eliminate surface geography. Mature surface forces still face terrain, distance, exposure, and the physical limitations of their phenotype.

## 13. Ecology and Biomass

The Plastai are deeply dependent upon the living world.

Wildlife can provide biomass, nutrients, and genetic mechanisms. Vegetation and other biological productivity contribute to the resource base. Megafauna can represent both enormous resource opportunities and dangerous biological targets.

Migration matters because resources move independently of faction control.

Ecological productivity matters because a region that is rich today can be degraded tomorrow.

Weather and environmental conditions can alter:

- biological productivity,
- wildlife distribution,
- migration,
- water availability,
- brood suitability,
- aquifer behavior,
- and harvesting conditions.

Overexploitation should have consequences.

A Plastai player who extracts too aggressively from a region may reduce its future productivity, disrupt migration, alter local ecology, or create highly visible ecological signals.

The biosphere is therefore not a collection of static resource nodes.

It is a changing strategic system that the Plastai exploit, depend upon, learn from, and potentially alter.

## 14. Megafauna and Titan-Scale Biology

The Plastai can eventually develop organisms at extreme scales.

These organisms are not casually produced super-units.

Titan-scale development requires extraordinary concentrations of resources, suitable developmental environments, extensive preparation, and long periods of growth.

The aquifer system is central to this process.

Large subterranean aquifer environments can support the concentration of enormous quantities of nutrients and the nurturing of organisms whose resource requirements exceed those of ordinary surface broods.

Such development may require:

- sustained nutrient diversion,
- large biological support systems,
- long maturation periods,
- suitable underground spaces,
- specialized developmental organisms,
- environmental regulation,
- and extensive protection.

Emergence is itself a major operation.

Hundreds or thousands of surface Plastai may assist with excavation, stabilization, clearing material, defending the site, or otherwise enabling a Titan-scale organism to break through to the surface.

A project of this magnitude should generate substantial signals.

Possible observable consequences include ecological depletion, unusual nutrient movement, changes in brood activity, hydrological anomalies, excavation, seismic activity, surface concentration of Plastai, and other physical disturbances.

A correctly interpreted Titan-scale project therefore provides opportunities for enemy intervention.

Titan-scale organisms remain possible expressions of advanced Plastai biological capability, but the faction is not defined by producing one giant organism, and Titan production is not automatically the sole Plastai victory condition.

## 15. Information and Signals

Plastai activity obeys the wider Project Signal information model.

Their actions create physical consequences that may be observed without automatically revealing their purpose.

Potential Plastai signals include:

- visible surface activity,
- brood expansion,
- ecological disturbance,
- unusual wildlife movement,
- disappearance of local fauna,
- biomass harvesting,
- aquifer changes,
- altered groundwater behavior,
- excavation,
- seismic activity,
- nutrient rerouting,
- changing activity at known brood sites,
- cocoon development,
- large-scale migration,
- and terrain alteration.

Signals reveal evidence, not necessarily intent.

A large nutrient transfer toward a region may represent preparation for an attack, brood expansion, ecological engineering, replacement of losses, preparation to harvest dangerous fauna, storage, or another biological project.

The Plastai may not even know that a nearby Vanguard installation exists.

Similarly, declining activity at a brood site does not inherently mean retreat. Resources may be exhausted, deliberately rerouted, placed into dormancy, moved into subterranean storage, or committed elsewhere.

Improved intelligence should reveal better facts rather than automatically explaining those facts.

A strong information system might establish where nutrients are flowing, how much brood development is occurring, or what phenotype characteristics have been observed. Determining why remains a strategic interpretation unless direct evidence resolves the question.

Every important signal should have plausible non-combat interpretations where physically appropriate.

## 16. Deception Doctrine

Plastai deception emerges naturally from their biological and logistical systems.

The faction does not require magical buttons that generate fake information.

A major brood may remain highly active in Valley A while the Plastai quietly invest enormous resources into creating an artificial aquifer interface in Valley B.

Vanguard may correctly identify Valley A as a major Plastai position and build its defensive planning around that fact.

The Plastai can then begin shifting strategic investment.

Structures capable of biological reclamation may be reabsorbed. Stored nutrients can be routed underground. Suitable biological material can be reclaimed. Valley A can decline while resources accumulate elsewhere.

Valley B can then become the new center of developmental activity.

This is not instantaneous relocation. Existing mature organisms do not automatically teleport between sites. Redeployment works primarily by shifting resources and future production potential.

Other deceptive patterns can include:

- maintaining visible activity at a strategically declining brood,
- allowing an apparently abandoned site to retain dormant organisms,
- preparing multiple possible emergence sites,
- routing nutrients without immediately expressing their purpose,
- delaying emergence after development,
- allowing ordinary ecological activity to conceal military preparation,
- and conducting real activity whose purpose the enemy incorrectly interprets.

A deceptive signal does not need to be false.

Often the most effective deception occurs because the enemy observes something true and assigns the wrong meaning to it.

## 17. Combat and Power Projection

Plastai strategic power emphasizes preparation, adaptation, and geographic uncertainty.

Vanguard can project destructive force rapidly through airpower, artillery, and other ranged industrial systems. Correctly located and exposed Plastai concentrations can suffer catastrophic losses.

The Plastai should not be protected from this simply through superior raw combat statistics.

Instead, their strategic defense lies partly in controlling when and where valuable biological capability becomes exposed.

The Plastai can:

- develop forces away from their eventual point of use,
- route nutrients through concealed networks,
- prepare dormant organisms,
- establish multiple potential brood sites,
- exploit terrain and ecology,
- shift developmental investment geographically,
- and adapt future phenotypes to observed problems.

This permits sudden concentrations of force without granting instantaneous reinforcement.

A prepared Plastai position can respond rapidly because capability was placed there in advance.

An unprepared position cannot simply summon an army because it is under attack.

This creates the fundamental contrast:

> **Vanguard responsiveness is based heavily on projection and transportation.**

> **Plastai responsiveness is based heavily on anticipation and preparation.**

The Plastai can contest Vanguard airpower through camouflage, biological air defense, aerial organisms, attacks against air infrastructure, environmental exploitation, and other appropriate adaptations. They should not simply become a biological copy of the Vanguard air force.

Likewise, the aquifer network remains a distinctive Plastai plane of strategic strength without making them dominant everywhere on the surface.

## 18. Strategic Strengths

The Plastai's major strategic advantages are:

- extreme phenotype specialization,
- genetic acquisition of new biological mechanisms,
- ability to exploit planetary ecology for both resources and capability,
- concealed subterranean nutrient movement,
- separation between resource-harvesting locations and developmental locations,
- ability to establish new aquifer emergence points at significant cost,
- distributed brood infrastructure,
- dormant and delayed-emergence capability,
- ability to prepare latent force before it is needed,
- geographic ambiguity,
- strategic deception arising naturally from logistics,
- adaptation to enemy behavior and environmental conditions,
- and the ability to move biological potential without necessarily moving finished armies across the surface.

Their strongest play emerges from combining several of these systems rather than relying on a single dominant combat unit.

## 19. Strategic Constraints

The Plastai's advantages carry real constraints.

- They require time to develop mature organisms.
- They depend upon nutrient acquisition and processing.
- Their aquifer mobility depends upon actual hydrological connectivity and biological infrastructure.
- New artificial access points are expensive.
- Large biological projects concentrate resources and create observable consequences.
- Prepared forces represent resources committed before their eventual usefulness is known.
- Dormant forces tie up developed capability.
- Specialized phenotypes may perform poorly outside the conditions for which they were developed.
- Surface forces remain vulnerable to Vanguard ranged destruction when discovered and exposed.
- Ecological overexploitation can damage the resource base upon which the Plastai depend.
- Acquiring a useful genetic mechanism does not remove developmental, metabolic, structural, or resource constraints.
- Aquifer disruption can interfere with future development, replacement, healing, expansion, or major projects even when already mature surface forces remain capable of operating.
- The Plastai therefore cannot ignore logistics simply because their logistics are biological.

## 20. Progression Philosophy

Plastai progression is not a conventional linear technology ladder.

Progression represents increasing biological possibility and increasing ability to express that possibility strategically.

Important progression dimensions include:

- acquisition of new biological mechanisms,
- expansion of phenotype options,
- improved developmental capability,
- increased brood capacity,
- improved nutrient processing and storage,
- increased aquifer reach and throughput,
- access to more difficult environments,
- increased ecological exploitation capability,
- increasingly sophisticated biological specialization,
- and eventually the ability to sustain strategic-scale organisms and biological projects.

Genetic mechanisms can differ in sophistication and acquisition difficulty without becoming a simple sequence of numerical upgrades.

A mechanism acquired early may remain useful throughout the game.

Powerful biological combinations should be constrained by physical costs such as development time, nutrient requirements, metabolic burden, structural requirements, compatibility, maturation complexity, and supporting infrastructure rather than only by arbitrary unit tiers.

Progression should expand the player's design space, not merely increase statistics.

## 21. Victory and Endgame Direction

The exact Plastai victory condition remains open design space.

The established direction is that the faction's endgame should represent a culmination of its biological, ecological, developmental, and aquifer systems rather than simply requiring construction of a larger conventional army.

Potential directions already under consideration include:

- extensive subterranean nursery networks,
- increasingly broad emergence capability,
- massive biological projects,
- aquatic super-organisms,
- large-scale hydrological or terrain alteration,
- ecological transformation,
- and Titan-scale organisms.

A mature subterranean brood network may eventually allow the Plastai to establish developmental presence across large portions of the map.

Large ecological projects may physically alter strategically important environments, including water systems and terrain.

Titan-scale organisms may represent extraordinary military or strategic projects.

None of these concepts is yet established as the sole victory condition.

In particular:

> **Producing a Titan does not automatically equal Plastai victory.**

The final victory design should express what is unique about the Plastai rather than reducing their entire faction arc to producing one extremely large organism.

## 22. What the Plastai Must Never Become

The Plastai must never become:

- a hive mind,
- a swarm of unintelligent drones,
- a generic "bug faction,"
- Zerg under another name,
- a conventional RTS faction with biological graphics,
- organic tanks, helicopters, factories, and artillery with superficial visual substitutions,
- an instant-spawn swarm faction,
- a faction with unrestricted underground teleportation,
- a faction with a magical global biomass wallet,
- a faction that ignores logistics,
- a simple biomass-equals-money economy,
- a creature-collection system where assimilating an animal unlocks a copy of that animal,
- a linear genetic upgrade ladder,
- a faction whose adaptation automatically produces perfect counters,
- a faction whose intelligence comes from magical omniscience,
- a faction whose ambushes exist without prior physical preparation,
- a faction immune to reconnaissance because its important systems are underground,
- a faction whose mature forces can be freely converted into different mature forms without developmental consequence,
- a faction whose strongest strategy is always producing the largest possible organism,
- or a faction dependent upon one mandatory Titan endgame.

The Plastai should remain physically grounded despite the extraordinary capabilities of their biology.

## 23. Open Design Questions

The following remain unresolved:

- What exact nutrient/resource categories should exist mechanically?
- How much of the aquifer network is explicitly simulated versus strategically abstracted?
- What exact factors determine aquifer throughput and movement time?
- How are artificial aquifer entrances created, detected, damaged, and permanently destroyed?
- Which types of biological material can physically travel through the aquifer network?
- To what extent can existing mature organisms be biologically reclaimed into reusable nutrients?
- What efficiency losses accompany large-scale reabsorption?
- Which phenotypes can enter long-term dormancy, and what does dormancy cost?
- What exact physical triggers can control delayed emergence?
- How much information can Vanguard ultimately obtain about hidden brood development?
- How does Vanguard physically disrupt or isolate portions of the aquifer network without invalidating the Plastai's core faction advantage?
- What exact developmental constraints prevent unrestricted combinations of high-value genetic mechanisms?
- What scale qualifies as megafaunal or Titan-scale for gameplay purposes?
- What strategic roles should Titan-scale organisms perform?
- What is the final Plastai victory condition?
- How do subterranean nursery networks, ecological transformation, terrain alteration, aquatic super-organisms, and Titan-scale projects relate to that victory condition?

These questions should be resolved through future system design without weakening the established faction rules above.

## 24. Plastai Design Test

Before adding a new Plastai mechanic, unit, trait, structure, progression system, or strategic capability, ask:

- Does it feel biologically native rather than mechanically reskinned?
- Does it preserve individual Plastai intelligence?
- Does it respect NYMPH → COCOON → MATURE?
- Does mature capability require appropriate preparation time?
- Does it interact meaningfully with nutrients, development, or ecology?
- If it uses the aquifer, does physical geography and connectivity still matter?
- Can the simulation explain where the required biomass and nutrients came from?
- Does it create physical consequences and potentially observable signals?
- Can those signals have plausible interpretations other than immediate hostile intent?
- Does it create opportunities for player interpretation or deception?
- Does powerful preparation have meaningful opportunity cost?
- Can an opponent who correctly discovers and interprets the preparation respond?
- Does it expand biological possibility rather than merely increase statistics?
- Does it avoid reproducing a Vanguard machine in biological form?
- Does it reinforce the Plastai relationship with the planetary biosphere?
- Does it preserve the importance of the contested surface world?
- If a Plastai force appears suddenly, can the simulation explain when, where, and how that force was actually developed?

If the answer to several of these questions is no, the mechanic probably does not belong in the Plastai faction.
