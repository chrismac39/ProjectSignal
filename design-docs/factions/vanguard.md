# Vanguard Faction Design

## 1. Faction Identity

The Vanguard is a human expeditionary extraction and defense force operating across a continent-scale alien environment.

It is defined by five core characteristics:

- human personnel are extremely limited and strategically valuable,
- limited humans multiply their reach through networks, automation, remote systems, and infrastructure,
- the faction operates an industrial economy dependent on physical logistics and one-way orbital deliveries,
- it can concentrate extraordinary ranged and aerial destruction against correctly identified targets,
- it possesses advanced sensing and engineering capabilities without possessing comprehensive understanding of the alien biosphere.

The Vanguard is not a conventional colonization army and is not the primary scientific expedition. Its mission is to establish, expand, protect, and exploit an extraction network.

Its civilization solves problems externally through machines, transportation systems, communications, manufactured infrastructure, and energy-intensive industry.

This fundamentally contrasts with the Plastai, whose civilization expresses itself biologically through bodies, phenotype specialization, brood systems, nutrient circulation, ecological interaction, and the alien world's existing physical systems.

The Vanguard's strategic strength is its ability to create overwhelming capability in selected places.

Its strategic weakness is that it cannot create that capability everywhere at once.

Its defining strategic question is therefore:

> **Where should limited personnel, infrastructure, information, logistics, and destructive power be committed across an enormous theater?**

## 2. Hard Rules

The following rules are faction-level constraints and should not be violated by future mechanics without deliberately revising the faction doctrine.

- Human personnel are strategically scarce.
- Vanguard power comes from multiplying limited human capability through machines, automation, communications, sensors, and infrastructure.
- Individual human losses can have strategic consequences.
- Disposable or replaceable machinery must not be treated as equivalent to specialized personnel.
- The Vanguard depends heavily on physical infrastructure.
- Logistics must materially affect what the Vanguard can do.
- Distance must materially affect what the Vanguard can do.
- Major industrial or military activity must have a geographic footprint.
- Orbital support is powerful but finite.
- Advanced equipment cannot all be manufactured locally.
- Orbital delivery does not bypass surface logistics.
- Roads, railways, bridges, airfields, depots, communications relays, sensor sites, extraction sites, receiving sites, and industrial facilities shape strategic geography.
- Infrastructure must provide real capability rather than functioning as decorative map objects.
- Aircraft and long-range weapons are powerful.
- Vanguard weapons are not balanced primarily by making them weak.
- Aircraft, artillery, and other long-range systems cannot provide effective coverage everywhere at once.
- Range, basing, fuel, ammunition, maintenance, personnel, communications, weather, transportation, sortie cycles, and travel time constrain force projection.
- The Vanguard cannot instantly reposition its major capabilities.
- Major commitment to one region creates opportunity elsewhere.
- Sensors provide observations and evidence, not automatic understanding.
- Vanguard technology does not grant biological omniscience.
- The Vanguard remains fundamentally ignorant of significant parts of the alien ecosystem throughout a session.
- The deployed force is primarily an extraction and defense organization, not a comprehensive xenobiological research expedition.
- Improved knowledge can reduce uncertainty but must not remove the faction's epistemic limitations entirely.
- Automation exists in degrees rather than as a binary distinction between manual control and fully independent artificial intelligence.
- Loss of communications should cause degradation, altered behavior, or reduced capability rather than making every machine immediately useless.
- Vanguard economics operate at strategic scale.
- The faction must not become a detailed crafting or factory-optimization game.
- Industry exists to sustain extraction, reach, logistics, infrastructure, and strategic projects.
- Expansion means extending useful networks and operational reach rather than merely increasing base radius.
- Major Vanguard actions should create observable signals for the opponent.
- Vanguard strength should create commitments that the opponent can observe, interpret, evade, exploit, or deceive.
- The Vanguard must remain dependent on the shared physical world rather than functioning as an abstract high-technology layer above it.
- Strategic progression should expand operational possibilities rather than merely replacing existing units with numerically superior versions.

## 3. Player Fantasy

Playing Vanguard should feel like commanding a very small human presence through a much larger industrial and technological system.

The player does not primarily expand by accumulating bodies.

The player expands by creating reach.

A road makes previously impractical territory accessible.

A railway makes distant industry supportable.

A relay extends command and remote-operation capability.

A sensor site makes a region more legible.

An airfield changes the effective radius of aerial operations.

A depot allows sustained activity farther from established infrastructure.

A bridge turns a geographic obstacle into a logistics corridor.

The player gradually imposes a human industrial network across an environment that remains only partially understood.

The satisfying moments come from:

- turning a remote location into a functioning operational hub,
- establishing long-distance corridors,
- extending industrial and communications networks,
- deciding where rare humans are worth exposing,
- moving heavy capability into position,
- interpreting incomplete signals correctly,
- identifying a real threat among environmental noise,
- concentrating overwhelming force at the correct place,
- realizing that a commitment elsewhere has created vulnerability,
- defending an extraction network that has become too geographically large to protect uniformly.

Vanguard decisions should feel consequential because major commitments cannot be reversed instantly.

## 4. Scarce Human Personnel

Human scarcity is a fundamental Vanguard mechanic rather than background lore.

The Vanguard expedition operates with a small human population relative to the geographic scale, industrial footprint, and number of machines under its control.

Humans therefore occupy roles where judgment, expertise, supervision, maintenance, command, authorization, or specialized physical intervention remains necessary.

Likely categories include:

- operators,
- technicians,
- engineers,
- commanders,
- specialized maintenance personnel,
- industrial specialists,
- flight personnel where applicable.

These categories do not yet define a final personnel roster.

The important distinction is between machines that can be replaced through logistics and industry and humans whose loss removes scarce expertise and operational capacity.

Losing unmanned utility vehicles may impose an industrial cost.

Losing the small technical team responsible for maintaining a remote airfield, reactor, relay, or industrial system may alter what an entire region can support.

This scarcity shapes deployment.

The player should routinely ask:

- Does this site justify permanent human presence?
- Can this work be automated?
- Can it be remotely supervised?
- Is the location safe enough for specialists?
- Can personnel be evacuated?
- What capability disappears if these people are killed?
- Is exposing personnel here worth the operational benefit?

Automation exists primarily because the Vanguard does not possess enough humans to operate its entire industrial and military network directly.

Personnel scarcity therefore creates the need for the faction's other defining systems.

## 5. Networks as Force Multipliers

The Vanguard compensates for low manpower by multiplying human capability through interconnected technological systems.

These include:

- remote control,
- programmed behavior,
- supervised autonomy,
- higher-autonomy systems,
- communications networks,
- sensor networks,
- unmanned vehicles,
- automated industrial systems,
- distributed command infrastructure.

Autonomy is not a binary state.

Different machines can operate with different requirements for:

- continuous control,
- periodic supervision,
- mission-level orders,
- local decision-making,
- human authorization,
- network access.

A communications failure therefore does not automatically immobilize every Vanguard machine.

Instead, systems fall back according to their capabilities.

A machine might:

- continue its assigned route,
- finish a local task,
- hold position,
- return to network coverage,
- operate with reduced efficiency,
- lose access to updated targeting or sensor information,
- become unable to accept new complex orders,
- refuse actions requiring human authorization.

Network reach determines how effectively a small human population can influence a large physical area.

The Vanguard is strongest where its machines, communications, sensors, logistics, and human supervision overlap.

## 6. Industrial Civilization

The Vanguard is an industrial technological civilization.

It solves problems by constructing external systems.

Its capabilities depend on:

- machinery,
- manufactured components,
- extraction,
- refining,
- transportation,
- power,
- construction,
- maintenance,
- communications,
- logistics.

A Vanguard solution to distance is transportation infrastructure.

A solution to limited manpower is automation.

A solution to poor visibility is sensing.

A solution to hostile concentration is ranged firepower.

A solution to increased industrial demand is greater extraction, energy, transportation, and maintenance capacity.

This creates a direct thematic contrast with the Plastai.

The Vanguard extends capability through external infrastructure.

The Plastai extend capability through biological systems.

The Vanguard player should think about industrial networks at strategic scale.

The faction is not about manually arranging individual production steps, optimizing conveyor layouts, manufacturing bolts, or constructing increasingly dense factory blocks.

Industry exists to support strategic capability.

## 7. Orbital Logistics

The Vanguard remains connected to off-world civilization through one-way orbital delivery.

Orbital logistics provide equipment and resources that the expedition cannot reasonably produce locally.

These may include:

- advanced electronics,
- specialized components,
- precision equipment,
- sophisticated aerospace systems,
- difficult-to-manufacture machinery,
- critical replacements,
- other high-value equipment.

The exact division between locally manufacturable and orbital-only equipment remains unresolved.

Orbital delivery is not an instant inventory system.

A delivery requires planning and creates physical consequences.

The expedition must:

- allocate or request delivery capacity,
- prepare or maintain suitable receiving infrastructure,
- receive the shipment,
- secure the receiving area,
- unload or process the delivered material,
- move it through the surface logistics network,
- integrate it into local operations.

Orbital support therefore supplements local industry rather than replacing it.

A sophisticated aircraft component delivered from orbit is useless at a remote theater without transportation, fuel, maintenance facilities, personnel, and a functioning airfield.

Orbital logistics should feel exceptionally powerful because they connect the expedition to a much more capable civilization.

They remain constrained because delivery capacity, time, infrastructure, and downstream logistics are finite.

Receiving sites also create strategic signals and vulnerabilities.

Large deliveries reveal commitment.

## 8. Local Industry and Extraction

The Vanguard establishes local industry because long-term operations cannot depend entirely on orbital supply.

Strategic facilities can include:

- extraction sites,
- refineries,
- processing facilities,
- fuel infrastructure,
- power generation,
- depots,
- repair facilities,
- transport hubs,
- receiving infrastructure,
- communications infrastructure,
- airfields,
- other facilities required to sustain the extraction mission.

Local industry supports:

- resource extraction,
- infrastructure construction,
- transportation,
- maintenance,
- energy production,
- repair,
- fuel supply,
- strategic expansion.

The purpose is not to create a self-contained manufacturing blob producing unlimited military units.

Industrial growth expands what the Vanguard can sustain across the map.

A mature industrial region should be valuable because it supports distant operations, transportation, aviation, repair, extraction, and strategic projects.

## 9. Infrastructure Network

Vanguard infrastructure changes the effective geography of the map.

Important infrastructure includes:

- roads,
- railways,
- bridges,
- airfields,
- depots,
- communications relays,
- sensor sites,
- extraction facilities,
- receiving sites,
- refineries,
- power infrastructure,
- repair facilities,
- transport hubs.

These structures must alter what is operationally possible.

A valley connected by road is different from the same valley without one.

A remote extraction site connected to rail can support throughput that truck transport alone could not sustain.

A bridge can turn two separated logistics regions into a single corridor.

A communications relay can extend remote operation and sensor integration into previously marginal territory.

An airfield can transform an isolated region into a center of rapid force projection.

Infrastructure therefore creates strategic geography rather than merely occupying tiles.

As the network grows, Vanguard capability increases.

At the same time, the surface area requiring protection also increases.

This creates a central faction tension:

> **expansion creates capability and vulnerability simultaneously.**

## 10. Ground Mobility

Vanguard ground mobility is primarily motorized and mechanized.

Machines allow relatively rapid tactical movement, but strategic movement across a continent-scale map remains constrained by:

- distance,
- terrain,
- road quality,
- bridges,
- transport availability,
- fuel,
- maintenance,
- network coverage,
- logistics.

A vehicle capable of high speed in tactical terms can still require significant time to cross hundreds of kilometers.

Strategic reach must therefore remain distinct from raw vehicle speed.

Infrastructure increases effective mobility.

Roads improve routine movement and logistical reliability.

Railways allow much greater long-distance throughput.

Difficult terrain remains meaningful because not every region is equally compatible with heavy industrial movement.

The Vanguard should be capable of extending itself into difficult areas, but doing so requires effort and infrastructure rather than ignoring geography through technological superiority.

## 11. Rail and Heavy Logistics

Rail represents major strategic commitment.

It is not merely a faster road.

Rail provides high-capacity long-distance movement for:

- bulk material,
- fuel,
- industrial output,
- heavy equipment,
- large logistical flows,
- major military systems where appropriate.

A railway can make otherwise impractical remote industry strategically useful.

It can connect extraction regions to processing centers, depots, receiving areas, and major infrastructure projects.

Rail also creates rigidity.

Its routes are visible.

Its bridges matter.

Its junctions matter.

Its maintenance matters.

Damage at critical points can affect a much larger network.

Railways therefore represent both Vanguard strength and vulnerability.

Constructing rail into a region signals that the Vanguard considers that region important enough to support sustained heavy activity.

That commitment itself becomes information for the Plastai player.

## 12. Aviation

Aviation is one of the Vanguard's defining strategic advantages.

Aircraft allow the faction to move personnel, sensors, supplies, or destructive force across terrain far more rapidly than surface movement allows.

Most importantly, Vanguard airpower can directly influence ground events at ranges and speeds that the Plastai aquifer system cannot directly mirror.

Airpower must therefore remain powerful.

Its balancing mechanisms are primarily operational and geographic rather than arbitrary combat weakness.

Aircraft depend upon:

- basing,
- airfields,
- fuel,
- maintenance,
- ammunition or payloads,
- trained or specialized personnel where required,
- weather,
- communications,
- targeting information,
- travel time,
- sortie generation,
- turnaround,
- relocation.

An aircraft may possess enough theoretical range to cross a large portion of the map while still being unable to provide immediate continuous coverage across that entire area.

Long missions consume time and operational capacity.

An aircraft striking a target hundreds of kilometers away is not simultaneously available to respond somewhere else.

Distance can therefore reduce effective sortie generation even when it does not place a target outside maximum range.

Airfields become major strategic infrastructure because they determine where aircraft can efficiently operate.

Forward basing increases local responsiveness but requires additional logistics and creates additional sites that must be protected.

Relocating aviation should be possible but should represent an operational decision rather than instantaneous redistribution.

Weather also matters.

Severe conditions can ground, reduce, delay, or complicate aerial operations.

This creates temporary changes in the map's balance of power without permanently weakening aviation.

The primary answer to Vanguard airpower is not universal Plastai anti-air capability.

The stronger balancing mechanisms are:

- immense theater size,
- competing priorities,
- uncertain targeting information,
- basing,
- distance,
- logistics,
- weather,
- operational tempo,
- deception,
- dispersal,
- timing.

## 13. Long-Range Firepower

The Vanguard can deliver extreme destructive force at range.

Possible systems include:

- artillery,
- missiles,
- unmanned strike systems,
- aircraft-delivered weapons,
- precision strike systems,
- other long-range industrial weapons.

The exact weapon roster remains unresolved.

The doctrinal principle is established:

> **If the Vanguard correctly identifies a strategically important target and successfully concentrates the required systems against it, the resulting attack should be extremely dangerous.**

The challenge is not that Vanguard weapons lack destructive power.

The challenge is:

- finding the target,
- interpreting it correctly,
- determining that it matters,
- positioning suitable assets,
- supplying those assets,
- maintaining them,
- completing the targeting chain,
- doing so before circumstances change.

Firepower therefore depends on both industrial preparation and information.

## 14. Concentration Versus Coverage

Concentration versus coverage is one of the defining Vanguard strategic tensions.

The Vanguard can create overwhelming local superiority.

It cannot create overwhelming superiority everywhere.

Its effective power in a region depends on combinations of:

- distance,
- basing,
- infrastructure,
- ammunition,
- fuel,
- maintenance,
- personnel,
- transport,
- communications,
- weather,
- information,
- competing threats.

A heavily defended industrial basin with nearby airfields, artillery, sensors, logistics, and communications may be extraordinarily difficult to attack directly.

That does not imply that the entire map receives the same protection.

The Plastai response can be to operate elsewhere.

If Vanguard aviation is concentrated in one theater, another receives less immediate support.

If heavy artillery protects one industrial complex, distant corridors remain outside that concentration.

If aircraft are committed to investigating or attacking activity hundreds of kilometers away, they are unavailable elsewhere during that operation.

The Plastai therefore counter Vanguard strength partly by manipulating where Vanguard attention and capability are committed.

This is intentional.

The Vanguard should experience moments where correct concentration annihilates an exposed Plastai force.

The Plastai should experience moments where that very concentration creates an opportunity somewhere else.

## 15. Communications

Communications are operational infrastructure.

The Vanguard depends on communications to connect limited humans to distributed machines, sensors, and industrial systems.

Communications infrastructure can include:

- repeaters,
- relays,
- fixed network sites,
- mobile network assets,
- other systems that extend command and data reach.

Coverage can be affected by:

- distance,
- terrain,
- infrastructure placement,
- damage,
- environmental conditions where appropriate.

Network quality can influence:

- remote operation,
- command reach,
- sensor integration,
- targeting,
- telemetry,
- mission updates,
- coordination,
- human supervision.

Loss of communication does not automatically disable every Vanguard unit.

Machines retain different levels of autonomous fallback behavior.

Degradation should depend on what the machine is being asked to do.

A simple logistics vehicle may continue a known route.

A remote platform asked to interpret a changing tactical situation may become substantially less effective.

A weapon requiring external authorization or targeting data may become unable to perform its intended role.

Communications therefore multiply human capability without completely replacing local autonomy.

## 16. Sensors and Reconnaissance

The Vanguard possesses advanced technical sensors.

Reconnaissance can include:

- aerial observation,
- unmanned reconnaissance,
- fixed sensors,
- patrols,
- remote platforms,
- environmental monitoring,
- other technical collection systems.

The faction should often be very good at detecting that something happened.

It should be much less certain about what that observation means.

A sensor may accurately determine:

- movement,
- heat,
- mass,
- atmospheric disturbance,
- biological concentration,
- excavation,
- unusual environmental activity,
- repeated travel patterns.

It does not automatically determine:

- species role,
- intelligence,
- developmental stage,
- intent,
- military significance,
- relationship to other organisms,
- relationship to the Plastai aquifer network,
- whether an anomaly is deliberate.

Vanguard reconnaissance therefore produces evidence rather than perfect truth.

## 17. Xeno Ignorance

Xeno ignorance is a permanent Vanguard faction constraint.

The Vanguard is technologically advanced.

It is not biologically omniscient.

The forces represented in the game are primarily extraction and defense forces.

They are not the primary scientific cataloging expedition and are not equipped to comprehensively characterize an entire alien biosphere.

Their personnel, software, procedures, and sensors are primarily optimized to answer operational questions:

- Where are useful resources?
- Can heavy equipment operate here?
- Is infrastructure at risk?
- Is local wildlife dangerous?
- Is a route usable?
- What is interfering with extraction?
- Does an observed event require a security response?

They are not optimized to completely reconstruct the ecology, cognition, lifecycle, social structure, or biological capabilities of every alien organism encountered across a continent.

This creates a critical distinction:

> **The Vanguard can observe accurately while interpreting incorrectly.**

A sensor may detect fifty organisms moving through a valley with extraordinary precision while failing to determine whether they represent:

- normal migration,
- resource gathering,
- territorial behavior,
- coordinated intelligent activity,
- logistics,
- preparation for attack.

Different Plastai phenotypes may initially appear to be unrelated species.

Brood activity may resemble reproduction or nesting without immediately revealing strategic purpose.

Nutrient movement may resemble unusual ecological processes.

Aquifer activity may initially appear geological or biological rather than infrastructural.

Cocoon or developmental stages may be misunderstood.

Megafauna behavior may be difficult to separate from Plastai influence.

This ignorance provides a fundamental explanation for why Vanguard technological superiority does not automatically identify and destroy the Plastai.

The problem is not necessarily detecting organisms.

The problem is determining:

> **What am I actually looking at?**

Knowledge improves over time through:

- repeated observation,
- contact history,
- recovered evidence,
- operational experience,
- limited specialist analysis,
- comparison of previous signals and outcomes.

Improved knowledge should allow increasingly useful pattern recognition.

It should not become complete planetary understanding.

The Vanguard never becomes fully equipped to catalog the environment during the timescale of a session.

## 18. Information and Signals

The Vanguard interacts with the Project Signal information model through an enormous stream of imperfect technical data.

Sources can include:

- remote sensing,
- infrastructure telemetry,
- reconnaissance,
- patrols,
- environmental monitoring,
- communications systems,
- contact history.

The faction can still suffer from:

- missing data,
- stale data,
- conflicting evidence,
- environmental noise,
- incomplete coverage,
- classifier uncertainty,
- interpretation errors,
- network disruption.

This creates a defining information asymmetry:

> **The Vanguard can possess excellent instrumentation while still possessing poor certainty.**

The player should rarely feel technologically blind in the conventional sense.

Instead, the player should experience the difficulty of converting observations into correct understanding.

## 19. Plausible Non-Combat Interpretation

Major Vanguard signals should often support multiple plausible interpretations.

The opponent should not automatically know the purpose of every observed action.

Examples include:

- construction that supports extraction rather than attack,
- increased aircraft activity caused by logistics rather than strike preparation,
- convoy movement caused by maintenance or resource needs,
- force movement intended for protection rather than offense,
- sensor activation intended for exploration rather than targeting,
- unusual infrastructure activity related to a new industrial project rather than combat.

The same principle applies in reverse.

Plastai activity should frequently resemble normal ecological activity to Vanguard observers.

A movement event may be:

- migration,
- feeding,
- relocation,
- territorial behavior,
- deliberate scouting,
- logistics,
- attack preparation.

The shared environment must generate enough legitimate non-combat activity that military meaning cannot simply be inferred from motion itself.

## 20. Deception Doctrine

Vanguard deception should emerge primarily from real systems rather than magical fake-signal abilities.

Possible methods include:

- unusual convoy routing,
- concealed force relocation,
- ambiguous construction,
- emphasizing activity in one region,
- decoy activity,
- manipulated communications patterns,
- deliberately exposed infrastructure,
- staging aircraft away from eventual targets,
- exploiting enemy assumptions about industrial intent.

The strongest deception occurs when an observed action is real but its purpose is misunderstood.

A construction project can genuinely exist while serving a different strategic goal than the opponent assumes.

A convoy can genuinely move while carrying less important cargo than expected.

A visible concentration can genuinely threaten one region while preparing to shift elsewhere.

Deception should therefore exploit the same signal interpretation systems used by reconnaissance.

## 21. Combat Philosophy

Vanguard combat emphasizes preparation, information, machine-heavy forces, and ranged effects.

The faction prefers to expose machinery rather than scarce humans whenever practical.

Its advantages include:

- remote engagement,
- unmanned systems,
- powerful long-range weapons,
- aviation,
- industrial logistics,
- sensor-assisted targeting,
- rapid movement along developed networks.

Its vulnerabilities include:

- exposed logistics,
- rare personnel,
- infrastructure dependence,
- imperfect information,
- geographic overextension.

Combat should frequently be the consequence of earlier planning.

The important choices happen before weapons fire:

- where assets were based,
- where fuel was stockpiled,
- which corridor was maintained,
- what sensors were deployed,
- which contact was considered important,
- where aircraft were committed,
- what infrastructure was protected,
- what information the player believed.

The Vanguard should not primarily play as a twitch-heavy conventional RTS faction.

## 22. Defensive Doctrine

Vanguard defense across a massive map is necessarily selective.

The player cannot defend every location equally.

Defense can involve:

- protecting rare personnel,
- protecting critical infrastructure,
- perimeter security,
- defending logistics corridors,
- layered sensing,
- mobile response forces,
- protecting airfields,
- protecting extraction sites,
- protecting receiving areas,
- protecting strategic industrial nodes.

Different locations justify different levels of investment.

A major refinery may warrant extensive protection.

A remote relay may be important but inexpensive enough that replacement is preferable to permanent heavy defense.

A long rail corridor may be impossible to secure continuously and instead depend on sensors, patrols, rapid repair, and mobile response.

The player must determine what:

- must survive,
- can be repaired,
- can be abandoned,
- can be rebuilt,
- can be defended indirectly through response capability.

The expansion of the network therefore creates a growing defensive problem.

## 23. Strategic Strengths

The Vanguard's established strategic strengths include:

- exceptional ranged destructive capability,
- powerful aviation,
- rapid movement along developed transport networks,
- precise industrial capability,
- advanced sensing,
- automation,
- strong communications-enabled force multiplication,
- large logistics throughput where infrastructure is mature,
- selective concentration of overwhelming force,
- ability to transform geography through infrastructure,
- powerful machinery supporting a very small human population.

These strengths are most effective where information, logistics, communications, and infrastructure overlap.

## 24. Strategic Constraints

The Vanguard's established limitations include:

- extremely limited human personnel,
- difficulty replacing specialized personnel,
- dependence on infrastructure,
- dependence on logistics,
- dependence on networks for maximum efficiency,
- orbital dependency for important equipment,
- inability to manufacture every advanced system locally,
- geographic overextension,
- maintenance burden,
- limited simultaneous theater coverage,
- delayed response across extreme distances,
- ecological ignorance,
- incomplete interpretation of alien activity,
- vulnerability to deception,
- vulnerability created by visible infrastructure,
- expensive strategic commitments.

These constraints are not incidental weaknesses.

They define how Vanguard strength is expressed.

## 25. Progression Philosophy

Vanguard progression increases operational possibilities rather than merely increasing statistics.

Potential progression axes include:

- extending communications reach,
- improving transportation infrastructure,
- increasing machine autonomy,
- improving logistics throughput,
- expanding local industrial capability,
- improving orbital support,
- improving extraction,
- expanding aviation operations,
- improving sensors,
- deploying greater long-range firepower,
- constructing new categories of strategic infrastructure.

Progression should change what the player can attempt.

Examples include:

- supporting operations farther from the original foothold,
- establishing additional air coverage,
- sustaining remote industry,
- operating machines with less direct supervision,
- transporting heavier systems,
- reacting across larger distances,
- maintaining multiple operational hubs,
- supporting larger strategic projects.

Old systems should remain relevant where their function remains useful.

Progression should not devolve into replacing Tier 1 equipment with visually similar Tier 5 equipment possessing larger numbers.

## 26. Expansion Philosophy

Vanguard expansion is network expansion rather than conventional RTS base expansion.

Expansion means:

- opening resource regions,
- establishing useful corridors,
- extending communications,
- building transport links,
- creating new logistics hubs,
- constructing new airfields,
- creating new sensor coverage,
- extending power and industrial support,
- placing facilities where they alter operational reach.

The player should think geographically.

Questions should include:

- What does this region provide?
- How do I connect it?
- How expensive is that connection?
- Can I defend the corridor?
- What new capability does this infrastructure create?
- What commitment does this reveal?
- How far does this extend my useful reach?

There should be no assumption that contiguous territory is required.

A functioning corridor between distant important locations can be more valuable than controlling every kilometer between them.

## 27. Strategic Projects and Endgame Direction

The Vanguard's strategic objective is not merely exterminating the opposing faction.

The established direction is the creation and protection of a large extraction system culminating in infrastructure equivalent in strategic significance to a space elevator or other orbital-scale extraction project.

This requires:

- major extraction capability,
- industrial support,
- transportation networks,
- protected strategic sites,
- long-distance logistics,
- continued access to key regions.

The endgame should represent a major transformation of the Vanguard's ability to extract material from the theater rather than simply the construction of a final combat unit.

The exact structure, requirements, stages, and mechanics of the final strategic project remain unresolved.

The established principle is that success depends on building and sustaining the network required to support the project.

## 28. What the Vanguard Must Never Become

The Vanguard must not become:

- a conventional RTS human faction with effectively infinite manpower,
- a Space Marine faction centered on individually dominant human soldiers,
- an omniscient high-technology faction,
- a faction capable of instantly projecting firepower anywhere,
- a faction that can ignore physical logistics,
- a faction that casually replaces specialized humans,
- a faction that manufactures every advanced system locally from generic resources,
- a Factorio-style factory optimization faction,
- a giant base blob,
- a faction where aircraft solve every strategic problem,
- a faction where artillery is globally available simply because it exists somewhere on the map,
- a faction whose sensors automatically identify alien biology,
- a faction that eventually researches away all xeno uncertainty,
- a faction where autonomy removes the need for communications, infrastructure, or human supervision,
- a faction whose logistics network exists only as visual flavor,
- a faction balanced primarily by making advanced weapons artificially weak,
- a faction that controls territory merely because a structure was placed inside it,
- a faction whose industrial progression becomes unlimited unit spam,
- a faction that can protect every important site equally,
- a faction whose technology allows it to ignore terrain, distance, weather, or the shared environment.

## 29. Open Design Questions

The following Vanguard issues remain genuinely unresolved:

- What is the exact scale of the human population represented in a session?
- How explicitly are individual humans modeled?
- How are different personnel specialties represented mechanically?
- Which aircraft categories ultimately exist?
- Which aerial systems require human flight personnel and which are unmanned?
- What exact degrees of machine autonomy exist?
- How does autonomy differ between logistics, industrial, reconnaissance, and combat systems?
- What exact capabilities are lost or degraded when communications fail?
- What equipment can local industry manufacture?
- What equipment remains dependent on orbital delivery?
- How much local production capacity exists at different campaign stages?
- How is orbital delivery capacity allocated and scheduled?
- How are sortie generation and aircraft operational tempo represented?
- How detailed are fuel and ammunition logistics?
- What exact long-range weapon systems exist?
- How are rail construction, maintenance, and disruption modeled?
- How are major infrastructure repair operations represented?
- How much explicit xenobiological knowledge progression exists?
- How is uncertain classification presented to the player?
- What exact strategic progression structure replaces or supplements a conventional technology tree?
- What are the exact stages and requirements of the Vanguard endgame extraction project?

These questions should be resolved within the constraints established by the rest of this document.

## 30. Vanguard Design Test

Before adding a Vanguard mechanic, unit, technology, structure, or progression system, ask:

- Does this reinforce scarce human personnel?
- Does it multiply human capability through networks or machinery rather than erase the manpower constraint?
- Does infrastructure matter?
- Does distance matter?

- Does Vanguard strength remain strongest where infrastructure, logistics, information, and preparation align?
- Does the opponent have opportunities created by where Vanguard power is not currently concentrated?

The central Vanguard doctrine is:

> **A tiny human force uses networks, machinery, infrastructure, and industrial logistics to exert enormous power across an alien continent. That power is real, but it must be positioned, supplied, maintained, informed, and committed correctly. The Vanguard can dominate a place. It cannot dominate every place at once.**
