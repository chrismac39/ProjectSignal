# Project Signal — Vision

## 1. Purpose

Project Signal is a large-scale asymmetric strategy game about **information, logistics, ecology, distance, deception, and preparation**.

The player does not control a conventional RTS army on a compact battlefield. Instead, each faction attempts to understand and shape a vast living theater over compressed periods of time.

The central question is not simply:

> Where is the enemy?

It is:

> What is actually happening in this world, what does it mean, and what should I commit to doing about it?

The world continues to exist whether or not the player is looking at a particular region. Wildlife migrates. Weather develops. Infrastructure moves resources. Broods mature. Aircraft relocate. Fires spread. Rivers rise. Convoys travel. Signals appear and disappear.

Both factions operate inside this persistent world, but they perceive it differently and interact with it through fundamentally different systems.

Project Signal should produce stories through the collision of those systems rather than through heavily scripted events.

---

# 2. The Core Fantasy

Project Signal is about commanding a war across a world too large to fully observe, understand, or control.

The player sees fragments.

A herd changes direction.

Radio traffic increases.

Aircraft begin operating from a distant airfield.

A previously quiet valley suddenly contains unusual biological activity.

A convoy disappears.

A brood site becomes inactive.

A storm front approaches.

A rail line under construction may indicate industrial expansion, military preparation, or something entirely unrelated to the player's current assumptions.

The player forms hypotheses from these signals and makes commitments.

Sometimes those conclusions are correct.

Sometimes the opponent deliberately encouraged them.

Sometimes both players misinterpret something that was actually caused by the world itself.

The resulting strategy should feel less like solving a board state and more like interpreting an enormous evolving situation.

---

# 3. Core Design Pillars

## 3.1 Information Is Gameplay

Information is not merely a visibility mechanic.

Gathering, interpreting, validating, concealing, and manipulating information are major parts of the game.

Players should rarely possess perfect knowledge of the world.

Observations may be:

* incomplete,
* delayed,
* ambiguous,
* outdated,
* indirect,
* incorrectly interpreted,
* or deliberately deceptive.

The underlying world state exists independently from either player's understanding of it.

The player must therefore distinguish between:

* what actually exists,
* what has been observed,
* what has been inferred,
* and what is merely suspected.

Reconnaissance should produce evidence rather than omniscience.

---

## 3.2 The World Exists Independently of the Players

Project Signal takes place inside a living environment rather than on a static RTS board.

Weather, terrain, wildlife, vegetation, water, seasons, migration, fire, and other environmental systems exist independently of either faction.

The world is not decorative scenery placed around combat encounters.

It is part of the strategic system.

Players exploit it, move through it, misunderstand it, damage it, depend upon it, and sometimes lose control of consequences they helped create.

---

## 3.3 Scale Changes Strategy

The map is intentionally enormous.

The reference scale is approximately that of the state of Wyoming, with geography altered as necessary for gameplay.

At this scale:

* units cannot be everywhere,
* reconnaissance cannot cover everything,
* artillery cannot dominate the entire theater,
* aircraft must operate from real locations,
* logistics corridors matter,
* infrastructure creates strategic geography,
* travel consumes meaningful time,
* and committing forces to one region creates vulnerability elsewhere.

Distance is therefore a strategic resource.

A powerful force in the wrong place may be functionally irrelevant.

---

## 3.4 Logistics Creates Power

Military capability does not exist independently of logistics.

Vanguard power depends on transportation networks, supply infrastructure, communications, industrial facilities, orbital deliveries, and scarce human operators.

Plastai power depends on biomass acquisition, nutrient movement, brood development, aquifer connectivity, biological preparation, and ecological access.

Both factions become stronger primarily by building systems that allow them to project power across the world.

Those systems also create vulnerabilities.

---

## 3.5 Asymmetry Goes Deeper Than Unit Rosters

The two primary factions should not feel like conventional RTS factions with different versions of infantry, tanks, aircraft, and buildings.

Their economies, mobility, intelligence systems, infrastructure, reproduction, logistics, and relationships with the environment are fundamentally different.

The Vanguard builds industrial networks.

The Plastai cultivate biological networks.

The Vanguard expands through infrastructure.

The Plastai expand through ecology and nutrient circulation.

The Vanguard depends on machines extending scarce human presence.

The Plastai depend on biological specialization and distributed living systems.

Their differences should change how the player thinks.

---

## 3.6 Plans Create Delayed Consequences

Many important actions should take time to mature.

Building infrastructure takes time.

Moving forces across the theater takes time.

Developing broods takes time.

Preparing large biological organisms takes time.

Moving industrial resources takes time.

Repairing damaged networks takes time.

Establishing reconnaissance coverage takes time.

The delay between decision and consequence creates opportunities for planning, prediction, deception, and interruption.

Power should often come from decisions made substantially earlier.

---

## 3.7 Uncertainty Must Be Legible

Project Signal embraces uncertainty, but uncertainty must not feel arbitrary.

The player should generally understand why they lack information and what could improve their knowledge.

Unknown information may be caused by:

* distance,
* terrain,
* weather,
* sensor limitations,
* communications loss,
* insufficient observation,
* unfamiliar biological behavior,
* deliberate concealment,
* or stale intelligence.

Players should be able to reason about uncertainty rather than simply suffer random hidden information.

---

## 3.8 Spectacle Emerges From Simulation

Large battles, fires, mass migrations, bombardments, Titan-scale organisms, collapsing logistics networks, and sudden ambushes may become spectacular.

However, spectacle is not the foundation of the game.

The interesting part is how those moments came to exist.

A catastrophic battle should often be the final consequence of decisions that began much earlier through reconnaissance, logistics, deception, environmental conditions, and positioning.

---

# 4. The World

Project Signal takes place across a continental-scale playable environment.

The reference geography is the approximate physical scale of Wyoming.

The world is not intended to recreate Wyoming literally. Its scale provides a familiar reference for distances and geographic relationships.

Terrain may include:

* mountain ranges,
* alpine regions,
* forests,
* grasslands,
* wetlands,
* deserts,
* rivers,
* lakes,
* large drainage systems,
* deep valleys,
* coastlines or inland seas,
* and extensive subterranean aquifer networks.

The baseline climate is broadly alpine and continental.

The biosphere is significantly more productive than modern Earth, supporting exceptional biodiversity and large populations of megafauna.

The intended ecological feel is closer to an exaggerated prehistoric Earth than a sparse modern ecosystem.

Large animals should be ordinary parts of the landscape rather than rare curiosities.

---

# 5. World Variability

The same map should not produce the same strategic environment every session.

Geography provides structure, while world-state variables modify how that geography functions.

Examples include:

* wet or dry climate conditions,
* altered precipitation patterns,
* vegetation density,
* wildlife abundance,
* migration intensity,
* aquifer conditions,
* resource concentration,
* snow persistence,
* wildfire risk,
* river conditions,
* and regional ecological productivity.

Important resources and ecological opportunities should not occupy perfectly predictable coordinates every match.

Players should learn regions rather than memorize loot locations.

A valley might be an exceptional biomass source in one session and relatively poor in another.

A normally minor river may become strategically important in a wet world.

A dry world may make fire one of the dominant environmental threats.

The map should be familiar without becoming solved.

---

# 6. Weather and Environmental Events

Weather exists as part of the world simulation.

Project Signal does not attempt to reproduce a complete meteorological model.

Weather should instead emerge from understandable environmental conditions using simplified systems capable of producing believable variation.

Possible weather includes:

* rain,
* snow,
* thunderstorms,
* lightning,
* fog,
* strong winds,
* heat,
* cold snaps,
* blizzards,
* drought conditions,
* and severe storm systems.

Weather should affect gameplay through interactions with existing world state.

A weather event should not have one universal scripted effect.

For example, a strong thunderstorm may:

* ground or severely limit Vanguard aviation,
* reduce visibility,
* interfere with communications,
* provide additional surface water,
* improve certain biological conditions,
* trigger lightning fires,
* extinguish existing fires,
* alter migration,
* or damage exposed infrastructure.

The result depends on where the storm occurs and what the world was already like.

On a wet world, lightning may have little ecological consequence.

On a dry world, the same storm could ignite a valley and destroy enormous quantities of biomass the Plastai intended to exploit.

Environmental events should therefore create asymmetric opportunities and problems without existing merely as faction-specific bonuses or penalties.

---

# 7. Time and Simulation

Project Signal uses compressed strategic time.

The world may simulate hours, days, weeks, seasons, and potentially years within a single campaign.

The player should not manually experience every second of that time.

Instead, time advances quickly when little requires attention and slows or stops when meaningful events occur.

Examples of possible attention triggers include:

* unexpected contacts,
* threats near infrastructure,
* major signals,
* convoy attacks,
* brood activity,
* weather emergencies,
* aircraft encounters,
* strategic construction completion,
* and player-defined conditions.

Players should eventually be able to configure which events interrupt accelerated time.

Difficulty should primarily affect how much assistance the player receives in noticing and interpreting important events rather than giving the AI hidden economic or combat bonuses.

The simulation remains continuous regardless of whether the player is observing a specific region.

---

# 8. Signals and Knowledge

The game's name comes from its central information system.

The player rarely interacts directly with objective truth.

Instead, the world produces signals.

Signals may include:

* movement,
* heat,
* radio activity,
* atmospheric changes,
* ecological disruption,
* migration anomalies,
* construction,
* damaged vegetation,
* disappearing wildlife,
* unusual water activity,
* logistics traffic,
* aircraft operations,
* surface excavation,
* fires,
* noise,
* industrial emissions,
* or patterns that become meaningful only after repeated observation.

A signal does not automatically provide an explanation.

Repeated exposure may allow players to become increasingly confident about what particular patterns indicate.

---

# 9. Reality and Perception

Internally, Project Signal should distinguish between objective world state and faction knowledge.

Conceptually there are at least three perspectives:

* **World Reality**
* **Vanguard Knowledge**
* **Plastai Knowledge**

Neither faction automatically knows everything contained in World Reality.

Each faction constructs its own understanding from observations.

A location may therefore simultaneously be:

* objectively occupied,
* believed empty by the Vanguard,
* partially understood by the Plastai,
* and incorrectly annotated by the player.

This separation is essential.

Fog of war should not simply mean that unseen tiles turn dark.

Knowledge itself is part of the simulation.

---

# 10. Plausible Interpretation

Where practical, strategically significant signals should have multiple plausible interpretations.

A Vanguard industrial buildup might indicate:

* construction of a refinery,
* preparation for an airfield,
* defensive expansion,
* a new orbital receiving site,
* logistical rerouting,
* or preparation for offensive operations.

A large Plastai movement might indicate:

* migration toward biomass,
* relocation of a brood,
* conflict with native megafauna,
* preparation for an attack,
* aquifer expansion,
* or response to an environmental threat.

The player should not always know whether an observation is military.

This protects the core intelligence game from collapsing into:

> Signal detected = enemy attack incoming.

---

# 11. The Vanguard

The Vanguard represents a human extraction and security force operating far from normal human civilization.

They are technologically advanced but constrained by distance, manpower, and logistics.

They are not a massive colonization army.

They are not an omniscient scientific expedition.

They are an operational force attempting to build and protect an industrial presence inside an unfamiliar biosphere.

---

## 11.1 Scarce Humans

Human personnel are extremely limited.

Individual people are strategically valuable.

Most Vanguard operations therefore rely on:

* autonomous systems,
* remotely operated systems,
* drones,
* vehicles,
* industrial machinery,
* communications networks,
* sensors,
* and automated infrastructure.

Networks multiply human capability.

Destroying a machine is inconvenient.

Losing specialized personnel may be strategically significant.

---

## 11.2 Industrial Economy

The Vanguard builds physical infrastructure.

Important systems may include:

* roads,
* railways,
* bridges,
* refineries,
* airfields,
* communications sites,
* sensor networks,
* power infrastructure,
* depots,
* extraction sites,
* orbital receiving facilities,
* and industrial processing locations.

Infrastructure should shape strategic geography.

A railway is not simply an efficiency upgrade.

It changes which regions can sustain heavy operations.

---

## 11.3 Orbital Dependence

The Vanguard cannot manufacture everything locally.

Critical equipment and advanced systems arrive through one-way orbital delivery.

This creates dependency on off-world logistics while preventing orbital supply from becoming unlimited magic inventory.

Deliveries require:

* planning,
* receiving infrastructure,
* secure operating areas,
* transportation,
* and time.

Orbital support should expand what the Vanguard can accomplish without eliminating the importance of local industry.

---

## 11.4 Range and Firepower

The Vanguard possesses extraordinary ranged destructive capability.

Depending on progression, this may include:

* aircraft,
* long-range artillery,
* missiles,
* drones,
* precision strike systems,
* and other remote weapons.

This capability should feel powerful.

If the Vanguard correctly identifies and concentrates against a Plastai target, the result may be devastating.

The primary limitation is strategic coverage.

A continent-sized theater cannot be saturated everywhere simultaneously.

Aircraft positioned in one region are not instantly available hundreds of kilometers away.

Artillery protects only the areas within its operational reach.

Fuel, ammunition, maintenance, communications, weather, infrastructure, and travel all limit power projection.

Vanguard strength is therefore intense but geographically finite.

---

## 11.5 Xeno Ignorance

The Vanguard does not fully understand the ecosystem.

Its forces are primarily concerned with extraction and security rather than comprehensive biological research.

This prevents technological sophistication from becoming automatic biological omniscience.

Vanguard operators may observe organisms without understanding:

* their developmental stage,
* their ecological role,
* their relationship to the Plastai,
* whether behavior is natural or coordinated,
* or whether apparently harmless activity has strategic significance.

Knowledge can improve during a campaign, but uncertainty should remain part of Vanguard play.

---

# 12. The Plastai

The Plastai are a recently sapient native lineage of biological engineers.

They are not a hive mind.

They are not mindless insects.

They do not share a single consciousness.

Individual Plastai possess individual intelligence.

Their civilization developed through biology rather than through conventional external tools and machinery.

---

## 12.1 Biological Civilization

Where humans build machines outside themselves, the Plastai specialize living organisms.

Their civilization expresses technology through:

* developmental biology,
* phenotype control,
* genetic adaptation,
* symbiosis,
* ecological manipulation,
* nutrient management,
* specialized cast forms,
* and controlled reproduction.

This should not simply produce biological versions of human machines.

The design should continually ask:

> How would a civilization solve this problem if advanced biology was its primary technological medium?

---

## 12.2 Extreme Polymorphism

The Plastai exhibit enormous physical variation.

Different cast forms may be specialized for:

* movement,
* combat,
* excavation,
* aquatic environments,
* scouting,
* transport,
* nutrient processing,
* environmental manipulation,
* defense,
* communication,
* or other tasks.

The diversity should resemble the dramatic functional variation found in social insects, but applied to intelligent individuals rather than a hive organism.

---

## 12.3 Lifecycle

The core Plastai lifecycle is:

**NYMPH → COCOON → MATURE**

This lifecycle is a foundational constraint.

Mature forms do not instantly appear because the player clicked a production button.

They require biological development.

This creates meaningful preparation time and allows enemies to interfere with future capability before it fully exists.

Lifecycle constraints are part of Plastai logistics.

---

## 12.4 Genetic Assimilation

The Plastai can acquire useful biological mechanisms from other life.

They do not simply copy animals.

Instead they may acquire biological solutions.

Examples might include mechanisms related to:

* thermal regulation,
* armor,
* toxins,
* sensory systems,
* aquatic respiration,
* tissue regeneration,
* camouflage,
* electrical generation,
* structural materials,
* or metabolic efficiency.

Assimilation expands biological possibility without turning the Plastai into a creature-collection faction.

---

# 13. Nutrient Circulation and Aquifers

The Plastai possess a subterranean nutrient circulation system built around underground water networks.

This is one of the defining mechanics of the faction.

Surface broods gather biomass.

That biomass is converted into usable nutrients and transferred into the underground network.

Nutrients can then move through connected aquifer systems toward locations where they are needed.

The underground network therefore acts as a biological equivalent of strategic logistics infrastructure.

It is not teleportation.

Movement should remain constrained by:

* geography,
* connection,
* capacity,
* access points,
* development,
* distance,
* and biological cost.

---

## 13.1 Surface and Subsurface Interaction

Surface sites gather resources and interact directly with the environment.

Subsurface systems redistribute those resources.

Brood-generating organisms or other developmental structures may move through the network to establish new surface presence near:

* springs,
* lakes,
* wetlands,
* underground outlets,
* or other viable emergence points.

At significant cost, the Plastai may eventually create new entrances or alter existing ones.

This prevents aquifer routes from becoming permanently solved choke points.

---

## 13.2 Brood Preparation

Plastai development can occur before immediate deployment.

Mature organisms may potentially remain dormant or held in biological stasis.

This allows delayed or triggered emergence.

The Plastai player may therefore prepare military capability without immediately revealing its final purpose.

A seemingly quiet region may contain substantial latent power.

This is essential to Plastai deception.

---

# 14. Megafauna and Titan-Scale Biology

The Plastai may eventually rear organisms at extraordinary scale.

These organisms require enormous resource concentration and preparation.

They should never function like casually produced super-units.

A Titan-scale organism may require:

* sustained biomass acquisition,
* enormous nutrient flow,
* a suitable underground development site,
* extended maturation,
* ecological preparation,
* and significant surface activity during emergence.

Thousands of organisms may assist with excavation or final emergence.

Such an event should leave signals.

The strategic game becomes whether the opponent recognizes those signals, interprets them correctly, and intervenes in time.

---

# 15. Asymmetric Power Projection

Both factions can produce overwhelming force.

They do so differently.

The Vanguard concentrates mechanical and ranged power through infrastructure.

The Plastai concentrates biological power through ecological preparation and nutrient networks.

The Vanguard may rapidly destroy a discovered concentration.

The Plastai may avoid that concentration entirely and develop somewhere else.

The Vanguard may shift aircraft across a region quickly but cannot maintain equal airpower everywhere.

The Plastai may prepare dormant broods near several potential objectives but cannot instantly create those broods.

Both factions should experience moments where preparation pays off dramatically.

A Vanguard player may correctly identify a brood site and destroy months of biological investment through concentrated bombardment.

A Plastai player may intentionally draw Vanguard aviation toward one theater before triggering prepared organisms against an inadequately defended refinery hundreds of kilometers away.

Both outcomes should feel earned rather than arbitrary.

---

# 16. Ecology as Strategic Infrastructure

To the Plastai, wildlife and ecosystems are potential resource networks.

To the Vanguard, those same systems may represent hazards, obstacles, environmental indicators, or poorly understood biological activity.

Wildlife should have its own behaviors.

Animals may:

* migrate,
* reproduce,
* feed,
* respond to weather,
* flee conflict,
* concentrate near water,
* compete with one another,
* and react to environmental disruption.

Their movement may itself generate useful or misleading signals.

A herd suddenly fleeing a valley could indicate:

* a predator,
* a storm,
* wildfire,
* Vanguard activity,
* Plastai activity,
* or some interaction between them.

Ecology therefore contributes to the information game rather than merely generating resources.

---

# 17. Resources and Economic Scale

Project Signal is not Factorio.

The player should not spend the game placing individual conveyor belts or optimizing machines tile by tile.

Economic decisions occur primarily at strategic scale.

The player chooses:

* which regions matter,
* which corridors to develop,
* which infrastructure to construct,
* where to concentrate resources,
* what capability to prioritize,
* and which vulnerabilities are acceptable.

Large predefined or procedurally influenced corridors may exist for:

* roads,
* rail,
* rivers,
* aquifers,
* industrial sites,
* resource regions,
* and air routes.

Infrastructure placement should matter without becoming precision construction micromanagement.

---

# 18. Combat Philosophy

Combat is important, but Project Signal is not primarily about tactical unit control.

The most meaningful decision in a battle may have occurred long before combat began.

Examples include:

* choosing where to establish an airfield,
* failing to detect brood preparation,
* cutting a rail corridor,
* preparing dormant organisms,
* moving artillery into a region,
* misidentifying migration as enemy movement,
* ignoring worsening weather,
* or concentrating forces against a deliberate decoy.

Tactical engagements should resolve consequences created by strategic systems.

Players may still influence combat, but the game should resist becoming an APM competition.

---

# 19. Deception

Deception is a first-class strategic mechanic.

Players should be able to create situations where the opponent reaches a reasonable but incorrect conclusion.

Potential examples include:

* dormant broods,
* false concentrations,
* unusual convoy routing,
* construction that resembles another type of project,
* deliberately exposed infrastructure,
* staged movement,
* abandoned sites,
* communication patterns,
* feigned expansion,
* hidden nutrient diversion,
* and activity designed to resemble ecological behavior.

Good deception should exploit the normal information systems of the game.

It should not depend primarily on explicit "create fake signal" buttons.

---

# 20. Counterplay

Powerful mechanics should usually create opportunities for observation and interference.

A major Vanguard project requires logistics and infrastructure.

Those activities create signals.

A major Plastai project requires nutrients and biological development.

Those activities also create signals.

Counterplay should frequently involve detecting preparation before the resulting capability becomes overwhelming.

This creates a strategic loop:

**Observe → Interpret → Prepare → Commit → Reveal → Adapt**

Neither player should automatically receive confirmation that their interpretation is correct.

---

# 21. Strategic Projects and Victory

Victory should represent a major transformation of the theater.

It should not primarily come from accumulating arbitrary score or capturing a small number of abstract victory points.

The Vanguard may pursue massive extraction or orbital infrastructure necessary to accomplish its expeditionary objective.

The Plastai may pursue enormous biological or ecological projects capable of fundamentally changing their strategic position.

Possible Plastai endgame concepts include:

* continent-scale brood infrastructure,
* massive subterranean nursery systems,
* aquatic super-organisms,
* deliberate terrain transformation,
* extreme ecological manipulation,
* or Titan-scale biological projects.

Exact victory conditions may evolve during development.

The governing principle should remain:

> Victory comes from successfully building, protecting, and completing something strategically enormous while preventing the opponent from doing the same.

---

# 22. Session Variability

Campaigns should differ substantially without requiring entirely new maps.

Project Signal uses several overlapping layers of session variability. These layers exist to prevent rote map memorization, force continued interpretation of the world, and ensure that players cannot reduce a campaign to a known optimal opening.

The four primary layers are:

- Public World Conditions
- Hidden World Conditions
- Private World Votes
- Faction / Subfaction Selection

Each layer modifies the session in a different way.

## Public World Conditions

Public World Conditions are major map-wide environmental modifiers rolled randomly by the game before the session begins.

Players do not vote on these conditions.

Because they can substantially alter the overall world state, they are revealed to both players at the beginning of the match for complete clarity.

Examples include:

- unusually wet world
- unusually dry world
- severe winters
- dense megafauna
- sparse vegetation
- unusually high surface water
- heightened wildfire conditions
- etc.

These are the largest environmental modifiers in the game.

They should alter the shared world itself rather than grant direct numerical bonuses or penalties to either faction.

A wet world does not simply give the Plastai `+20%` to something. It creates more water, changes vegetation, affects wildfire risk, alters animal distribution, changes aquifer conditions, influences weather outcomes, and creates a different strategic environment for both factions.

The two factions may benefit or suffer differently from the resulting world, but those consequences should emerge from their interaction with the modified environment.

Public World Conditions establish the broad environmental identity of a session.

## Hidden World Conditions

Hidden World Conditions are less dramatic world-state variables that are not revealed at the beginning of the match.

They must instead be discovered through exploration, observation, reconnaissance, and interaction with the environment.

Examples include:

- resource density and distribution
- aquifer structure and quality
- wildlife distribution
- species present within particular regions
- migration corridors
- unusually productive ecological zones
- geological opportunities
- regional water availability
- etc.

Like Public World Conditions, these should modify the world rather than directly modify faction statistics.

They exist primarily to make prior knowledge of the map incomplete.

A player may know the geography of a valley from previous campaigns while still having no certainty about what that valley contains in the current session.

Hidden World Conditions ensure that exploration and interpretation remain necessary even on a familiar map.

## Private World Votes

Each player receives a limited hidden pre-game vote that can influence one narrower aspect of the shared world.

These votes allow players to deliberately bias the environment toward a strategic possibility without directly granting themselves a conventional faction bonus.

The selected modifier remains hidden from the opponent.

Examples include:

- Plastai guarantees the presence of several unusually docile herbivore herds
- Vanguard guarantees the presence of a dangerous montane predator population
- a particular ecological niche is guaranteed to exist somewhere on the map
- a specific environmental opportunity becomes more likely within an appropriate biome
- etc.

Private World Votes should be significantly narrower in scope than Public World Conditions.

They should not redefine the entire campaign.

Instead, they create one deliberate irregularity inside the world that the selecting player understands in advance and may attempt to exploit.

Critically, the vote should modify the shared world rather than simply grant a hidden statistical advantage.

The opponent can encounter, observe, exploit, or interfere with the resulting condition.

A player therefore gains **foreknowledge, not exclusive ownership**.

Private World Votes create asymmetry of information without creating asymmetric reality.

## Faction / Subfaction Selection

Faction or subfaction selection is separate from world-state modification.

Each faction may make a hidden pre-game strategic choice that changes which capabilities, developmental paths, units, technologies, or biological expressions can become available during that session.

Certain capabilities may only be accessible through particular subfaction selections.

These choices should meaningfully alter how a faction can operate rather than provide simple percentage bonuses.

A subfaction should therefore represent a strategic commitment such as:

- access to a particular form of Vanguard infrastructure or automation
- a specialized orbital support path
- a particular Plastai biological lineage
- access to certain phenotype expressions
- a unique method of environmental exploitation
- or another capability that changes the player's strategic options

The opponent is not told which selection was made.

However, the consequences of that selection should eventually become observable.

Players should be able to infer an opponent's subfaction through signals, infrastructure, organisms, tactics, and repeated encounters.

Subfaction identity therefore becomes another part of the information game.

## Combined Effect

These systems should work together to make every campaign strategically distinct even when played on the same underlying geography.

Public World Conditions define the broad environmental character of the session.

Hidden World Conditions make the world's contents uncertain.

Private World Votes allow each player to secretly influence one limited aspect of that world.

Subfaction Selection changes the capabilities each faction can ultimately express.

Together, they ensure that geographic familiarity provides useful context without allowing rote memorization to become a dominant strategy.

The player may know the mountains, rivers, valleys, and major corridors.

They should never know with certainty what those places mean in the current campaign.

Success should therefore continue to depend on the core Project Signal loop:

**Observe → Interpret → Prepare → Commit → Reveal → Adapt**

---

# 23. Difficulty

Difficulty should avoid traditional hidden AI advantages wherever possible.

Increasing difficulty should primarily reduce the amount of assistance given to the player.

Examples include:

* fewer automatic alerts,
* less interpretation of signals,
* less aggressive time pausing,
* weaker identification assistance,
* shorter intelligence history,
* or fewer warnings about unusual world behavior.

The simulation itself should remain consistent.

The ideal expert player is better because they notice and interpret more, not because enemy units secretly receive enormous statistical bonuses.

---

# 24. Presentation

Project Signal should work as a 2D strategic game.

The core design does not depend on fully simulated 3D battles.

The player should perceive a giant operational theater through:

* maps,
* terrain,
* symbols,
* overlays,
* movement,
* signal visualization,
* infrastructure,
* environmental effects,
* and selective animation.

The simulation comes first.

The renderer explains the simulation.

A visually spectacular 3D implementation may someday be possible, but it must never become a prerequisite for proving the game.

---

# 25. Interaction Philosophy

The player should interact with regions and systems rather than micromanaging hundreds of individual pieces.

The user interface should emphasize:

* strategic intent,
* geographic relationships,
* information confidence,
* logistics,
* timelines,
* environmental state,
* and consequences.

Players should be able to zoom between broad theater awareness and specific local situations without changing into an entirely different game.

The interface should make a 500-kilometer problem understandable without pretending it is a five-kilometer battlefield.

---

# 26. Emergent Narrative

Project Signal should generate stories naturally.

Examples of desirable player stories:

> I thought those organisms were migrating toward the lake, but they were actually preparing a brood site behind my refinery.

> I moved my aircraft north because their surface activity looked like an attack, and then they emerged near my rail corridor two days later.

> I was preparing to harvest that entire valley until a dry-season lightning storm started a wildfire and destroyed the herd.

> I spent weeks building a rail extension toward the western mountains, which made my opponent think I had discovered a major resource deposit. I was actually building an airfield.

> They bombed the brood site exactly like I expected, but I had already moved the developmental stock through the aquifer.

These stories should come from interacting mechanics rather than scripted narrative branches.

---

# 27. What Project Signal Is Not

Project Signal is not a traditional base-building RTS.

It is not StarCraft on a giant map.

It is not Factorio.

It is not primarily a tactical wargame.

It is not a society simulator.

It is not a city builder.

It is not an ecosystem simulator built for scientific accuracy.

It is not a meteorological simulator.

It is not a game about manually controlling thousands of individual soldiers.

It is not a game where technological progression simply replaces old units with statistically superior versions.

It is not dependent on 3D combat spectacle.

The Plastai are not a hive mind.

The Vanguard is not omniscient.

The world is not simply scenery.

Fog of war is not simply hidden tiles.

Resources are not permanently memorized coordinate locations.

Weather is not a random collection of temporary buffs and debuffs.

Large units are not casually manufactured super-weapons.

Uncertainty is not an excuse for arbitrary outcomes.

---

# 28. Development Doctrine

The prototype should prove the strategic loop before attempting to provide large amounts of content.

Build systems before breadth.

The initial development priority should roughly follow:

**World → Time → Movement → Resources → Signals → Knowledge → Faction Interaction → Consequences**

The prototype does not require:

* dozens of unit types,
* extensive lore,
* elaborate graphics,
* detailed animations,
* comprehensive technology trees,
* a large user interface,
* cinematic battles,
* or final balance.

The first meaningful prototype succeeds if the player can observe a changing world, interpret incomplete information, make a strategic commitment, and later experience a consequence produced by that commitment.

Everything else can grow from there.

---

# 29. Design Against Premature Complexity

Project Signal contains many systems that could individually become enormous projects.

Development should continuously resist building complexity that does not prove the central game.

A system should initially be represented by the simplest model capable of producing the desired strategic behavior.

Weather does not need atmospheric fluid dynamics.

Ecology does not need individual genetic simulation.

Logistics does not need every crate modeled.

Combat does not need detailed ballistic simulation.

Aquifers do not need geological hydrodynamics.

The simulation should be deep where depth creates decisions.

It should be abstract where additional realism produces only engineering work.

---

# 30. The Project Signal Test

When considering a new mechanic, feature, unit, resource, or subsystem, evaluate it against the following questions.

### Does it create something meaningful for players to observe or interpret?

If a mechanic exists entirely behind the scenes and creates no meaningful decision, it may not deserve simulation.

### Does it interact with the persistent world?

Features should ideally connect with terrain, time, ecology, infrastructure, information, logistics, or other existing systems.

### Does it strengthen faction asymmetry?

A new feature should usually reinforce the fundamentally different ways Vanguard and Plastai operate.

### Does distance matter?

Project Signal loses part of its identity if capability can be projected everywhere instantly.

### Does time matter?

Preparation, travel, construction, development, and recovery should create strategic consequences.

### Does logistics matter?

Power should generally require systems that sustain it.

### Can the opponent observe or infer something about it?

Major capabilities should usually produce some evidence before their full consequences occur.

### Is there counterplay?

Counterplay may involve prevention, interference, avoidance, deception, adaptation, or exploiting the opponent's commitment.

It does not always require direct combat.

### Can the same event mean different things?

Ambiguous evidence strengthens the information game.

### Does this create stories through interacting systems?

The strongest Project Signal mechanics should produce events that players want to recount afterward.

### Is this solving a Project Signal problem, or importing a convention from another genre?

Do not add a mechanic simply because RTS games traditionally contain it.

---

# 31. Final Vision

Project Signal should feel like commanding one side of an enormous conflict that neither player can fully see.

A continent-sized world continues moving beneath both factions.

Weather changes plans.

Animals migrate.

Infrastructure expands.

Nutrients flow underground.

Aircraft relocate.

Broods mature.

Signals accumulate.

Players form theories.

They commit resources based on those theories.

Eventually reality reveals whether they were right.

The defining moments of Project Signal should not simply be:

> I built the stronger army.

They should be:

> I understood what was happening before my opponent did.

Or:

> I convinced my opponent that they understood what was happening.

And sometimes:

> Neither of us understood what was happening until the world made the decision for us.
