# Project Signal — Prototype Scope

## 1. Purpose

The Project Signal prototype exists to answer one central question:

**Is it compelling to observe an evolving large-scale world, interpret incomplete signals, make a strategic commitment, and later experience consequences produced by that decision?**

The prototype does not exist to prove final graphics, final balance, content breadth, complete faction rosters, endgame systems, campaign structure, multiplayer, full ecological realism, or production architecture.

It exists to prove the game.

The prototype therefore concentrates development effort on the interaction between world simulation, incomplete information, distance, time, logistics, faction asymmetry, and delayed consequences.

If those elements do not create compelling strategic decisions in a deliberately small vertical slice, adding more units, more biomes, more technology, more art, or a larger map does not solve the underlying problem.

---

## 2. Prototype Doctrine

**Build the smallest simulation that can produce the intended strategic loop.**

The prototype prefers:

* one working interaction over ten planned systems
* coarse abstraction over unnecessary fidelity
* visible cause and effect over hidden complexity
* deterministic or simple behavior where sufficient
* functional UI over polished UI
* scenario depth over content breadth
* systems that interact over systems that merely exist

Every system must justify its inclusion by helping prove the core experience.

Prototype development should aggressively resist the instinct to reproduce the final design at reduced scale. The prototype is an experiment, not a miniature production build.

A crude system that creates the intended decision is more valuable than a sophisticated system that does not.

---

## 3. Core Loop to Prove

The central prototype loop is:

**Observe → Interpret → Prepare → Commit → Reveal → Adapt**

Everything else exists to support this cycle.

### Observe

The player sees incomplete evidence generated by the world.

This evidence may come from sensors, visible infrastructure, wildlife behavior, logistics movement, weather, terrain disturbance, biological activity, or other indirect observations.

The player does not automatically receive the underlying truth.

### Interpret

The player decides what the evidence probably means.

A signal may indicate enemy activity, normal ecological behavior, weather effects, logistical preparation, or several possibilities at once.

Interpretation is a player decision rather than an automatic classification step.

### Prepare

The player begins positioning or developing capability in response to that interpretation.

Preparation may include:

* repositioning reconnaissance
* moving aircraft
* shifting defensive forces
* routing supply
* building infrastructure
* accumulating nutrients
* developing brood
* investigating another region

Preparation consumes time and usually creates additional observable evidence.

### Commit

The player makes a choice that consumes time, resources, geographic opportunity, or strategic flexibility.

A Vanguard aircraft moved hundreds of kilometers away cannot instantly defend another region.

A Plastai nutrient investment committed to one brood site is not simultaneously available everywhere else.

Commitment is the point where interpretation becomes strategically consequential.

### Reveal

Later events clarify the underlying situation.

A suspected migration may prove natural.

A seemingly active Plastai site may prove to be a diversion.

A previously quiet valley may erupt with a force that required substantial hidden preparation.

The reveal should emerge from simulation rather than arbitrary surprise.

### Adapt

The player responds to whether the original interpretation was correct.

Adaptation may involve recovering from a mistake, exploiting a successful read, redirecting logistics, abandoning an objective, reinforcing a threatened region, or changing how future signals are interpreted.

The prototype succeeds only if completing this cycle feels meaningful.

---

## 4. One Small but Real Strategic Theater

The prototype uses only a subset of the eventual Wyoming-scale world.

It does not attempt to implement the full state.

The active region must nevertheless be large enough for:

* distance
* movement time
* logistics
* incomplete coverage
* regional differences
* power-projection limits
* delayed response

to matter.

The prototype must not collapse Project Signal into a conventional RTS arena where every important location is only seconds from every other location.

A limited geographic region is acceptable.

Compressed strategic relationships are not.

The exact dimensions remain an open prototype question, but the selected theater should contain several distinct regions separated by meaningful travel time and terrain.

---

## 5. Full-Scale Coordinate Philosophy

Distances are represented at approximately intended real-world scale even when only part of the eventual world is active.

This allows the prototype to test:

* travel time
* sensor range
* aircraft range
* logistics reach
* response delay
* weather movement
* migration
* time compression

without discovering later that the design only worked because the prototype placed everything unrealistically close together.

Full-scale coordinates do not require full geographic fidelity.

Terrain may be simplified, stylized, or hand-authored.

Its presentation may be flat, relief-based, or genuinely three-dimensional. The prototype should use terrain geometry only where it economically improves the readability of elevation, valleys, passes, slopes, or other strategically meaningful geography.

The scale relationships must remain meaningful.

---

## 6. Minimal Terrain

The prototype includes only enough terrain variation to create strategic differences.

A sufficient initial set is:

* open plains
* forest
* elevated or mountainous terrain
* river or lake
* wetland or productive valley

Terrain exists only where it changes gameplay.

A terrain type should affect at least one meaningful system such as:

* movement
* sensing
* logistics
* ecology
* fire behavior
* weather interaction
* faction access
* power projection

If a terrain category does not affect a decision, it does not yet need to exist.

The prototype does not require a comprehensive Wyoming biome catalog.

---

## 7. Minimal Weather

Weather exists from the beginning in simplified form.

The prototype only needs enough weather behavior to prove that current world state can change the strategic consequences of an event.

An initial set may include:

* clear conditions
* rain
* thunderstorm
* dry or fire-prone conditions

The important test is not weather variety.

It is:

**Can one weather event create different consequences depending on the current world state?**

For example, a thunderstorm may:

* reduce or temporarily ground Vanguard aviation
* produce lightning
* ignite vegetation if the affected region is sufficiently dry
* fail to ignite vegetation in a wet world state
* drive wildlife away from fire
* destroy or displace biomass important to Plastai
* create additional signals and uncertainty

Weather therefore becomes part of the world rather than a global combat modifier.

---

## 8. Minimal Ecology

Ecology is highly simplified.

The prototype may represent fauna as:

* herd entities
* migration groups
* regional biomass concentrations

rather than individually simulated animals.

The initial ecology needs only to prove that:

* wildlife moves independently of both factions
* migration produces detectable signals
* Plastai can exploit biomass
* weather alters ecological conditions
* faction activity alters ecological behavior
* ecological behavior can be mistaken for faction activity

The ecology must be independent enough to create evidence the player did not directly cause.

It does not need food webs, detailed reproduction, species genetics, or complete population simulation.

The prototype does not build an ecosystem simulator.

It builds enough ecology to make the strategic world feel alive and occasionally ambiguous.

---

## 9. Minimal Faction Scope

Both Vanguard and Plastai exist in the prototype.

Neither requires anything resembling a final roster.

The goal is to prove asymmetry.

Each faction needs only enough capability to:

* gather information
* move through the world
* establish logistics
* accumulate or route resources
* prepare an action
* project force
* create detectable signatures
* conceal or confuse intent
* suffer persistent consequences

The two factions should not be implemented as mirrored economies with different names.

Their prototype systems must already express their distinct strategic identities.

---

## 10. Prototype Vanguard

The minimal Vanguard implementation centers on infrastructure, limited human presence, networked reach, logistics, sensors, and concentrated technological power.

A minimal capability set may contain:

* one scarce human hub or personnel abstraction
* one ground reconnaissance capability
* one sensor or communications capability
* one logistics corridor
* one depot or logistical hub
* one industrial facility such as a refinery
* one airfield
* one aircraft capability
* one ranged strike or defensive capability

The exact unit labels matter less than proving that:

* infrastructure creates reach
* Vanguard capability depends on functioning networks
* aircraft are extremely powerful but geographically constrained
* distance and travel time matter
* information quality affects strike decisions
* logistical activity reveals intent
* Vanguard cannot maintain overwhelming force everywhere simultaneously
* losing rare infrastructure or personnel matters

The prototype does not need tanks, infantry squads, helicopters, trains, drones, artillery families, aircraft classes, or a complete combined-arms roster.

---

## 11. Prototype Plastai

The minimal Plastai implementation centers on biological preparation, biomass, nutrient movement, aquifer connectivity, maturation, hidden buildup, and sudden emergence.

A minimal capability set may contain:

* one biomass-gathering capability
* one brood site
* a simplified nutrient network
* one aquifer connection
* one mobile reconnaissance phenotype
* one combat phenotype
* one dormant or delayed-emergence mechanic

The prototype must prove:

* biological preparation
* nutrient routing
* maturation or delayed capability
* hidden buildup
* strategic repositioning through the aquifer system
* surface activity as a source of signals
* sudden but earned emergence

The Plastai should feel capable of making long preparations whose significance becomes clear only later.

The prototype does not require a phenotype catalog, advanced genetic assimilation, Titans, or a complete lifecycle simulation.

---

## 12. Minimal Resource Model

Resources remain deliberately simple.

### Vanguard

The initial model may use only:

* industrial supply
* fuel or operational capacity

### Plastai

The initial model may use only:

* biomass
* nutrients

The objective is not to create elaborate production chains.

Resources exist to test:

* location
* acquisition
* transport
* accumulation
* allocation
* opportunity cost
* strategic concentration

A resource matters because having it in the wrong place is different from having it in the right place.

The prototype should avoid resources that merely produce additional resource types.

---

## 13. Minimal Logistics

Both factions require visible logistics because power in Project Signal must exist somewhere rather than globally.

### Vanguard

The initial logistics model may consist of:

* one road or rail corridor
* one logistical hub
* physical movement of supply
* one remote facility dependent on that supply

Supply does not need detailed inventory simulation.

The player should nevertheless understand that remote capability is sustained by a physical connection.

### Plastai

The initial system consists of:

* one aquifer route
* one nutrient-flow network
* one or more connected brood locations

Nutrients should move through the network rather than behaving as a global currency.

The key prototype question is:

**Can disrupting, extending, or relocating logistics meaningfully change where power can exist?**

If logistics does not affect power projection, the implementation is too shallow.

---

## 14. Minimal Time System

Time compression is mandatory.

It is not a later convenience feature.

The player must be able to:

* pause
* run at a useful observation speed
* accelerate through strategically quiet periods
* be interrupted when meaningful events occur

The game operates across distances and processes that cannot function at conventional RTS pacing.

Construction, migration, logistics, brood development, reconnaissance, weather, and long-distance movement all require time.

Without time compression, the prototype cannot test the intended strategic timescale.

The purpose of accelerated time is not to make waiting tolerable.

It is to allow the player to move rapidly between meaningful decisions.

---

## 15. Minimal Attention System

The prototype includes a simple automatic interruption system.

Useful initial triggers include:

* new contact detected
* important facility threatened
* major signal appears
* critical weather condition develops
* construction completes
* brood emergence begins
* logistics route is disrupted

The first version does not require fully player-configurable trigger logic.

It only needs to demonstrate that the player can safely accelerate long periods without manually watching every system continuously.

Difficulty should not be created by forcing the player to stare at the map at low speed in case something happens.

---

## 16. Minimal Signal Model

The signal model is one of the highest-priority prototype systems.

At minimum, simulation state distinguishes:

* **World Reality**
* **Vanguard Knowledge**
* **Plastai Knowledge**

World Reality contains the actual state.

Faction knowledge contains only information that faction has acquired.

The player never automatically receives omniscient truth.

An initial signal vocabulary may include:

* movement
* ecological disturbance
* infrastructure activity
* unknown biological activity
* logistics movement

Each signal may contain:

* location
* observation time
* rough classification
* source where appropriate
* confidence or uncertainty where useful

The prototype must resist converting every signal directly into a known unit or known enemy action.

Signals are evidence.

---

## 17. Stale Information

Information ages.

A contact observed ten minutes or ten simulated hours ago does not continue following the true entity simply because it was seen once.

Previously observed information remains as historical knowledge.

Simple representations are sufficient:

* ghost marker
* timestamp
* faded icon
* uncertainty radius

The important rule is:

**Known once does not mean known forever.**

This alone should create meaningful differences between reconnaissance and omniscience.

---

## 18. Ambiguous Signals

At least one prototype signal must support multiple plausible explanations.

For example, a herd begins moving unexpectedly through a valley.

Possible explanations include:

* normal migration
* weather
* predators
* nearby Plastai activity
* Vanguard disturbance

The player may investigate, ignore it, reposition assets, or make a partial commitment.

The system should not immediately tell the player which explanation is correct.

This ambiguity is essential to Project Signal.

Uncertainty must nevertheless remain grounded.

The player should be able to look backward after a reveal and understand why the signal occurred.

---

## 19. Minimal Player Annotation

Player annotation is desirable but not mandatory for the earliest playable slice.

If implemented, the system remains extremely simple.

Useful annotation types include:

* suspected Plastai site
* danger
* investigate
* possible route
* expected movement

The purpose is not map drawing.

It is to test whether externalizing player hypotheses improves the process of interpreting incomplete information across long simulated periods.

If annotation materially delays the core prototype, it may be deferred.

---

## 20. One Meaningful Deception Scenario

The prototype intentionally supports at least one situation in which ordinary game systems allow one faction to encourage a reasonable false interpretation.

The strongest initial example is Plastai site deception.

Plastai maintains visible activity at Site A while routing resources and preparing meaningful capability at Site B.

The Vanguard player sees genuine evidence at Site A.

The evidence is not fake.

The interpretation is incomplete.

A Vanguard equivalent may emerge from logistics activity toward one region while airpower or another capability is being prepared for a different objective.

The prototype does not need explicit deception abilities.

It needs to prove that deception can emerge naturally because preparation leaves evidence and evidence requires interpretation.

---

## 21. Minimal Combat

Combat is deliberately coarse.

It only needs to prove that:

* preparation matters
* range matters
* support matters
* information matters
* local concentration matters
* losses persist

Combat resolution may initially use simple strength calculations, range checks, support modifiers, and understandable attrition.

Early development should avoid:

* detailed ballistics
* elaborate animation
* complex armor models
* component damage simulation
* dozens of combat statistics
* tactical micromanagement

Combat is a consequence generator.

It is not the main source of strategic depth in the prototype.

---

## 22. One Vanguard Power-Projection Moment

The prototype supports at least one moment in which Vanguard power feels overwhelming because the player correctly prepared for the situation.

For example:

The player identifies a Plastai brood site with sufficient confidence, positions aircraft or ranged fire within operational reach, and destroys the site decisively.

The event should demonstrate:

* Vanguard firepower is dangerous
* intelligence enabled the strike
* infrastructure enabled the strike
* geography constrained the strike
* assets committed here are unavailable elsewhere

The correct lesson is not that Vanguard weapons are weak enough for Plastai to survive anything.

The lesson is that Vanguard can deliver devastating force where it has correctly concentrated attention and capability.

---

## 23. One Plastai Power-Projection Moment

The prototype supports at least one equally clear Plastai payoff.

A prepared force near a secondary objective may remain dormant while Vanguard observes obvious activity elsewhere.

After Vanguard commits its mobile firepower to the visible threat, the prepared Plastai force emerges near a refinery or logistical corridor.

This demonstrates:

* prior investment
* hidden preparation
* geographic commitment
* delayed consequence
* exploitation of enemy attention
* asymmetric mobility

The emergence may appear sudden to Vanguard.

It must not be arbitrary.

From the Plastai side and from an omniscient debug view, the preparation should be clearly visible.

---

## 24. Minimal Combat Persistence

Combat changes the world.

At least some consequences remain after fighting ends.

Examples include:

* destroyed infrastructure
* lost brood
* consumed resources
* damaged logistics
* dead herd
* burned terrain
* abandoned facility

The map should remember that something happened.

Persistent consequence reinforces that combat is part of the evolving simulation rather than an isolated tactical minigame.

---

## 25. One Weather–World Interaction

The prototype explicitly demonstrates:

**Weather + World State = Outcome**

A thunderstorm provides a useful initial case.

It may:

* limit Vanguard aviation
* generate lightning
* ignite sufficiently dry vegetation
* spread fire through vulnerable terrain
* drive wildlife elsewhere
* reduce nearby biomass
* create new movement signals

The same storm in a wetter state may ground aircraft without causing a major wildfire.

The weather event itself therefore does not contain a fixed list of faction buffs and debuffs.

Its effects emerge from interaction with current conditions.

---

## 26. One Ecology–Signal Interaction

The prototype includes at least one situation where ecology creates information ambiguity.

Wildlife may begin migrating through or away from a suspected Plastai region.

The Vanguard player must consider whether:

* this is ordinary movement
* weather caused it
* something frightened the herd
* Plastai activity caused it

The system does not need deep ecology.

It needs ecology capable of producing strategically meaningful evidence.

---

## 27. One Logistics–Signal Interaction

Logistics reveals intent.

The prototype includes at least one case where increasing logistical activity becomes evidence of preparation.

For Vanguard, this may be increased traffic toward a remote airfield or facility.

For Plastai, it may be increased nutrient flow toward a brood site that otherwise appears inactive.

This interaction is particularly important because it creates signals directly from ordinary strategic behavior.

The player should sometimes realize:

**I could see what they were preparing. I simply interpreted it incorrectly.**

That is preferable to hidden scripted surprises.

---

## 28. Minimal Renderer

The renderer follows `renderer/renderer-vision.md`.

The prototype renderer only needs:

* a strategic map, with 3D terrain permitted where useful
* terrain
* basic entity icons, sprites, billboards, or similarly simple symbols
* visible movement
* selectable entities
* signal markers
* simple uncertainty representation
* time controls
* a few useful overlays

Visual polish is deliberately low priority.

The renderer succeeds when the player can understand the simulation quickly enough to make strategic decisions.

It does not need to make those decisions cinematic.

---

## 29. Required Overlays

The initial overlay set remains small.

Useful overlays include:

* terrain
* faction infrastructure
* sensor or knowledge coverage
* logistics
* Plastai aquifer network
* weather

Not every overlay must be visible to both factions.

The aquifer network, for example, belongs to Plastai knowledge unless Vanguard has somehow inferred or detected relevant activity.

The prototype does not require a comprehensive GIS-style overlay catalog.

---

## 30. Debug Views

Debug visualization is essential.

Development builds should allow inspection of:

* World Reality
* Vanguard Knowledge
* Plastai Knowledge
* active signals
* entity routes
* weather state
* logistics state
* resource movement
* aquifer connectivity
* brood preparation
* sensor coverage

Debug views may be ugly.

They should make systemic failures obvious.

A developer must be able to determine whether a surprising outcome was caused by intended simulation behavior, bad information propagation, broken pathing, incorrect state, or simple balance.

Early debug visibility is more valuable than presentation polish.

---

## 31. Determinism and Replay

Simulation runs should be reproducible where practical.

A fixed seed or equivalent mechanism is valuable for:

* debugging
* comparing design changes
* reproducing emergent situations
* testing signal generation
* examining different player responses to the same world

Full production replay functionality is not required.

Basic event history, periodic snapshots, or deterministic reruns are sufficient initially.

The important requirement is the ability to inspect cause and effect.

---

## 32. Minimal AI

The first prototype does not attempt sophisticated strategic AI.

Opposition may use:

* scripted behavior
* rule-based agents
* scenario logic
* simple priorities

The AI exists to create situations that test the player's ability to interpret signals and make commitments.

It does not need to beat expert players.

A carefully constrained opponent using real systems is preferable to a broad but incompetent general AI.

AI sophistication comes after the core loop proves itself.

---

## 33. Scenario-Driven Prototype

The first playable prototype should be scenario-driven.

A curated scenario is preferable because it can reliably expose the systems the prototype is intended to test.

The scenario may deliberately establish:

* ambiguous migration
* one visible Plastai buildup
* one hidden Plastai buildup
* one Vanguard logistical expansion
* one significant weather event
* one major reveal

The scenario should create initial conditions and broad pressures.

It should not fake outcomes.

Wherever practical, migration, buildup, movement, sensing, logistics, combat, and weather consequences should use the same real systems intended for later free simulation.

---

## 34. Player Faction for First Playable Version

The first player-controlled faction remains an open scope decision.

Both factions must exist within the simulation, but both do not need full player-facing controls immediately.

The strongest prototype path is likely:

* one faction player-controlled
* one faction driven by scenario logic and simple AI
* omniscient debug control available during development

This avoids doubling UI and interaction scope before the strategic loop has been proven.

Full two-faction playability should be added when it becomes necessary to test whether the asymmetry is compelling from both perspectives.

---

## 35. Content Limits

The prototype intentionally contains very little content.

A useful target is:

* a handful of entity types
* a handful of facilities
* a handful of signal categories
* a handful of terrain categories
* a handful of weather states
* a handful of ecological populations
* one primary strategic scenario

Content breadth does not prove design quality.

Every additional content item creates implementation, balance, visualization, debugging, and interaction cost.

New content should be added only when existing systems cannot answer an important design question.

---

## 36. No Technology Tree Yet

The prototype does not contain a full technology tree.

Required capabilities simply exist within controlled scenario constraints.

Research pacing, unlock order, strategic specialization, and faction progression can be tested after the underlying world and signal systems work.

The prototype should not spend months building progression around a loop that may still require redesign.

---

## 37. No Full Victory System Yet

The prototype does not require final victory conditions.

A session may end when:

* the central strategic scenario resolves
* one side completes or prevents a major objective
* a predetermined simulation horizon is reached
* the Observe → Interpret → Prepare → Commit → Reveal → Adapt loop has produced enough evidence for evaluation

Endgame megaprojects are unnecessary.

The prototype proves decision-making, not campaign closure.

---

## 38. No Full Campaign Yet

The prototype does not build:

* campaign maps
* meta progression
* persistent unlocks
* long-term narrative structure
* matchmaking
* multiplayer campaign systems

It is one strategic simulation slice.

A compelling single scenario is more valuable than an empty campaign framework.

---

## 40. Vertical Slice Scenario

A successful prototype session may unfold like this:

1. Vanguard operates a refinery and airfield connected by a logistics corridor.
2. The facilities provide valuable capability but are far enough apart that positioning matters.
3. Plastai gathers biomass from nearby herds and routes nutrients through a small aquifer network.
4. Wildlife begins moving unexpectedly through one valley.
5. Vanguard detects movement and biological disturbance but cannot determine whether the cause is ordinary migration or Plastai activity.
6. Plastai maintains visible activity around one brood site.
7. A second site quietly receives nutrient investment elsewhere.
8. Vanguard investigates the visible site and increasingly interprets it as the primary threat.
9. Vanguard moves aircraft or ranged capability toward that region.
10. The movement creates logistical activity that Plastai can potentially observe.
11. Weather begins deteriorating.
12. A thunderstorm restricts Vanguard aviation.
13. In sufficiently dry terrain, lightning may also create a wildfire.
14. Wildlife reacts to the changing conditions, creating additional movement signals.
15. Plastai triggers previously prepared emergence near the refinery or its logistics route.
16. Vanguard discovers that much of its mobile power is committed elsewhere.
17. The player must respond using whatever capability remains within effective range.
18. Combat resolves from the preparation and positions already established.
19. Infrastructure, brood, resources, wildlife, or terrain remain changed afterward.
20. The player can review the preceding signals and understand which clues were interpreted correctly or incorrectly.

This scenario exercises:

* map scale
* time
* movement
* signals
* stale knowledge
* ecology
* weather
* logistics
* faction asymmetry
* combat
* deception
* delayed consequences

The final outcome is not scripted.

---

## 41. Prototype Milestones

Milestones are based on playable capability rather than code completion.

### Milestone 1 — World Runs

* map exists
* time advances
* entities exist
* entities move
* simulation state can be inspected

At this point, there is a world but not yet a game.

### Milestone 2 — World Changes

* weather changes
* wildlife migrates
* resources exist geographically
* logistics moves resources
* world state produces visible consequences

The simulation begins generating activity without player input.

### Milestone 3 — Knowledge Is Separate

* World Reality differs from faction knowledge
* sensing creates observations
* signals are generated
* stale information exists
* debug views expose the difference

This milestone establishes the foundation of Project Signal.

### Milestone 4 — Factions Prepare

* Vanguard infrastructure and logistics function
* Plastai biomass, nutrients, aquifers, and brood preparation function
* preparation requires time
* preparation creates evidence

Both factions can now create future capability.

### Milestone 5 — Commitment Matters

* assets take meaningful time to reposition
* resources are consumed
* logistics constrains reach
* geography limits response
* choosing one region weakens another

The player can now make strategically expensive decisions.

### Milestone 6 — Reveal and Consequence

* hidden or uncertain situations become clearer
* combat or emergence occurs
* earlier preparation affects outcomes
* losses persist
* player interpretation can be evaluated in hindsight

The prototype now contains the core payoff.

### Milestone 7 — Playable Loop

The player can complete:

**Observe → Interpret → Prepare → Commit → Reveal → Adapt**

without developer intervention.

At this point, the central design can be judged.

## 42. Success Criteria

The prototype is successful when players can truthfully say things like:

* “I saw evidence but wasn't sure what it meant.”
* “I made a decision based on that interpretation.”
* “Moving my forces took enough time that the commitment mattered.”
* “I realized too late that I had interpreted the situation incorrectly.”
* “The opponent's plan had visible clues in hindsight.”
* “I thought that signal meant one thing, but now I understand what caused it.”
* “Weather changed what I could do.”
* “The world felt like it was doing things independently of me.”
* “I couldn't protect everything at once.”
* “The battle felt like the consequence of decisions I made earlier.”
* “Their attack felt surprising without feeling arbitrary.”
* “My successful strike felt earned because I found the right target.”
* “I wanted to try again because I thought I could read the situation better next time.”

These reactions matter more than visual polish.

The most important sign of success is not that the player enjoyed watching the simulation.

It is that the player formed hypotheses, made commitments based on them, experienced consequences, and immediately began thinking about how they would interpret the same world differently next time.

---

## 43. Failure Criteria

The prototype requires redesign if the dominant experience becomes:

* waiting for bars to fill
* watching units walk
* conventional RTS base building
* clicking faster than the opponent
* memorizing resource locations
* memorizing scripted events
* reacting to obvious enemy icons
* reading endless notifications
* managing tiny logistics details
* performing repetitive construction
* manually babysitting routine movement
* watching the simulation play itself
* guessing because information is arbitrary
* losing to events with no detectable warning
* maintaining perfect awareness by leaving time slow
* spending more time fighting UI friction than interpreting the world
* treating every unidentified signal as automatically hostile
* discovering that logistics exists only as flavor
* discovering that distance rarely changes decisions
* discovering that each faction solves problems in essentially the same way

These failure modes should trigger design changes before new content is added.

---

## 44. Prototype Metrics

Prototype evaluation remains lightweight.

Useful questions include:

* How often does the player change plans because of new information?
* How long does the player remain at maximum time compression?
* How often do meaningful events interrupt accelerated time?
* Can the player explain why an asset, facility, brood site, or route was lost?
* Can the player identify signals they ignored or misread in hindsight?
* Can the player distinguish evidence from confirmed knowledge?
* Does one faction feel fundamentally different from the other?
* Does distance materially affect decisions?
* Does logistics materially affect power projection?
* Does weather change player behavior?
* Does ecology create useful uncertainty?
* Do players investigate ambiguous events?
* Does committing capability to one region make another region meaningfully weaker?
* Does the player want another attempt because they believe better interpretation would improve the outcome?

The prototype does not require a live-service telemetry platform.

Observation, event logs, simple counters, and player discussion are sufficient.

---

## 45. Complexity Budget

Every prototype system consumes complexity budget.

Before adding any feature, ask:

* Does this create a new decision?
* Does this clarify an existing decision?
* Does this enable the core loop?
* Does this create useful interaction with another system?
* Is there a simpler representation?
* Will the player actually notice it?

If the answer is no, defer it.

Complexity is justified by gameplay leverage, not by simulation elegance.

---

## 46. Prototype Abstraction Rules

Temporary abstractions are expected.

Examples include:

* herds represented as circles or icons
* aircraft represented as moving symbols
* nutrient flow represented as lines
* logistics represented by simple moving packets
* combat represented by coarse resolution
* weather represented as regional states
* construction represented by timers
* brood development represented by staged state
* fire represented as changing regional cells
* detection represented by simple range and probability rules

Prototype abstraction is not technical debt by default.

It is a deliberate method for testing design before investing in fidelity.

An abstraction becomes a problem only when it prevents the prototype from answering the design question it exists to test.

---

## 47. Systems Before Content

Development priority is:

**World → Time → Movement → Resources → Signals → Knowledge → Faction Interaction → Consequences**

This ordering matters.

A large catalog of units built before movement, information, logistics, and consequences work provides little useful evidence about Project Signal.

A single herd, one aircraft, one brood site, one sensor, and one logistics route interacting correctly are more valuable than fifty disconnected content definitions.

Systems prove the game.

Content expands it later.

---

## 48. Simulation Before Renderer Polish

The simulation remains usable in ugly debug form.

Early versions may rely on:

* simple shapes
* labels
* lines
* range circles
* debug panels
* colored regions
* crude icons

before establishing a final visual language.

The renderer must expose enough state for humans to understand the simulation, but visual production should not become the project.

A beautiful renderer cannot rescue an uninteresting strategic loop.

---

## 49. Avoid Premature Generalization

The prototype does not build abstract frameworks solely because the final game might eventually need them.

Avoid premature systems such as:

* universal moddable unit schemas
* generic scripting engines
* elaborate plugin architectures
* universal ability frameworks
* generalized effect stacks
* comprehensive data-driven content pipelines
* massive event frameworks
* generic campaign engines

Code should remain understandable and separable.

It does not need to solve hypothetical requirements several years in advance.

Generalize when the prototype produces a second or third real use case that requires generalization.

---

## 50. Architecture Boundary

This document defines prototype behavior, not exact software architecture.

Implementation should preserve several important boundaries:

* simulation and rendering remain separable
* faction knowledge remains distinct from World Reality
* simulation state remains inspectable
* deterministic or reproducible behavior is used where practical
* the simulation can run without final visual fidelity

Detailed engine, rendering, storage, serialization, component, threading, and code-organization decisions belong in technical documents.

---

## 51. What Should Come After the Prototype

If the core loop works, subsequent development should increase depth before breadth.

Possible expansions include:

* larger active map region
* richer logistics
* more signal types
* additional ecological behaviors
* additional Plastai phenotypes
* additional Vanguard infrastructure
* richer weather interactions
* more deception possibilities
* stronger player annotation tools
* deeper faction knowledge mechanics
* subfactions
* session variability
* strategic projects

These are not an automatic roadmap.

Prototype results determine priority.

If players love interpreting signals but logistics feels shallow, deepen logistics.

If signals feel arbitrary, improve information causality.

If geography does not matter, fix scale and movement before expanding the map.

If both factions feel similar, deepen asymmetry before adding roster breadth.

Development should follow evidence.

---

## 52. What the Prototype Must Never Become

The prototype is:

* not a miniature version of the entire final game
* not a content-production exercise
* not an excuse to build every system simultaneously
* not a conventional RTS with Project Signal terminology layered on top
* not dependent on final art
* not dependent on detailed 3D game content
* not dependent on sophisticated AI
* not a full ecosystem simulation
* not a full economy simulation
* not a full campaign
* not a technology-tree prototype
* not multiplayer-first
* not optimized around speculative future scale before the loop is fun
* not required to make both factions equally complete
* not a showcase for engine technology
* not a procedural-generation demonstration
* not a vehicle roster simulator
* not a wildlife simulator
* not a logistics spreadsheet
* not a tactical combat game pretending to be strategic
* not a prototype that hides weak gameplay behind complexity

The prototype exists to expose weak gameplay as early as possible.

That is success even if the result forces major redesign.

---

## 53. Open Prototype Questions

The following questions remain legitimately open:

* What exact geographic size best preserves intended scale while remaining manageable?
* Which faction should be player-controlled first?
* At what milestone should the second faction become fully player-controlled?
* What is the minimum useful entity count?
* What combat abstraction produces understandable consequences without tactical overdevelopment?
* How many weather states are actually required?
* Is road logistics sufficient initially, or does rail provide an essential distinction?
* Does aircraft capability belong in the earliest playable build or immediately after the initial ground loop?
* Do player annotations justify inclusion in the first slice?
* How much of the Plastai aquifer network should be visible in the normal player interface?
* What level of AI scripting is sufficient for repeatable tests without making scenarios feel predetermined?
* How much seed and replay support is needed before systematic playtesting?
* What exact condition ends the first prototype scenario?

These questions should be answered through implementation pressure and playtesting rather than speculative design.

---

## 54. Prototype Design Test

Before adding a feature to the prototype, ask:

* Does this help prove the core signal loop?
* Does it create a meaningful strategic decision?
* Does it make distance matter?
* Does it make time matter?
* Does it create information?
* Does it help the player interpret information?
* Does it preserve uncertainty?
* Does it reinforce faction asymmetry?
* Does it create delayed consequences?
* Does it interact with the world?
* Does it create evidence of strategic intent?
* Can it be represented more simply?
* Can the prototype succeed without it?
* Are we adding it because Project Signal needs it, or because strategy games usually have it?
* Will the player notice this feature through gameplay?
* Will this feature help expose a design failure?
* Does this help us decide whether the final game is worth building?

The final question overrides everything else:

**Is the Project Signal loop fun before we spend years building Project Signal?**
