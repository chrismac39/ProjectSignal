# World and Ecology

## Core Principle

The world is not a backdrop.

The world is an active system.

The planet exists before the arrival of either faction.

It possesses:

* Ecosystems
* Wildlife
* Migration patterns
* Predators
* Climate
* Geography
* Biological cycles

The world continues functioning whether the players interact with it or not.

---

## Unknown World

Humanity possesses limited knowledge of the planet.

Prior to arrival, humanity has access only to:

* Orbital imagery
* Spectral analysis
* Atmospheric readings
* Long-range observation

Humans know the planet contains:

* Megaflora
* Megafauna
* Extensive ecosystems

Humans do not possess a complete biological catalog.

Humans cannot automatically distinguish:

* Native wildlife
* Alien organisms
* Ecological disturbances
* Adaptation structures

The world itself creates uncertainty.

---

## The Planet Must Be Believable Without Players

If both factions were removed from the simulation, the world should still appear alive.

Examples:

* Animals migrate.
* Predators hunt prey.
* Ecosystems expand and contract.
* Forests spread or decline.
* Seasonal changes occur.
* Populations fluctuate.

The world should not feel like empty terrain waiting for players.

---

## Geography Matters

Maps should contain meaningful geographic features.

Examples:

* Mountain systems
* Valleys
* Forests
* Wetlands
* Rivers
* Lakes
* Coastlines
* Islands
* Deserts
* Tundra
* Grasslands

Geography should influence:

* Human logistics
* Alien ecology
* Reconnaissance
* Expansion
* Mobility

---

## Large Operational Scale

Maps are intended to represent operational rather than tactical space.

Example target scale:

100 km x 100 km

Potential future variation may exist.

The map should feel large enough that:

* Reconnaissance matters.
* Logistics matter.
* Movement decisions matter.
* Entire regions can remain poorly understood.

Players should not possess immediate awareness of everything happening.

---

## Wildlife Is Not Decoration

Wildlife exists for reasons beyond visual immersion.

Wildlife may influence:

* Human observations
* Alien resource systems
* Migration signals
* False positives
* Ecological disturbances
* Reconnaissance clues

Wildlife should create information.

Not simply scenery.

---

## Multiple Species

The world should contain many forms of life.

Examples:

* Herd animals
* Predators
* Burrowers
* Flyers
* Aquatic species
* Apex megafauna
* Territorial organisms

Different species create different patterns.

Players learn to recognize those patterns over time.

## Native Megafauna

The planet contains native lifeforms that exist independently of both factions.

Some species may be:

* Large
* Territorial
* Migratory
* Predatory
* Environmentally disruptive

These organisms are not a third playable faction.

They do not participate in strategic objectives.

However, they may influence the battlefield indirectly.

---

### The World Is Dangerous

The planet should occasionally create problems for both factions.

Examples:

* A migration blocks a logistics corridor.
* A territorial megafauna attacks a convoy.
* A predator population disrupts reconnaissance activity.
* Wildlife avoids a region after major combat operations.
* Ecological shifts alter established movement patterns.

Neither faction is guaranteed safe passage through the environment.

---

### Humans Do Not Fully Understand Native Life

Humanity arrives with limited knowledge.

Orbital surveys identify:

* Large organisms
* Major ecosystems
* General biological activity

They do not provide a complete understanding of behavior.

A human commander may initially struggle to distinguish:

* Alien activity
* Native wildlife activity
* Environmental events

The world itself contributes uncertainty.

---

### Aliens Do Not Control The Entire Ecosystem

Aliens are part of the planet's ecology.

They are not masters of it.

Native organisms possess their own behaviors, needs, and territories.

Alien expansion may require:

* Avoiding dangerous species
* Redirecting migrations
* Competing for ecological resources
* Adapting to environmental pressures

The ecosystem is an opportunity, not a guaranteed asset.

---

### Native Life Creates Signal Noise

Many observations should have non-military explanations.

Examples:

* A large migration resembles alien expansion.
* Predator activity resembles a raid.
* A population collapse resembles ecological harvesting.
* Territorial behavior resembles defensive activity.

Players should frequently ask:

"What am I actually looking at?"

rather than immediately concluding:

"This is enemy activity."

---

### Strategic Consequences

Native life should occasionally create meaningful decisions.

Examples:

* Route a convoy through dangerous territory to save time.
* Shell a migration corridor to disrupt possible alien activity.
* Avoid a region entirely due to environmental risk.
* Exploit native behavior to conceal operations.

The world should create opportunities and problems for both factions.

---

### Core Principle

The planet is not empty terrain.

The planet is an active participant in the simulation.

Native life exists for its own reasons and may help, hinder, reveal, conceal, or disrupt both factions without regard for their objectives.


---

## Ecological Noise

Not every observation should be caused by players.

Examples:

* Seasonal migration
* Predator activity
* Wildfire
* Drought
* Population collapse
* Breeding cycles

The world naturally produces signals.

This prevents players from assuming:

Observation = Enemy

---

## Human Relationship To The World

Humans view the planet as:

* Terrain
* Resources
* Infrastructure opportunities
* Hazards
* Unknown biological systems

Humans seek to understand and exploit the environment.

---

## Alien Relationship To The World

Aliens view the planet as:

* Habitat
* Nutrient flow
* Genetic opportunity
* Ecological connectivity

Aliens are not separate from the ecosystem.

They are participants within it.

---

## The World Creates Stories

Many important events should emerge from interaction between:

* Humans
* Aliens
* The environment

Examples:

* A migration accidentally reveals an alien project.
* A drought changes expansion priorities.
* A predator population disrupts reconnaissance.
* A valley becomes strategically important for different reasons to each faction.

The world should generate situations rather than merely host them.

---

## Core Principle

The world is not the battlefield.

The world is the environment in which the battlefield emerges.

Humans, aliens, and the planet itself continuously influence one another.
