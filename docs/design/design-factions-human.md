# Human Faction

## Purpose

The Human Faction represents an industrial expeditionary civilization arriving on an inhabited and poorly understood world.

Unlike the Alien Faction, Humans solve problems through technology, logistics, infrastructure, and organization.

Humans do not possess deep ecological understanding.

They seek to expand, exploit resources, establish permanent settlements, and secure a future on the planet despite uncertainty.

The Human experience should emphasize planning, logistics, reconnaissance, and decision-making under incomplete information.

---

# Core Identity

## An Expeditionary Civilization

Humans are newcomers.

The planet existed long before their arrival.

Humanity enters the world with:

* Limited knowledge
* Limited infrastructure
* Limited personnel
* Limited logistical reach

The Human Faction must build its presence over time.

Expansion is not automatic.

Every operation requires planning and support.

---

## Industrial Rather Than Biological

Humans project power through industry.

Human civilization creates:

* Roads
* Railways
* Airfields
* Ports
* Refineries
* Sensor networks
* Vehicles
* Logistics hubs

Human strength comes from organization and technology rather than ecological integration.

---

## The World Is Not Understood

Humans arrive with incomplete knowledge.

Prior to arrival, humanity possesses:

* Orbital imagery
* Spectral analysis
* Atmospheric readings
* Long-range observation

Humans know the planet contains:

* Extensive ecosystems
* Megaflora
* Megafauna
* Complex biological activity

Humans do not possess a complete understanding of the world.

The unknown is a persistent feature of gameplay.

---

# Strategic Objective

## Establish A Permanent Presence

Humans seek to transform a hostile and uncertain environment into a sustainable civilization.

This requires:

* Exploration
* Resource extraction
* Infrastructure construction
* Territorial expansion
* Risk management

The Human Faction's long-term objective is to secure humanity's future on the planet.

---

## Expansion Through Development

Humans do not expand through adaptation.

Humans expand through investment.

New territory becomes useful when:

* Surveyed
* Connected
* Developed
* Supplied
* Defended

Infrastructure is the foundation of expansion.

---

# Resources

## Material Resources

Human civilization depends upon physical resources extracted from the environment.

Examples may include:

* Minerals
* Industrial materials
* Fuel sources
* Water
* Construction inputs

Specific resource implementations remain flexible.

---

## Personnel

Humans rely upon people.

Personnel may be required for:

* Exploration
* Construction
* Maintenance
* Logistics
* Security

Human capabilities are constrained by available manpower.

---

## Logistics

Resources are only useful if they can be moved.

Transportation systems are fundamental.

Examples include:

* Convoys
* Rail networks
* Air transport
* Maritime transport

Logistics are a strategic asset rather than a background mechanic.

---

# Infrastructure

## Civilization Made Visible

Human power should have physical presence on the map.

Examples include:

* Roads
* Railways
* Refineries
* Power generation
* Storage facilities
* Sensor stations
* Settlements

Human influence is visible and material.

---

## Infrastructure Creates Capability

Infrastructure enables:

* Expansion
* Resource extraction
* Mobility
* Information gathering
* Military operations

Humans become stronger by building systems rather than evolving new forms.

---

# Reconnaissance

## Information Is Valuable

Humans begin with incomplete understanding.

Reconnaissance is necessary because:

* The map is large.
* The world is active.
* Signals are ambiguous.
* Ecological activity is constant.

Information is a resource.

---

## Investigation Over Omniscience

Human players should frequently encounter situations where:

* Something happened.
* The cause is unknown.
* Multiple explanations are plausible.

The challenge is deciding which observations deserve attention.

---

# Human Taxonomy Blindness

## Observations Rather Than Classifications

Humans do not automatically understand the planet's life.

Human systems report observations.

Examples:

* Movement detected
* Biological activity detected
* Heat source detected
* Infrastructure damage detected
* Migration observed

Humans do not automatically receive biological classifications.

Examples:

Not:

* Alien scout detected
* Alien assault caste detected
* Alien influence structure detected

Instead:

* Unknown organism detected
* Unusual activity detected
* Coordinated movement observed

Humans must infer causes from observations.

---

## Signal Equivalence

Different causes may generate identical signals.

Examples:

Movement detected:

* Wildlife migration
* Predator activity
* Alien reconnaissance

Infrastructure damage:

* Predator attack
* Stampede
* Alien assault

Population shifts:

* Seasonal change
* Ecological disruption
* Alien harvesting

Human decision-making occurs under uncertainty.

---

# Information Model

## Industrial Understanding

Humans naturally understand:

* Infrastructure
* Logistics
* Construction
* Manufacturing
* Resource extraction

Human players can easily interpret industrial systems.

---

## Ecological Blindness

Humans lack deep ecological knowledge.

They may struggle to interpret:

* Species behavior
* Predator patterns
* Ecological disruption
* Migration changes

The ecosystem generates uncertainty.

---

## Signals And Interpretation

A signal is not information.

A signal is an observation.

Information emerges when players interpret observations and act upon them.

This principle is central to Project Signal.

---

# Mobility

## Infrastructure Mobility

Humans project power through transportation networks.

Examples include:

* Roads
* Railways
* Airfields
* Maritime routes
* Logistics corridors

Infrastructure determines operational reach.

---

## Route Dependency

Humans cannot instantly appear anywhere.

Operations depend upon:

* Travel time
* Route availability
* Supply access
* Infrastructure quality

Mobility is a strategic problem.

---

## Ecological Constraints

The environment creates barriers.

Examples include:

* Predator territories
* Hostile megafauna
* Migration corridors
* Dangerous ecosystems

Humans may possess technological solutions.

However, ecological barriers remain meaningful.

---

# Expansion

## Territory Must Be Made Useful

Control alone is insufficient.

Territory becomes valuable when connected to human systems.

Examples:

* Roads built
* Resources extracted
* Sensors deployed
* Settlements established

Human expansion is a process of development.

---

## Frontier Regions

Large portions of the map may remain:

* Poorly understood
* Poorly monitored
* Poorly connected

These regions generate uncertainty and opportunity.

---

# Relationship To The Planet

## Resource And Opportunity

Humans view the planet as:

* A frontier
* A source of resources
* A future home
* A strategic opportunity

Humanity seeks to understand and exploit the environment.

---

## Environmental Risk

The world is not passive.

Examples include:

* Dangerous species
* Ecological disruption
* Migration events
* Natural disasters

The planet can create challenges independent of alien activity.

---

# Relationship To Aliens

## An Uncertain Presence

Humans do not begin with confirmed knowledge of an intelligent alien civilization.

Instead, humans encounter:

* Unusual observations
* Biological anomalies
* Suspicious activity
* Infrastructure losses
* Ecological disturbances

The existence, nature, and intentions of the Alien Faction may initially remain uncertain.

---

## Interpreting The Unknown

Humans spend much of the game attempting to answer questions such as:

* What caused this event?
* Is this natural?
* Is this intentional?
* Is this dangerous?
* Is intervention necessary?

The challenge is not simply defeating an enemy.

The challenge is understanding the world.

---

# Deception And Counter-Deception

## Human Deception

Humans may deliberately create misleading signals.

Examples:

* Decoy infrastructure
* False logistics activity
* Controlled demolitions
* Sensor emissions
* Fake expansion efforts

Aliens must interpret these signals.

---

## Vulnerability To Deception

Humans are equally vulnerable to misinterpreting ecological activity.

Not every unusual observation represents alien action.

This uncertainty should create meaningful strategic tension.

---

# Victory Philosophy

Humans are not attempting to preserve the ecosystem.

Humans are attempting to establish civilization.

Success is measured through:

* Expansion
* Infrastructure
* Resource security
* Territorial control
* Long-term sustainability

The Human Faction wins by transforming an uncertain frontier into a stable and enduring human presence.
