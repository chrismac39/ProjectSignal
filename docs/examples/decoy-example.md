# Decoy Example

> Status: Illustrative future example. The described deception mechanics are not implemented in Prototype 0.

## Purpose

This example demonstrates how deception works in ProjectSignal.

Decoys are not magical misinformation tools.

Decoys consume real resources.

Decoys generate believable signals.

Decoys can be investigated.

Decoys can be exposed.

Most importantly:

The owner of a decoy usually does not know whether the deception succeeded.

---

## Omniscient Reality

The Vanguard commander operates:

* A real ammunition depot in the western valley.
* A real refinery in the south.
* A fake refinery in the eastern basin.

The fake refinery contains:

* Power generation.
* Heat emissions.
* Vehicle activity.
* Visible infrastructure.

The fake refinery does not contain:

* Meaningful production.
* Strategic resources.
* Critical logistics.

Its purpose is to attract attention.

---

## Vanguard Reality

The commander sees:

* Real western depot.
* Real southern refinery.
* Fake eastern refinery.

The commander knows which sites matter.

The commander does not know what the Plastai believe.

---

## Plastai Reality

Plastai ecological sensing detects:

* Persistent heat.
* Habitat disruption.
* Mechanical vibration.

All three sites appear important.

The Plastai player must decide where to investigate.

---

## Turn Sequence

### Turn 31

The fake refinery begins operating.

The surrounding ecosystem changes.

Wildlife avoids the area.

Thermal signatures increase.

The signal is real.

---

### Turn 33

Plastai scouts identify three suspicious locations.

The Plastai player cannot investigate all of them immediately.

A choice must be made.

---

### Turn 35

A Plastai scout reaches the eastern basin.

Observation:

Industrial activity exists.

Further observation continues.

---

### Turn 36

The scout determines:

* Production is minimal.
* Logistics traffic is limited.
* Resource throughput is absent.

Assessment:

The refinery is likely a decoy.

---

### Turn 37

The scout withdraws.

The Plastai player shifts attention elsewhere.

The Vanguard player receives no notification.

---

## Vanguard Assumption

The Vanguard commander continues believing:

The fake refinery is consuming Plastai attention.

Additional resources are invested into maintaining the deception.

---

## Reality

The deception has already been penetrated.

The Plastai are now observing the western valley.

The Vanguard commander does not know this.

---

## Alternative Outcomes

### Decoy Success

The Plastai player attacks the fake refinery.

The real depot remains undiscovered.

The deception created strategic value.

---

### Partial Success

The Plastai player eventually discovers the truth.

Several turns of reconnaissance effort were consumed.

The deception still created value.

---

### Failure

The Plastai player identifies the decoy quickly.

Little attention is wasted.

The resources invested in the deception generate little return.

---

## Design Lesson

The value of a decoy is not measured by whether it survives.

The value of a decoy is measured by how it influences enemy decisions.

A decoy that consumes three turns of enemy attention may be more valuable than a decoy that survives the entire game.

---

## Replay View

Vanguard assumption:

The fake refinery protected the western depot.

Plastai reality:

The fake refinery was exposed on Turn 36.

Plastai reconnaissance shifted immediately afterward.

The Vanguard commander never learned this during the match.

Only the replay reveals the true sequence of events.

---

## Core Principle

Deception creates uncertainty.

It does not create ignorance.

The purpose of a decoy is to distort the enemy's understanding of the battlefield long enough for that misunderstanding to matter.
