# Hidden Logistics Example

## Purpose

This example demonstrates how logistics can be more important than frontline forces.

The human commander focuses on visible military strength.

The alien commander focuses on dependency.

Neither commander possesses complete information.

---

## Omniscient Reality

The human military presence consists of:

* Northern artillery park.
* Western sensor station.
* Southern security outposts.

The most important human asset is none of these.

The most important asset is a concealed ammunition depot located in a wooded valley.

The depot supplies:

* Three artillery batteries.
* Multiple outposts.
* Long-range fire support operations.

If the depot is lost, the artillery remains intact but becomes ineffective over time.

---

## Human Reality

The commander sees:

* Operational artillery.
* Stable supply reports.
* Active sensor coverage.
* No enemy contacts near the depot.

The depot is considered secure.

Attention is focused on frontline operations.

---

## Alien Reality

The aliens observe:

* Repeated convoy movement.
* Persistent ecological disruption.
* Habitat avoidance patterns.
* Mechanical vibration along a specific route.

The exact destination is unknown.

The activity appears significant.

---

## Turn Sequence

### Turn 41

Alien scouts detect repeated movement through a forest corridor.

The pattern differs from normal environmental activity.

The location is marked for observation.

---

### Turn 43

Additional scouts observe:

* Regular movement intervals.
* Consistent route usage.
* Heavy ecological disturbance.

The aliens conclude that the route is important.

The reason remains unknown.

---

### Turn 45

The alien player faces a choice:

* Continue observing.
* Attack the route.
* Search for the destination.
* Focus elsewhere.

The player chooses to investigate further.

---

### Turn 47

The destination is identified.

A hidden depot exists within the valley.

The aliens still do not know exactly what the depot stores.

However, they determine it is strategically important.

---

### Turn 49

A raid is launched.

The depot is destroyed.

---

## Immediate Human Reality

The artillery remains operational.

The frontline appears stable.

No major military assets have been lost.

The situation appears manageable.

---

## Omniscient Reality

The artillery batteries are now consuming remaining ammunition reserves.

No replacement supplies exist.

The degradation has already begun.

The human commander does not yet realize it.

---

## Turn 52

Artillery fire missions become less frequent.

Some planned strikes are cancelled.

The commander investigates.

---

## Turn 54

The full consequences become clear.

The depot was not merely a storage facility.

It was a critical dependency.

---

## Alternative Outcome

The aliens attack the artillery directly.

Several guns are destroyed.

The remaining batteries continue operating.

Replacement guns may eventually be produced.

The overall impact is limited.

---

The aliens attack the depot.

No artillery is destroyed.

Operational capability collapses anyway.

---

## Design Lesson

The most important asset is not always the most visible asset.

A commander who understands dependencies can inflict disproportionate damage.

A commander who misunderstands dependencies may defend the wrong locations.

---

## Replay View

Human assumption:

The artillery park was the critical asset.

Alien assumption:

The convoy route indicated a critical dependency.

Reality:

The ammunition depot was the center of gravity for the entire region.

The replay reveals how both commanders interpreted the same battlefield differently.

---

## Core Principle

Humans depend on logistics.

Many human capabilities are only as strong as the supply networks supporting them.

The destruction of a dependency can be more important than the destruction of the capability itself.
