# Titan Discovery Example

## Purpose

This example demonstrates how major threats emerge gradually through signals, assumptions, investigation, and interpretation.

The goal is not to surprise the player with a hidden titan.

The goal is to create situations where the player sees clues but may not understand their significance.

The example also demonstrates that discovering a strategic threat and destroying a strategic threat are separate problems.

---

## Omniscient Reality

The aliens have begun developing a Titan Organism.

The process requires:

* Significant nutrient concentration.
* Genetic diversity.
* Ecological stability.
* Multiple supporting biological structures.

The Titan Nursery is hidden within a remote valley.

The valley itself is not important.

The surrounding ecosystem is.

The development process spans many turns.

The Titan Nursery is a major strategic asset.

It is resilient, concealed, and difficult to destroy without precise targeting information.

---

## Human Reality

The commander observes:

* Increasing wildlife movement.
* Reduced animal populations in neighboring valleys.
* Unusual activity near a remote ecosystem.
* Occasional unexplained thermal signatures.

No direct evidence of a Titan exists.

The observations are real.

Their meaning is unknown.

---

## Alien Reality

The alien player sees:

* Nutrient flows increasing.
* Adaptation processes progressing.
* Supporting ecosystems stabilizing.
* Titan development advancing.

The alien player understands exactly what is happening.

Protecting the process becomes a strategic priority.

---

## Turn Sequence

### Turn 18

Wildlife populations begin shifting toward the region.

The change is subtle.

The human commander notices nothing unusual.

---

### Turn 22

A long-range drone observes large wildlife concentrations.

The area is recorded.

No follow-up occurs.

---

### Turn 26

Several nearby ecosystems show unusual patterns.

Animal populations decrease.

Migration routes change.

The observations are disconnected.

The commander sees individual clues but not the larger pattern.

---

### Turn 29

A reconnaissance mission is proposed.

The commander chooses another target.

Resources are limited.

The valley remains uninvestigated.

---

### Turn 33

Additional biological activity is observed.

The commander considers several possibilities:

* Seasonal migration.
* Feeding grounds.
* Adaptation site.
* Something larger.

No confirmation exists.

---

### Turn 37

The commander suspects a significant alien presence.

A long-range artillery barrage is ordered against the valley.

The strike causes disruption.

Possible effects include:

* Wildlife casualties.
* Damaged feeding grounds.
* Nutrient flow interruption.
* Destruction of minor biological structures.

The Titan Nursery survives.

Development is slowed but not stopped.

The exact target remains unidentified.

---

### Turn 41

A dedicated reconnaissance mission finally reaches the area.

The commander discovers:

* Extensive biological infrastructure.
* Large-scale ecological manipulation.
* A concealed Titan Nursery.
* Supporting adaptation structures.

For the first time, the threat is clearly understood.

---

### Turn 42

The commander now possesses actionable targeting information.

Possible responses include:

* Guided missile strike.
* Strategic bomber attack.
* Precision artillery mission.
* Special operations raid.

The target can now be engaged directly.

---

### Turn 44

Depending on previous decisions:

* The Titan emerges.
* The Titan is delayed.
* The Titan project is destroyed.

The outcome is determined by the quality of intelligence and the effectiveness of the response.

---

## Alternative Outcomes

### Early Investigation

The commander aggressively investigates unusual ecosystem activity.

The nursery is identified before maturation.

A precision strike destroys the facility.

The Titan never emerges.

---

### Early Barrage

The commander acts on suspicion rather than confirmation.

The valley is repeatedly shelled.

Supporting ecosystems suffer significant damage.

Alien development slows.

However, the nursery survives.

The threat remains active.

---

### Delayed Response

The commander focuses elsewhere.

The observations are never fully investigated.

The Titan emerges largely uncontested.

---

## Design Lesson

Discovering a threat is not the same as destroying a threat.

Major strategic targets should not be eliminated through random chance or speculative bombardment.

Reconnaissance creates understanding.

Understanding enables precision.

Precision enables decisive action.

---

## Replay View

Human perception:

A series of unrelated ecological events that gradually appeared more suspicious.

Alien perception:

A major strategic investment requiring protection and concealment.

Omniscient reality:

The entire Titan development process was visible through its effects on the ecosystem long before the nursery itself was identified.

The replay reveals how much information existed, how it was interpreted, and which opportunities were missed.

---

## Core Principle

Major threats should rarely appear without warning.

The challenge is recognizing the warning.

Major threats should also rarely be destroyed without understanding.

The challenge is obtaining the information necessary to target them effectively.

ProjectSignal is not a game about hidden information.

It is a game about misunderstood information.
