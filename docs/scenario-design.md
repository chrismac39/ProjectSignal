# Scenario Design

> Status: Current design standard and scenario catalog.

## Purpose

Scenarios are controlled experiments for the information game. They are not primarily missions, lore delivery, or demonstrations of combat content.

Each scenario should isolate one or two mechanisms while still requiring a consequential decision.

## Required Scenario Structure

Every scenario states:

* The design question being tested.
* The objective situation and active world processes.
* Each faction's objective and prior assumptions.
* Objective events and the physical signatures they create.
* Available collectors and their collection conditions.
* What each faction can report from collected evidence.
* At least two plausible explanations for key early evidence.
* A legal investigation that could discriminate between explanations.
* A decision that must be committed before all uncertainty is resolved.
* Later evidence that assesses the decision.
* What the AAR should reveal.

## Quality Tests

A strong scenario demonstrates:

1. **Same cause, different legibility** - faction expertise or access exposes different distinctions.
2. **Different causes, similar evidence** - at least one early report supports multiple explanations.
3. **Actionable uncertainty** - investigation can change the evidence available.
4. **Consequential commitment** - delay and premature action both have possible costs.
5. **No privileged narrator** - faction reports do not tell commanders what conclusion to draw.
6. **Traceable divergence** - the AAR can connect beliefs to collection conditions and reports.
7. **Counterfactual value** - at least one legal alternative investigation would have exposed different evidence.

Different wording alone does not satisfy faction asymmetry.

## Current Acceptance Scenario

### The Empty Corridor

Status: Implemented as the first three-turn vertical slice.

Design question: Can collection access and expertise cause both factions to make understandable but different operational commitments?

Objective reality: Alien nursery growth changes soil chemistry and displaces wildlife while humans prepare a convoy route through the same corridor.

Human record: A remote survey finds unusual ecological silence. A later soil sample reveals an unfamiliar biological process but not its source, intent, or function.

Alien record: A nursery network recognizes the ecological sequence. Separate observation is needed to distinguish human route preparation from generic mechanical disturbance.

Commitment: Humans reroute the convoy at a time cost. Aliens extend observation toward the prepared route and discover the alternate passage after commitment.

## Next Scenario Tests

### Ashfall Ledger

Status: Designed, not implemented.

Tests overlapping causes. A natural fire and refinery emissions create thermal and particulate evidence in the same area. Chemical residue and ecological recovery offer different discriminating investigations.

### The False Artery

Status: Designed, not implemented.

Tests logistics deception. A busy decoy route reproduces traffic signatures but lacks the maintenance, waste, and consumption pattern of a sustaining route.

### Nursery Echo

Status: Designed, not implemented.

Tests biological mimicry and counter-reconnaissance. Harmless organisms reproduce some early nursery effects while human collection behavior exposes which signatures humans prioritize.

### Broken Cadence

Status: Designed, not implemented.

Tests ambiguous industrial interruption. A failed pumping station appears to aliens as a stopped ecological pulse followed by concentrated human activity.

### Lessons That Do Not Transfer

Status: Future design test.

Tests adaptation without omniscience. Each faction overgeneralizes a pattern learned from an earlier encounter and must seek evidence its prior model did not require.
